\title{\vspace{-1.4cm}\textbf{An inapplicable thin-interface correction inflates the\\
sublimation kinetic coefficient}\\
\large Diagnosis, analysis and proposed correction}
\author{\normalsize Working note --- \code{lunar\_regolith\_DSM}, \code{enceladus\_DSM}}
\date{\normalsize 2026-08-27}

\begin{document}\maketitle\vspace{-0.7cm}

\begin{center}\fbox{\parbox{0.93\textwidth}{\small
\textbf{Abstract.} The kinetic coefficient realized by the solver exceeds the requested
\code{-beta\_sub0} by a factor of $1.22$, so that sublimation and deposition proceed
$18\,\%$ more slowly than intended. The capillary length is realized correctly. The
excess corresponds exactly to the two thin-interface corrections added to
$\tau_{\mathrm{sub}}$ at \code{lunar\_main.c:829} in anticipation of their removal by the
sharp-interface asymptotics; measurement indicates that no such removal occurs. An inner-region
analysis shows why: for a one-sided vapor diffusivity the inner deviation of
$\sigma$ vanishes identically, so the vapor correction cannot arise. The thermal
correction is separately misspecified, omitting the Clausius--Clapeyron coupling by which
temperature acts on $\sigma$. A four-arm parameter sweep in $\bsub$ confirms the diagnosis
over a range of $40$: the measured ratio varies from $0.32$ to $0.95$ as predicted, with
$\beta_{\rm fit}/\bbare=1.0009\pm0.0007$. The correction required is the removal of the
two terms from $\tau_{\mathrm{sub}}$; the Gibbs--Thomson relation used for comparison is
correct as written.}}\end{center}

\section{Measurement}

The wedge geometry places an apex-centered ice annulus in a tapering pore, with the virtual
apex at $y=L_y/2$. On the centerline the interface normal is therefore exactly $\pm\hat x$
and $\vn$ reduces to a one-dimensional displacement rate.
\code{postprocess/wedge\_gt\_velocity.py} measures $\vn$ on both menisci of all nine runs
and compares it with
\begin{equation}
\vn=\frac{\sigma\,\rho_{vs}(T)/\rho_{vs}(T_0)-d_{0,\mathrm{sub0}}\,\chi}{\bsub},
\qquad \sigma=\frac{\rho_v-\rho_{vs}(T)}{\rho_{vs}(T)} .
\label{eq:gt}
\end{equation}
The solver recovers $0.826$ (inner meniscus) and $0.811$ (outer) of the predicted rate.
Both values are constant over 90 days and across nine vapor boundary conditions whose
residual driving force $\sigma-d_{0,\mathrm{sub0}}\chi$ spans two orders of magnitude.
Interface-extraction noise is $0.5\,\%$, against an effect of $18\,\%$, and three
independent estimators of $\vn$ agree on the median to within $1\,\%$; the shortfall is
therefore not an artifact of the measurement.

\section{Identification of the affected coefficient}

Rather than assume Eq.~\eqref{eq:gt} and report a ratio, both coefficients may be fitted.
Each snapshot supplies a triple $(\sigma,\vn,\chi)$, so that
\begin{equation}
\sigma = \beta\,\vn + d_0\,\chi \qquad\text{(no intercept)}
\end{equation}
constitutes a two-column least-squares problem. The design is identifiable because the two
menisci carry curvature of opposite sign, the inner being concave and the outer convex,
while the $3\times3$ reservoir grid varies $\vn$ at nearly fixed $\chi$:
\begin{center}\small\begin{tabular}{lr}\toprule
$\chi$ & $-5.259\times10^{3}$ \ldots\ $+3.459\times10^{3}$ m$^{-1}$ \\
$\vn$  & $-1.459\times10^{-12}$ \ldots\ $+1.378\times10^{-12}$ m\,s$^{-1}$ \\
$\mathrm{corr}(\vn,\chi)$ & $-0.7095$ \\
condition number & $2.58$ \\ \bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}

Fitting $n=3492$ points (nine runs, two menisci, every fifth snapshot after the initial
transient):
\begin{center}\small\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}\toprule
 & $\beta$ [s\,m$^{-1}$] & $/\,\bsub$ & $d_0$ [m] & $/\,d_{0,\mathrm{sub0}}$\\ \midrule
pooled & $7.2324\times10^{5}\pm1.8\times10^{2}$ & 1.2214 & $1.0172\times10^{-9}\pm3.5\times10^{-14}$ & 1.0006\\
inner  & $7.1384\times10^{5}\pm1.6\times10^{2}$ & 1.2055 & $1.0166\times10^{-9}\pm2.7\times10^{-14}$ & 1.0000\\
outer  & $7.2716\times10^{5}\pm1.3\times10^{2}$ & 1.2280 & $1.0167\times10^{-9}\pm3.5\times10^{-14}$ & 1.0001\\ \bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}

The pooled fit gives $R^2=0.999997$ with an rms residual of $5.4\times10^{-9}$, or
$0.14\,\%$ of $|\sigma|$. Holding $d_{0,\mathrm{sub0}}$ fixed and fitting $\beta$ alone
gives $7.2106\times10^{5}$, within $0.3\,\%$ of the free fit, indicating that the two
parameters are not compensating one another.

Leave-one-run-out refitting displaces $\beta$ over
$7.2204$--$7.2419\times10^{5}$, a spread of $0.30\,\%$ about the mean, so no single vapor
boundary condition determines the result. The residual difference between the two menisci
($1.206$ against $1.228$) is $\pm1\,\%$ and is consistent with the reported
$\sigma$-extraction sensitivity of $0.7\,\%$ and $0.3\,\%$ respectively.

The capillary length is thus recovered to $0.06\,\%$: the curvature terms of the
Gibbs--Thomson relation are realized correctly, and the discrepancy resides entirely in
$\beta$, as a multiplicative factor of $1.22$.

\section{The realized kinetic coefficient}

Dividing the phase-field residual by $3\,\mathrm{mob}/\epsilon$ gives the Moure \& Fu form
\begin{equation}
\tau_{\rm sub}\,\partial_t\phi=\epsilon^2\nabla^2\phi-f'(\phi)
+\lambda_{\rm sub}\,g(\phi)\,\frac{\rho_{vs}(T)}{\rho_{\rm ice}}\,\sigma ,
\end{equation}
whose sharp-interface limit is Eq.~\eqref{eq:gt} with realized coefficients
\begin{equation}
d_{0,\rm realized}=d_{0,\mathrm{sub0}},\qquad
\beta_{\rm realized}=\underbrace{\frac{\tau_{\rm sub}\,d_{0,\mathrm{sub0}}}{\epsilon^{2}}}_{\textstyle \bbare}\;-\;C_{\rm corr}.
\label{eq:limit}
\end{equation}
Here $C_{\rm corr}$ is the $O(\epsilon)$ term generated by the variation of $\sigma$ within
the diffuse interface. The solver inflates $\tau_{\rm sub}$ by the anticipated magnitude of
$C_{\rm corr}$, so that the two cancel and $\beta_{\rm realized}=\bsub$:
\begin{equation}
\tau_{\rm sub}=\epsilon\lambda_{\rm sub}\Big(\underbrace{\tfrac{\beta_{\rm sub}}{a_1}}_{82.2\,\%}
+\underbrace{\tfrac{a_2\epsilon}{\texttt{diff\_sub}}}_{13.1\,\%}
+\underbrace{\tfrac{a_2\epsilon}{\texttt{dif\_vap}}}_{4.7\,\%}\Big),
\qquad
\bbare=7.2036\times10^{5}\ \mathrm{s\,m^{-1}} .
\label{eq:tau}
\end{equation}
The bracket is written in the $\rho_{vs}/\rho_{\rm ice}$-scaled variables used internally
by the solver; $\bbare$ is quoted in the unscaled units of $\bsub$. The fit gives
$\beta/\bbare=1.0040$, so that $C_{\rm corr}\approx0$: the anticipated cancellation does
not occur and the inflation is retained in full. This gives $\bbare/\bsub=1.2165$ and a
predicted ratio of $0.8220$, against measured values of $0.826$ and $0.811$.

The defect therefore lies in the definition of a model parameter. Eq.~\eqref{eq:gt} is the
intended physics and is correct; the solver does not realize the value of $\beta$ supplied
to it.

\subsection{Alternative explanations considered}

\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.15em}
\item \emph{Grid pinning or lattice friction.} The discrepancy is a constant
      multiplicative factor, identical on menisci whose velocities differ by a factor of
      60. A threshold or friction mechanism would not produce this behavior.
\item \emph{Bias in the interface extraction.} Three independent estimators of $\vn$
      agree on the median to within $1\,\%$; the residual scatter is $0.5\,\%$,
      zero-mean, and correlated with sub-cell phase.
\item \emph{Incorrect conversion of $a_1$ or $a_2$.} The $\epsilon$ used here is Moure \& Fu's
      $\epsilon$, and is deliberately \emph{not} Kaempfer \& Plapp's $W$
      ($W_{\rm KP}=\sqrt2\,\epsilon$); the constants already absorb the difference.
      Mapping to K\&P's $[-1,1]$ form gives
      $d_{0,\mathrm{sub}}=a_1^{\rm KP}\sqrt2\cdot4\cdot\epsilon/\lambda_{\rm sub}
      =5.0000\,\epsilon/\lambda_{\rm sub}$ against the coded $a_1=5.0$, and
      $a_1^{\rm KP}a_2^{\rm KP}\sqrt2=0.78338$ against the coded $a_1a_2=0.7905$
      ($0.91\,\%$). This is already documented at
      \code{enceladus\_DSM/src/assembly.c:46-58}. Recovery of $d_{0,\mathrm{sub0}}$ to
      $0.06\,\%$ by the free fit constitutes an independent measurement of $a_1$.
\item \emph{The temporal scaling $\xi_v$.} It multiplies the vapor diffusion and the ice source
      identically, so it cancels between them in the inner balance
      (\S\ref{sec:inner}) and survives only in storage/diffusion,
      $v_n\epsilon/(\xi_v D_v)=2.4\times10^{-11}$. The unscaled \code{dif\_vap} in
      Eq.~\eqref{eq:tau} is the correct choice; substituting
      $\xi_v\!\cdot\!$\code{dif\_vap} would put the bracket at $6.36$ instead of
      $0.133$, predicting a shortfall of a factor of $58$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Verification by parameter sweep}\label{sec:ladder}
The preceding analysis rests on a single $(\epsilon,\bsub,T)$ configuration. Because the
offset is additive while $\bsub$ is not, the predicted shortfall depends strongly on
$\bsub$, so that a sweep in $\bsub$ discriminates between the candidate explanations
without modification of the solver. Four runs were performed, identical except for
\code{-beta\_sub0}, each of 3406 steps and approximately two control-point spacings of
meniscus displacement (batch \code{2026-08-25\_\_18.14.15\_beta\_ladder}):

\begin{center}\small\begin{tabular}{rrrrrrr}\toprule
$\times$ & \code{-beta\_sub0} & $\bbare$ & predicted & \multicolumn{2}{c}{measured} & $\beta_{\rm fit}/\bbare$\\
 & & & ratio & inner & outer & \\ \midrule
0.10 & 5.9216e4 & 1.8741e5 & 0.3160 & 0.3239 & 0.3082 & 1.0004 \\
0.25 & 1.4804e5 & 2.7623e5 & 0.5359 & 0.5444 & 0.5254 & 1.0015 \\
1.00 & 5.9216e5 & 7.2036e5 & 0.8220 & 0.8253 & 0.8126 & 1.0017 \\
4.00 & 2.3686e6 & 2.4968e6 & 0.9487 & 0.9471 & 0.9429 & 1.0000 \\ \bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}

The measured ratio varies from $0.32$ to $0.95$, a factor of $2.9$, and follows the
predicted curve to within $2.3\,\%$ in the worst case. The two alternative hypotheses are
excluded by every arm: a constant value of $1.00$, corresponding to the corrections
operating as intended, and a constant value of $0.82$, corresponding to a fixed factor of
other origin.

The sweep varies only $\bsub$ and carries a single vapor boundary condition per arm, so
the two-parameter fit is ill-conditioned there; $d_{0,\mathrm{sub0}}$ was measured to
$0.06\,\%$ on the nine-run $3\times3$ batch and is independent of $\beta$, and is
therefore held fixed. This gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{\beta_{\rm fit}}{\bbare}=1.0009\pm0.0007
\qquad\text{over a }40\times\text{ range in }\bsub .
\end{equation}
so that $C_{\rm corr}=0$ to better than one part in $10^3$. The correction implied by this
result is given in \S\ref{sec:fix}.
