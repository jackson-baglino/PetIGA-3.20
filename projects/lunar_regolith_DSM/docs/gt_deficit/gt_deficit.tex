\title{\vspace{-1.4cm}\textbf{The solver does not deliver the $\bsub$ it is given}\\
\large Diagnosis and remediation plan for \code{lunar\_regolith\_DSM}}
\author{\normalsize Working note — evidence from batch \code{2026-08-07\_\_07.26.38\_wedge\_bc}}
\date{\normalsize 2026-08-25}

\begin{document}\maketitle\vspace{-0.7cm}

\begin{center}\fbox{\parbox{0.93\textwidth}{\small
\textbf{Summary.} The realised kinetic coefficient is $1.22\times$ the requested
\code{-beta\_sub0}, so every run sublimates and deposits $18\,\%$ slower than intended.
The capillary length $d_0$ is exact. The excess is precisely the two Karma--Plapp
thin-interface corrections that \code{lunar\_main.c:829} adds to $\tau_{\mathrm{sub}}$ for
the asymptotics to subtract back off --- and which the measurement says are never
subtracted. One of the two (the thermal one, 13.1 of the 17.8 points) is wrong on its
face and should be fixed regardless. The fix is to $\tau_{\mathrm{sub}}$, not to the
Gibbs--Thomson prediction, which is correct as written.}}\end{center}

\section{What was measured}

The wedge geometry places an apex-centred ice annulus in a tapering pore with the virtual
apex at $y=L_y/2$, so on the centreline the interface normal is \emph{exactly} $\pm\hat x$
and $\vn$ is a one-dimensional displacement rate.
\code{postprocess/wedge\_gt\_velocity.py} measures it on both menisci of all nine runs and
compares against
\begin{equation}
\vn=\frac{\sigma\,\rho_{vs}(T)/\rho_{vs}(T_0)-d_{0,\mathrm{sub0}}\,\chi}{\bsub},
\qquad \sigma=\frac{\rho_v-\rho_{vs}(T)}{\rho_{vs}(T)} .
\label{eq:gt}
\end{equation}
The solver recovers $0.826$ (inner) / $0.811$ (outer) of that rate --- flat over 90 days,
and flat across nine vapour-boundary combinations whose residual driving force
$\sigma-d_0\chi$ spans two orders of magnitude. Interface-extraction noise is $0.5\,\%$
against an $18\,\%$ effect, and three independent $\vn$ estimators agree on the median to
$1\,\%$, so the shortfall is not a measurement artifact.

\section{The regression: which coefficient is wrong}

Rather than assume Eq.~\eqref{eq:gt} and read off a ratio, fit both coefficients freely.
Every snapshot supplies a triple $(\sigma,\vn,\chi)$, so
\begin{equation}
\sigma \;=\; \beta\,\vn \;+\; d_0\,\chi \qquad\text{(no intercept)}
\end{equation}
is an ordinary two-column least-squares problem. \textbf{The design is identifiable
because the two menisci carry opposite-sign curvature} --- the inner one is concave, the
outer convex --- while the $3\times3$ reservoir grid moves $\vn$ at nearly fixed $\chi$.
Those are two genuinely different directions:
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{lr}\toprule
$\chi$ spans & $-5.259\times10^{3}$ \ldots\ $+3.459\times10^{3}$ m$^{-1}$ \\
$\vn$ spans  & $-1.459\times10^{-12}$ \ldots\ $+1.378\times10^{-12}$ m/s \\
$\mathrm{corr}(\vn,\chi)$ & $-0.7095$ \quad(correlated, but far from collinear) \\
condition number & $2.58$ \\ \bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}

Fitting $n=3492$ points (nine runs $\times$ two fronts $\times$ every fifth snapshot after
the initial transient):
\begin{center}\small\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}\toprule
 & $\beta$ [s/m] & $\times\,\bsub$ & $d_0$ [m] & $\times\,d_{0,\mathrm{sub0}}$\\ \midrule
pooled, both fronts & $7.2324\times10^{5}\pm1.8\times10^{2}$ & \textbf{1.2214} & $1.0172\times10^{-9}\pm3.5\times10^{-14}$ & \textbf{1.0006}\\
left front only     & $7.1384\times10^{5}\pm1.6\times10^{2}$ & 1.2055 & $1.0166\times10^{-9}\pm2.7\times10^{-14}$ & 1.0000\\
right front only    & $7.2716\times10^{5}\pm1.3\times10^{2}$ & 1.2280 & $1.0167\times10^{-9}\pm3.5\times10^{-14}$ & 1.0001\\ \bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}

$R^2=0.999997$ pooled; the rms residual is $5.4\times10^{-9}$, i.e.\ $0.14\,\%$ of
$|\sigma|$. Holding $d_0$ fixed at \code{-d0\_sub0} and fitting $\beta$ alone gives
$7.2106\times10^{5}$, within $0.3\,\%$ of the free fit --- the two parameters are not
trading off against each other.

\textbf{Robustness.} Leave-one-run-out (drop each of the nine in turn and refit) moves
$\beta$ over $7.2204$--$7.2419\times10^{5}$, a spread of $0.30\,\%$ of the mean. No single
vapour-BC condition drives the result. The residual left/right split ($1.206$ vs $1.228$)
is $\pm1\,\%$ and is consistent with the quoted $\sigma$-extraction sensitivity
($0.7\,\%$ left, $0.3\,\%$ right).

\textbf{Conclusion.} $d_0$ is recovered to $0.06\,\%$. The Gibbs--Thomson \emph{curvature}
physics is exact and the prediction formula is right. The entire deficit lives in $\beta$,
and it is a clean multiplicative factor of $1.22$.

\section{$\beta$ lands on the \emph{bare} Karma--Plapp coefficient}

Dividing the phase residual by $3\,\mathrm{mob}/\epsilon$ gives the Karma--Plapp form
\begin{equation}
\tau\,\partial_t\phi=\epsilon^2\nabla^2\phi-f'(\phi)+\lambda\,g(\phi)\,u,
\qquad u=\frac{\rho_v-\rho_{vs}(T)}{\rho_{\rm ice}},
\end{equation}
whose sharp-interface limit is $u_{\rm int}=d_0\chi+\beta_{\rm real}V$ with
\begin{equation}
d_0=\frac{a_1\epsilon}{\lambda},\qquad
\beta_{\rm real}=\underbrace{\frac{a_1\tau}{\lambda\epsilon}}_{\textstyle \bbare}-\;C_{\rm corr}.
\label{eq:limit}
\end{equation}
$C_{\rm corr}$ is an $O(\epsilon)$ term generated by $u$ varying \emph{inside} the diffuse
interface. \code{lunar\_main.c:829} pre-inflates $\tau$ by exactly what it assumes
$C_{\rm corr}$ to be, so that the two cancel and $\beta_{\rm real}=\bsub$:
\begin{equation}
\tau_{\rm sub}=\epsilon\lambda\Big(\underbrace{\tfrac{\beta_{\rm sub}}{a_1}}_{82.2\,\%}
+\underbrace{\tfrac{a_2\epsilon}{\texttt{diff\_sub}}}_{13.1\,\%}
+\underbrace{\tfrac{a_2\epsilon}{\texttt{dif\_vap}}}_{4.7\,\%}\Big),
\qquad
\bbare=\frac{\tau_{\rm sub}\,d_{0,\mathrm{sub0}}}{\epsilon^2}=7.2036\times10^{5}\ \mathrm{s/m}.
\label{eq:tau}
\end{equation}
The fit gives $\beta/\bbare=1.0040$. \textbf{$C_{\rm corr}\approx0$: nothing is subtracted,
so the inflation survives intact}, and $\bbare/\bsub=1.2165\Rightarrow$ ratio $0.8220$
against a measured $0.826/0.811$.

This is a defect in a \emph{model parameter definition}. Eq.~\eqref{eq:gt} is the target
physics and is correct; the solver simply does not realise the $\beta$ it was handed.

\section{Why neither correction materialises}\label{sec:why}

\subsection{The thermal term is wrong on its face}
\code{a2*eps/diff\_sub} treats latent heat as if it drove the phase field at unit strength.
It does not: temperature reaches $u$ only through $\rho_{vs}(T)$, so that channel carries a
Clausius--Clapeyron factor. Working the inner balance per channel and reading off
$\mathrm{d}u/\mathrm{d}(VW)$:
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{llr}\toprule
channel & correct coefficient & value \\ \midrule
vapour  & $1/D_v$ & $5.27\times10^{4}$ \\
        & \emph{coded}: $1/\texttt{dif\_vap}$ & $4.59\times10^{4}$ \quad\checkmark \\[2pt]
thermal & $(\mathrm{d}\rho_{vs}/\mathrm{d}T)\,L_{\rm sub}/k$ & $1.0\times10^{2}$ ($k_{\rm ice}$) -- $1.2\times10^{4}$ ($k_{\rm air}$) \\
        & \emph{coded}: $1/\texttt{diff\_sub}$ & $1.29\times10^{5}$ \quad$\times$ \\ \bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}
Over-weighted by 11$\times$ to 1300$\times$ depending on where in the interface $k$ is
evaluated --- and \code{ThermalCond} interpolates between the two, so \emph{any} reasonable
choice leaves this term negligible. This is 13.1 of the 17.8 points and is a defect
independent of anything else in this note.

\subsection{The vapour term is right, and still is not delivered}
It is worth being explicit that $\xi_v$ is \emph{not} implicated here. In the inner region
$\xi_v$ multiplies the vapour diffusion and the ice source identically, so it cancels
between them and survives only in storage/diffusion, $VW/(\xi_v D_v)=2.4\times10^{-11}$.
The unscaled \code{dif\_vap} in Eq.~\eqref{eq:tau} is correct; substituting
$\xi_v\!\cdot\!$\code{dif\_vap} would put the bracket at $6.36$ instead of $0.133$ and
predict a $58\times$ shortfall rather than $1.22\times$.

So the vapour term is structurally sound, yet the measurement says its $4.7$ points are not
subtracted either. \textbf{Leading hypothesis, not established:} vapour diffusion is
one-sided through \code{phi\_aef}, and the $-a_1a_2W/D$ correction is derived for the
symmetric model. The one-sided case requires an anti-trapping current
(Karma 2001) to remove solute-trapping and interface-stretching terms before a clean
correction of that form emerges; this solver has no such current, so the symmetric-model
correction has no reason to apply.

\section{What this has been costing}

$\bsub$ encodes the condensation coefficient, $\beta\propto1/\alpha_c$. A realised
$\beta$ that is $1.2165\times$ too large is an $\alpha_c$ that is $0.822\times$ too small:
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{lr}\toprule
requested \code{-beta\_sub0} $=5.9216\times10^5$ & $\alpha_c=1.341\times10^{-2}$ \\
realised $\beta_{\rm eff}=7.2036\times10^5$ & $\alpha_c^{\rm eff}=1.102\times10^{-2}$ \quad(82.2\,\%) \\ \bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}

Worse, because the inflation is \emph{additive},
$\beta_{\rm eff}=\bsub+\Delta$ with
$\Delta=a_1a_2\epsilon\,(1/D_T+1/D_v)\,\rho_{\rm ice}/\rho_{vs}(T)$, so $\Delta$ is a
\textbf{hard floor on the kinetics the model can represent at all} --- no value of
\code{-beta\_sub0} can go below it. $\Delta\propto\epsilon$ and $\propto1/\rho_{vs}(T)$,
so coarser interfaces and colder temperatures are both worse:
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{rrrr}\toprule
$T$ [$^\circ$C] & $\rho_{vs}$ [kg/m$^3$] & $\Delta$ [s/m] & largest reachable $\alpha_c$ \\ \midrule
$-60$ & $8.885\times10^{-6}$ & $1.225\times10^{7}$ & 0.0006 \\
$-40$ & $1.055\times10^{-4}$ & $1.031\times10^{6}$ & 0.0077 \\
$-30$ & $3.123\times10^{-4}$ & $3.484\times10^{5}$ & 0.0228 \\
$-20$ & $8.487\times10^{-4}$ & $1.282\times10^{5}$ & 0.0619 \\
$-10$ & $2.139\times10^{-3}$ & $5.086\times10^{4}$ & 0.1561 \\
$  0$ & $5.047\times10^{-3}$ & $2.156\times10^{4}$ & 0.3684 \\ \bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}
(at $\epsilon=8.584\times10^{-7}$ m; halving $\epsilon$ halves every $\Delta$.)
At $-60\,^\circ$C the floor is $20.7\times$ the reference $\beta$: the model would be
running kinetics roughly twenty times slower than requested no matter what is asked for.
\textbf{Any earlier result whose conclusion depends on the absolute rate --- rather than on
a scaling exponent or a ratio --- should be re-examined against this factor.}

\section{What has been ruled out}

\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.15em}
\item \textbf{Grid pinning / lattice friction.} The deficit is a constant \emph{factor},
      identical on fronts whose speeds differ 60$\times$. A threshold or friction cannot
      behave that way.
\item \textbf{Interface-extraction bias.} Three independent $\vn$ estimators agree on the
      median to $1\,\%$; the residual scatter is $0.5\,\%$, zero-mean, and locked to
      sub-cell phase.
\item \textbf{A mis-converted $a_1$ or $a_2$.} $\epsilon$ is indeed not the Karma--Plapp
      $W$ ($W_{\rm KP}=\sqrt2\,\epsilon$), but the constants already absorb it: mapping to
      Karma's $[-1,1]$ form gives $d_0=a_1^{\rm KP}\sqrt2\cdot4\cdot\epsilon/\lambda
      =5.0000\,\epsilon/\lambda$ against the coded $a_1=5.0$, and
      $a_1^{\rm KP}a_2^{\rm KP}\sqrt2=0.78338$ against the coded $a_1a_2=0.7905$
      ($0.91\,\%$). The free fit recovering $d_0$ to $0.06\,\%$ is an independent
      measurement of $a_1$.
\item \textbf{$\xi_v$.} Cancels from the inner balance (\S\ref{sec:why}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Remediation plan}

\subsection{Step 1 --- measure before changing anything: the $\bsub$ ladder}
Everything above rests on one $(\epsilon,\bsub,T)$ point. Because the inflation is additive
and $\bsub$ is not, the predicted shortfall is a strong function of $\bsub$ --- so a ladder
in $\bsub$ separates the hypotheses without touching the solver. Four runs, identical but
for \code{-beta\_sub0}, with \code{-t\_final} and \code{-dtmax} set per arm so all four
take the same 3406 steps and move the menisci the same ${\sim}2$ control-point spacings:

\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{rrrrrr}\toprule
$\times$ & \code{-beta\_sub0} & predicted ratio & $\tau_{\rm sub}$ [s] & \code{-dtmax} & \code{-t\_final} \\ \midrule
0.10 & 5.9216e4 & \textbf{0.3160} & 135.8 & 1.485e2 & 5.0577e5 \ (5.9 d) \\
0.25 & 1.4804e5 & \textbf{0.5359} & 200.2 & 2.188e2 & 7.4548e5 \ (8.6 d) \\
1.00 & 5.9216e5 & \textbf{0.8220} & 522.1 & 5.707e2 & 1.9440e6 \ (22.5 d) \\
4.00 & 2.3686e6 & \textbf{0.9487} & 1809.8 & 1.978e3 & 6.7381e6 \ (78.0 d) \\ \bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}
\begin{quote}\code{./scripts/HPC/submit\_batch.sh -{-}tag beta\_ladder \textbackslash\\
\hspace*{1em}-{-}tests-file scripts/HPC/tests\_beta\_ladder.txt}\end{quote}
Read \code{meas / pred} from each run's \code{wedge\_gt\_velocity.py} summary:
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.1em}
\item $0.316/0.536/0.822/0.949$ --- $C_{\rm corr}=0$ confirmed. Proceed to Step 2.
\item $1.00$ at all four --- the corrections do operate; the post-processing is wrong and
      this note is wrong with it.
\item $0.82$ at all four --- a constant factor from some other source; start over.
\end{itemize}
An intermediate curve --- following the shape but not the amplitude --- would mean
$C_{\rm corr}$ is real but mis-sized, which is the outcome that most needs the Step-4
modelling work rather than a coefficient change.

\subsection{Step 2 --- the $\tau_{\rm sub}$ change}
Make the correction explicit and switchable rather than silently baked in, so both forms
stay runnable and the ladder can be repeated against either. In \code{lunar\_main.c}
around line 829:
\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
/* Thin-interface corrections. Each channel enters with the strength\\
~~*~with which it actually drives u = (rho\_v - rho\_vs(T))/rho\_ice, NOT\\
~~*~with unit strength. See docs/gt\_deficit/. */\\
PetscReal c\_vap~~= a2 * eps / user.dif\_vap;\\
PetscReal c\_therm = a2 * eps * drhovs\_dT * user.lat\_sub / k\_eff;\\
if (!user.thin\_iface\_corr) \{ c\_vap = 0.0; c\_therm = 0.0; \}\\
tau\_sub = eps * lambda\_sub * (beta\_sub / a1 + c\_therm + c\_vap);
\end{quote}
with \code{-thin\_iface\_corr} defaulting to whatever the ladder says, \code{drhovs\_dT}
from the existing \code{RhoVS\_I} derivative output --- \code{lunar\_main.c:735} already
requests \code{d\_rhovs} and then never uses it, and it is exactly
$\mathrm{d}\rho_{vs}/\mathrm{d}T=8.1521\times10^{-5}$ kg\,m$^{-3}$K$^{-1}$ at
$-20\,^\circ$C, and \code{k\_eff} from \code{ThermalCond} at
$\phi=1/2$. Print the three bracket terms and $\bbare/\bsub$ in the run header next to
$\tau_{\rm sub}$, so every future \code{outp.txt} states how far the realised kinetics sit
from the requested ones.

\subsection{Step 3 --- recalibrate what depends on $\beta$}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.15em}
\item \code{preprocess/comp\_eps.py} sizes $\epsilon$ using \code{beta\_uns}
      (\code{eps\_ceiling}, \code{kin\_over\_curv}). With the correction in place the
      relation becomes implicit --- $\epsilon$ sets $\Delta$, which sets $\beta_{\rm eff}$,
      which feeds the $\epsilon$ bound --- so it needs either an iteration or an explicit
      note that it reports the \emph{requested} rather than the realised kinetics.
\item Re-derive $\epsilon$ for the standard $-20\,^\circ$C geometries after the change and
      confirm the mesh element counts do not move; if they do, meshes must be regenerated
      before any comparison run.
\item Audit past conclusions for absolute-rate dependence. Exponent studies and
      dimensionless ratios are unaffected; anything quoting a time-to-event or an
      absolute growth rate is off by the factor in \S5 for its own $(\epsilon,T)$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Step 4 --- the open modelling question}
If the ladder confirms $C_{\rm corr}=0$, the honest position is that this model has
\emph{no} thin-interface correction, and dropping both terms is a calibration fix, not a
derivation. Making the correction genuinely valid means adding an anti-trapping current to
the vapour equation so the one-sided asymptotics close. That is a real project --- new flux
term in \code{assembly.c}, matching Jacobian, and re-verification with
\code{-snes\_test\_jacobian} --- and should only be undertaken if a case appears where the
$O(\epsilon)$ physics actually matters. At present $\epsilon/r\approx4\times10^{-3}$ and
the interface is deeply attachment-limited, so it does not.

\subsection{Interim: how to get the kinetics you want today}
Until Step 2 lands, pre-compensate. To realise a target $\beta$, pass
$\bsub=\beta_{\rm target}-\Delta$; at $-20\,^\circ$C and
$\epsilon=8.584\times10^{-7}$ m that is $4.6396\times10^{5}$ in place of
$5.9216\times10^{5}$. This fails outright when $\beta_{\rm target}<\Delta$ --- see the
floor table --- and it is a workaround, not a fix: it must be recomputed for every
$(\epsilon,T)$ and it silently stops working when someone changes either.
