\section{The measurement}

The wedge geometry places an apex-centred ice annulus in a tapering pore, with the
virtual apex at $y=L_y/2$. On the centreline the interface normal is therefore
\emph{exactly} $\pm\hat{x}$, so $\vn$ reduces to a one-dimensional displacement rate.
\code{postprocess/wedge\_gt\_velocity.py} measures it and compares against
\begin{equation}
\vn=\frac{\sigma\,\rho_{vs}(T)/\rho_{vs}(T_0)-d_{0,\mathrm{sub0}}\,\chi}{\beta_{\mathrm{sub0}}},
\qquad \sigma=\frac{\rho_v-\rho_{vs}(T)}{\rho_{vs}(T)} .
\end{equation}
Across all nine runs the solver recovers $0.826$ (inner) / $0.811$ (outer) of that rate,
flat over 90 days and flat across a residual driving force $\sigma-d_0\chi$ spanning two
orders of magnitude.

\textbf{Free two-parameter fit.} Regressing $\sigma=\beta \vn+d_0\chi$ over all nine runs
$\times$ both fronts $\times$ 1000 snapshots ($n=3492$; condition number 2.6, because the
two fronts carry opposite-sign $\chi$):
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{lrr}\toprule
 & fitted & vs.\ requested \\ \midrule
$d_0$   & $1.0171\times10^{-9}$ m   & $\mathbf{1.0005}\times$ \code{-d0\_sub0} \\
$\beta$ & $7.227\times10^{5}$ s/m   & $\mathbf{1.2205}\times$ \code{-beta\_sub0} \\ \bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}
The curvature physics is exact to $0.05\,\%$. The entire deficit lives in $\beta$.

\section{$\beta$ lands on the \emph{bare} Karma--Plapp coefficient}

The phase equation, after dividing the residual by $3\,\mathrm{mob}/\epsilon$, is
\begin{equation}
\tau\,\partial_t\phi=\epsilon^2\nabla^2\phi-f'(\phi)+\lambda\,g(\phi)\,u,
\qquad u=\frac{\rho_v-\rho_{vs}(T)}{\rho_{\rm ice}} .
\end{equation}
Its sharp-interface limit is $u_{\rm int}=d_0\chi+\beta_{\rm real}V$ with
\begin{equation}
d_0=\frac{a_1\epsilon}{\lambda},\qquad
\beta_{\rm real}=\underbrace{\frac{a_1\tau}{\lambda\epsilon}}_{\textstyle \beta_{\rm bare}}-\;C_{\rm corr}.
\end{equation}
$C_{\rm corr}$ is an $O(\epsilon)$ term generated by $u$ varying \emph{inside} the diffuse
interface. \code{lunar\_main.c:829} pre-inflates $\tau$ by precisely what it assumes
$C_{\rm corr}$ to be, so that the two cancel and $\beta_{\rm real}=\beta_{\rm sub0}$:
\begin{equation}
\tau_{\rm sub}=\epsilon\lambda\Big(\underbrace{\tfrac{\beta_{\rm sub}}{a_1}}_{82.2\,\%}
+\underbrace{\tfrac{a_2\epsilon}{\texttt{diff\_sub}}}_{13.1\,\%}
+\underbrace{\tfrac{a_2\epsilon}{\texttt{dif\_vap}}}_{4.7\,\%}\Big).
\label{eq:tau}
\end{equation}
Measured: $\beta_{\rm real}=\beta_{\rm bare}$ to $0.3\,\%$, i.e.\ $\mathbf{C_{\rm corr}\approx0}$.
\textbf{Nothing is subtracted, so the inflation survives}, and
$\beta_{\rm bare}/\beta_{\rm sub0}=1.2165 \Rightarrow$ predicted ratio $0.8220$.

This is a defect in a \emph{model parameter definition}, not in the prediction. The
prediction is the target physics and it is right; the solver simply does not realise the
$\beta$ it was asked for.

\textbf{Grid pinning is ruled out} independently: the deficit is a constant \emph{factor},
identical on fronts whose speeds differ 60$\times$. A friction or threshold cannot do that.

\section{$\xi_v$ does \emph{not} enter the interface condition}

In the inner region, moving frame, the vapour equation reads
\[
\underbrace{\phi_a\tfrac{V}{W}\partial_\eta\rho_v}_{\rm storage}
=\underbrace{\tfrac{\xi_v}{W^2}\partial_\eta\!\big(D_v\phi_a\partial_\eta\rho_v\big)}_{\rm diffusion}
+\underbrace{\xi_v\rho_{\rm ice}\tfrac{V}{W}\partial_\eta\phi}_{\rm ice\ source}.
\]
$\xi_v$ multiplies diffusion \emph{and} source identically, so it cancels between them and
survives only in storage/diffusion, which here is $VW/(\xi_v D_v)=2.4\times10^{-11}$.
\textbf{The unscaled \code{dif\_vap} in Eq.~\eqref{eq:tau} is correct.} Substituting
$\xi_v\!\cdot\!$\code{dif\_vap} would be wrong on principle and catastrophic in practice:

\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{lrr}\toprule
$\tau_{\rm sub}$ bracket & $\beta_{\rm bare}/\beta_0$ & predicted ratio \\ \midrule
as coded & 1.2165 & 0.8220 \\
with $a_2\epsilon/(\xi_v\,\texttt{dif\_vap})$ & \textbf{58.13} & \textbf{0.0172} \\ \bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}

$\xi_v$ would only reach the interface condition once $V\gtrsim \xi_v D_v/\epsilon
=2.5\times10^{-2}$ m/s --- about $4\times10^{10}$ times the actual velocities.

\subsection{What $\xi_v$ \emph{does} cost: mass conservation}
The coded vapour equation conserves
$M_{\rm mod}=\!\int\!\phi_a\rho_v+\xi_v\rho_{\rm ice}\!\int\!\phi$, not the physical
$M_{\rm phys}=\!\int\!\phi_a\rho_v+\rho_{\rm ice}\!\int\!\phi$. Equivalently, dividing
through by $\xi_v$: the ice source and the diffusion are physical and only the vapour
\emph{storage} term is inflated by $1/\xi_v$. So mass is exact in the quasi-static limit,
which is the regime the model is designed for. Magnitude:
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{lrr}\toprule
 & $-20\,^\circ$C & $0\,^\circ$C \\ \midrule
$M_{\rm vap}/M_{\rm ice}$ & $1.9\times10^{-6}$ & $5.5\times10^{-6}$ \\
worst-case error, $(1/\xi_v)M_{\rm vap}/M_{\rm ice}$ & $1.9\times10^{-3}$ & $5.5\times10^{-3}$ \\
observed over 90 d (three runs) & \multicolumn{2}{c}{$1.4$--$4.8\times10^{-5}$} \\ \bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}
So: acknowledge and document, do not chase. It is two to three orders of magnitude below
the effect under investigation. If exact conservation is ever needed, the only real fix is
$\xi_v=1$ with a stiffer vapour solve --- $\xi_v$ is a stiffness knob
($D_v/dx^2=5.2\times10^{7}\,$s$^{-1}$ unscaled), and its price is a wrong vapour
\emph{transient} plus this bounded bookkeeping error.

\section{The thermal $a_2$ term is wrong on its face}\label{sec:thermal}

\code{a2*eps/diff\_sub} treats latent heat as if it drove the phase field at unit strength.
It does not: temperature reaches $u$ only through $\rho_{vs}(T)$, so that channel carries a
Clausius--Clapeyron factor. Working the inner balance for each channel and reading off
$\mathrm{d}u/\mathrm{d}(VW)$:
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{llr}\toprule
channel & correct coefficient & value \\ \midrule
vapour  & $1/D_v$ & $5.27\times10^{4}$ \\
        & \emph{coded}: $1/\texttt{dif\_vap}$ & $4.59\times10^{4}$ \quad\checkmark \\[2pt]
thermal & $(\mathrm{d}\rho_{vs}/\mathrm{d}T)\,L_{\rm sub}/k$ & $1.0\times10^{2}$ ($k_{\rm ice}$) -- $1.2\times10^{4}$ ($k_{\rm air}$) \\
        & \emph{coded}: $1/\texttt{diff\_sub}$ & $1.29\times10^{5}$ \quad$\times$ \\ \bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}
Over-weighted by 11$\times$ to 1300$\times$ depending on where in the interface $k$ is
evaluated --- and \code{ThermalCond} interpolates between the two, so \emph{any} reasonable
choice leaves this term negligible. That is 13.1 of the 17.8 percentage points, and it is
wrong regardless of how the asymptotics behave.

The vapour term (the remaining 4.7 points) is structurally \emph{correct} but still is not
delivered. Most likely --- \textbf{conjecture, not established} --- because vapour diffusion
is one-sided through \code{phi\_aef}, the case that requires an anti-trapping current the
solver does not have; the symmetric-model correction simply does not apply there.

\section{$\epsilon$ is not $W_{\rm KP}$ --- but $a_1,a_2$ already absorb it}

This model's well gives the equilibrium profile $\phi=\tfrac12[1+\tanh(x/2\epsilon)]$;
Karma's gives $\tfrac12[1+\tanh(x/\sqrt2 W)]$. Hence
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;W_{\rm KP}=\sqrt{2}\,\epsilon\;}
\end{equation}
Mapping the $[0,1]$ equation onto Karma's $[-1,1]$ form gives
$\tau_{\rm KP}=2\tau$, $W=\sqrt2\epsilon$, $\lambda_{\rm KP}=\lambda/4$, and therefore
\begin{align}
d_0&=a_1^{\rm KP}\tfrac{W}{\lambda_{\rm KP}}
=0.883883\cdot\sqrt2\cdot 4\cdot\tfrac{\epsilon}{\lambda}=5.0000\,\tfrac{\epsilon}{\lambda}
&&\text{code: }a_1=5.0 \quad(0.000\,\%)\\
C_{\rm corr}&=a_1^{\rm KP}a_2^{\rm KP}\tfrac{W}{D}=0.783375\,\tfrac{\epsilon}{D}
&&\text{code: }a_1a_2=0.7905\quad(0.91\,\%)
\end{align}
So the $\sqrt2$ is real and already accounted for. Independently confirmed: the free fit
recovers $d_0$ to $0.05\,\%$, which is a direct measurement of $a_1=5$.

\section{The bumps in $\vn$ are the reading, not the ice}

The original periodic ripple satisfied $|\vn|\,T_{\rm ripple}=6.0\times10^{-7}$ m on every
moving front (0.90--1.06 $\times dx$ over twelve fronts): one cycle per control-point
spacing traversed. Each front has its own period simply because $T=dx/|\vn|$ and the fronts
move at different speeds. The wobble in $r(t)$ falls 15$\times$ as the reading improves,
which is what rules out lattice pinning (real stick-slip would be identical in all four):
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{lrr}\toprule
wobble in $r(t)$, units of $dx$ & 2-pt linear & tanh fit \\ \midrule
control net (p=2 B-spline coefficients) & 0.0026 & 0.0009 \\
true NURBS, 4$\times$ & 0.0012 & \textbf{0.0002} \\ \bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}
Two causes compound: the \code{.vts} carries \emph{control coefficients}, not field values
($\sim10^{-9}$ m of position error that drifts in phase as the interface crosses the grid),
and a two-sample $\phi=0.5$ crossing is biased by where those samples fall. The default is
now \code{--interface tanh}; the residual $\vn$ wobble is $0.5\,\%$ and, as the figure shows,
still locked to sub-cell phase.

\textbf{Is it biasing the answer? No.} Three independent estimators agree on the median:
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{lrr}\toprule
estimator & median vs.\ reference & wobble \\ \midrule
position difference + sliding fit (current default) & --- & 0.49\,\% \\
level set $-\phi_t/\phi_x$, $\phi_x$ from \code{np.gradient} & $+4.5\,\%$ & 0.13\,\% \\
level set, $\phi_x=1/(4\epsilon)$ analytic & $+1.0\,\%$ & 0.95\,\% \\ \bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}
The $+4.5\,\%$ is fully explained: a centred difference underestimates $|\nabla\phi|$ of a
$\tanh$ on this grid by $1-\tanh(h)/h=3.94\,\%$ with $h=dx/2\epsilon$. Correcting for it
brings that estimator within $0.2$--$0.4\,\%$ of the default \emph{and} keeps the
$0.13\,\%$ wobble --- available as an upgrade if ever needed.

\subsection{In-solver or post-processing?}
Post-processing, for now. The extraction is at $0.5\,\%$ against an effect of $18\,\%$, and
the medians are confirmed by independent estimators. An in-solver $\vn=-\phi_t/|\nabla\phi|$
at quadrature points would use the true NURBS field and the integrator's own $\phi_t$,
eliminating both grid-phase bias and snapshot differencing --- worth doing if (a) a
$\tau_{\rm sub}$ fix must be validated to $\sim\!1\,\%$ across a sweep, or (b) the check is
wanted on geometries with no convenient centreline. Not needed to settle the present question.

\section{The test: a $\beta_{\rm sub0}$ ladder}

Four runs, identical but for $\beta_{\rm sub0}$. Because the $a_2$ inflation is
\emph{additive} and $\beta_{\rm sub0}$ is not, the predicted shortfall is a strong function
of $\beta_{\rm sub0}$. \code{-t\_final} and \code{-dtmax} are set per arm so all four run the
same 3406 steps and move the menisci the same ${\sim}2$ control-point spacings --- equal
cost, equal precision.

\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{rrrrrr}\toprule
$\times$ & \code{-beta\_sub0} & predicted ratio & $\tau_{\rm sub}$ [s] & \code{-dtmax} & \code{-t\_final} \\ \midrule
0.10 & 5.9216e4 & \textbf{0.3160} & 135.8 & 1.485e2 & 5.0577e5 \ (5.9 d) \\
0.25 & 1.4804e5 & \textbf{0.5359} & 200.2 & 2.188e2 & 7.4548e5 \ (8.6 d) \\
1.00 & 5.9216e5 & \textbf{0.8220} & 522.1 & 5.707e2 & 1.9440e6 \ (22.5 d) \\
4.00 & 2.3686e6 & \textbf{0.9487} & 1809.8 & 1.978e3 & 6.7381e6 \ (78.0 d) \\ \bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}

\textbf{Reading the outcome} (\code{meas / pred} from each run's summary):
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.1em}
\item $0.316/0.536/0.822/0.949$ --- the $a_2$ terms are never subtracted. Fix $\tau_{\rm sub}$.
\item $1.00$ at all four --- they \emph{do} operate; the post-processing is wrong somewhere.
\item $0.82$ at all four --- neither story; the deficit is a constant factor from something else.
\end{itemize}
