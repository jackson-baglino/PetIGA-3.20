\section{$\Delta$: what it is, and why it matters}\label{sec:delta}

\textbf{Definition.} $\Delta$ is the amount by which the realized kinetic coefficient
exceeds the requested one. From Eq.~\eqref{eq:tau} with $C_{\rm corr}=0$:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\Delta \;\equiv\; \bbare-\bsub \;=\; a_1a_2\,\epsilon
\left(\frac{1}{\texttt{diff\_sub}}+\frac{1}{\texttt{dif\_vap}}\right)
\frac{\rho_{\rm ice}}{\rho_{vs}(T)}\;}
\label{eq:delta}
\end{equation}
in the unscaled units of $\bsub$ (s/m). The bracket is the pair of $a_2$ terms
$\tau_{\rm sub}$ carries; $a_1$ converts the bracket to a kinetic coefficient; the
$\rho_{\rm ice}/\rho_{vs}$ factor undoes the scaling the code works in internally. At
the wedge parameters ($\epsilon=8.584\times10^{-7}$ m, $T=-20\,^\circ$C):
\begin{center}\small\begin{tabular}{lrr}\toprule
 & value [s/m] & share of $\Delta$\\ \midrule
thermal, $a_1a_2\epsilon\,\rho_{\rm ice}/(\rho_{vs}\texttt{diff\_sub})$ & $9.44\times10^{4}$ & 73.7\,\%\\
vapor, \ \,$a_1a_2\epsilon\,\rho_{\rm ice}/(\rho_{vs}\texttt{dif\_vap})$ & $3.37\times10^{4}$ & 26.3\,\%\\ \midrule
$\Delta$ & $\mathbf{1.282\times10^{5}}$ & \\
$\bsub$ (requested) & $5.9216\times10^{5}$ & \\
$\bbare=\bsub+\Delta$ (realized) & $7.2036\times10^{5}$ & \\ \bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}

\textbf{Why it matters --- three properties, none of them benign.}

\emph{(i) It is additive, not multiplicative.} The shortfall is
$\bsub/(\bsub+\Delta)$, so it is not a fixed factor: it depends on how large $\bsub$
itself is. The ladder measured exactly this, $0.32\to0.95$ across four arms
(\S\ref{sec:ladder}). Any ``just scale $\beta$ by the observed ratio'' fix is therefore
calibrated to one parameter point and wrong everywhere else --- see \S\ref{sec:fix}.

\emph{(ii) It scales as $\epsilon$ and as $1/\rho_{vs}(T)$.} Coarser interfaces and
colder temperatures both make it worse, and neither is a free knob: $\epsilon$ is set by
resolution requirements and $T$ by the science. At Molaro's $\epsilon=1.2\times10^{-7}$ m
with $\alpha_c=0.1$, $\Delta/\bsub=0.23$; at the wedge's it is $0.22$; at
$-60\,^\circ$C it is $20.7$.

\emph{(iii) It is a hard floor on the kinetics the model can represent.} Since
$\beta_{\rm realized}=\bsub+\Delta\ge\Delta$ for any positive $\bsub$, and
$\beta\propto1/\alpha_c$, there is a \textbf{ceiling on the attachment coefficient} no
input can exceed:
\begin{center}\small\begin{tabular}{rrrr}\toprule
$T$ [$^\circ$C] & $\rho_{vs}$ [kg/m$^3$] & $\Delta$ [s/m] & largest reachable $\alpha_c$\\ \midrule
$-60$ & $8.885\times10^{-6}$ & $1.225\times10^{7}$ & 0.0006\\
$-40$ & $1.055\times10^{-4}$ & $1.031\times10^{6}$ & 0.0077\\
$-20$ & $8.487\times10^{-4}$ & $1.282\times10^{5}$ & 0.0619\\
$  0$ & $5.047\times10^{-3}$ & $2.156\times10^{4}$ & 0.3684\\ \bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}
(at $\epsilon=8.584\times10^{-7}$ m; halving $\epsilon$ halves every $\Delta$ and doubles
every ceiling.) In the Molaro configuration this bites directly: the requested
$\alpha_c=0.1$ is delivered as $0.081$, and at $\alpha_c\to1$ --- the end one would want
for an attachment-limited Demmenie replication --- $\Delta$ \emph{exceeds} $\bsub$ by
$2.3\times$.

\section{Why $C_{\rm corr}=0$: the inner-region analysis}\label{sec:inner}

\subsection{What the correction actually is}
It is not physics. At finite $\epsilon$ the diffuse model produces a \emph{spurious}
addition to the sharp-interface kinetic coefficient, because $\sigma$ varies inside the
interface where the phase-change source lives. $\tau_{\rm sub}$ is inflated by exactly
that spurious amount so the two cancel and the model realizes $\bsub$. The construction
is right; the question is only whether this model produces the spurious term it is being
compensated for.

\subsection{The solvability condition}
Stretch the interface: $\zeta$ is signed normal distance (positive into the vapor),
$\eta=\zeta/\epsilon$. At leading order the phase equation gives the equilibrium profile
$\phi_0=\tfrac12(1+\tanh(\eta/2))$. At the next order the linear operator is singular,
and the Fredholm condition --- projecting onto the translation mode $\partial_\eta\phi_0$
--- is what produces the interface condition. Writing $\sigma=\sigma_{\rm int}+\delta\sigma(\eta)$
with $\sigma_{\rm int}$ the outer solution extrapolated to $\zeta=0$, the two integrals
that set the coefficients are
\begin{equation}
\int(\partial_\eta\phi_0)^2\,\mathrm{d}\eta=\tfrac16,\qquad
\int g(\phi_0)\,\partial_\eta\phi_0\,\mathrm{d}\eta=\tfrac1{30},\qquad
a_1=\frac{1/6}{1/30}=\mathbf{5}
\end{equation}
--- verified numerically to six digits, and exactly the $a_1$ the code uses. The
correction rides on a \emph{third} integral,
\begin{equation}
I=\int g(\phi_0)\,\partial_\eta\phi_0\;\delta\sigma(\eta)\,\mathrm{d}\eta ,
\label{eq:Iint}
\end{equation}
and the entire question is whether $I$ is zero.

\textbf{The weight $g(\phi_0)\,\partial_\eta\phi_0$ is even in $\eta$}, because
$\phi_0(-\eta)=1-\phi_0(\eta)$ makes $g=\phi_0^2(1-\phi_0)^2$ even and
$\partial_\eta\phi_0=\tfrac14\mathrm{sech}^2(\eta/2)$ even. So $I$ picks out the
\emph{even} part of $\delta\sigma$ and is blind to the odd part.

\subsection{Symmetric $D$: the deviation is even, and $I\neq0$}
With $D$ equal on both sides and the flux splitting $\pm j/2$, the quasi-static inner
balance $\partial^2_{\eta\eta}\rho_v\propto\partial_\eta\phi_0$ integrates to
$\partial_\eta\rho_v\propto(\phi_0-\tfrac12)$, which is \emph{odd}; so
$\delta\sigma\propto\Psi(\eta)=\ln\cosh(\eta/2)-|\eta|/2+\ln2$ is \emph{even} and decays
to zero at both infinities. Even deviation against even weight: $I\neq0$, and this is
where Karma \& Rappel's $-a_1a_2\epsilon/D$ comes from.

\subsection{One-sided $D_v\phi_a$: the deviation is identically zero}
Our vapor equation carries $D_v\phi_a$, which vanishes in the ice. Dropping storage
(justified: $v_n\epsilon/(\xi_v D_v)=2.4\times10^{-11}$) the inner balance is
\begin{equation}
\partial_\eta\!\left(D_v\phi_a\,\partial_\eta\rho_v\right)
=-\epsilon\,v_n\rho_{\rm ice}\,\partial_\eta\phi_0 .
\end{equation}
Integrate once; no flux into the ice fixes the constant at $C=\epsilon v_n\rho_{\rm ice}$:
\begin{equation}
D_v\phi_a\,\partial_\eta\rho_v=\epsilon v_n\rho_{\rm ice}(1-\phi_0)
=\epsilon v_n\rho_{\rm ice}\,\phi_a
\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;
\partial_\eta\rho_v=\frac{\epsilon v_n\rho_{\rm ice}}{D_v}\quad\text{wherever }\phi_a\neq0 .
\end{equation}
\textbf{The $\phi_a$ cancels.} And that constant is precisely the outer Stefan gradient
--- $D_v\partial_\zeta\rho_v|_{\rm int}=v_n\rho_{\rm ice}$ gives the same value. The inner
solution \emph{is} the outer solution continued through the interface, so
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\delta\sigma\equiv0\;\Longrightarrow\;I=0\;\Longrightarrow\;C_{\rm corr}=0\;}
\end{equation}
This is a \emph{vanishing theorem}, not a cancellation: $\delta\sigma$ is not merely odd,
it is null, so $I=0$ for \emph{any} weight and no prefactor is needed. (Deep in the ice
the \code{air\_lim}$=10^{-6}$ floor on $\phi_a$ breaks the cancellation, but $g(\phi_0)$
there is $\lesssim10^{-12}$ and contributes nothing.)

\subsection{An honest limit on this derivation}
The vanishing result above is robust because it needs no prefactor. The \emph{symmetric}
branch is not equally settled here: constructing $\delta\sigma=\Psi$ as above and
evaluating Eq.~\eqref{eq:Iint} gives $a_2=-6I=0.087$, against Karma \& Rappel's $0.158$.
So this sketch is missing O($\epsilon$) contributions --- the curvature term, the
$\phi_1$ back-reaction, or the precise matching of the flux split. \textbf{We have not
re-derived $a_2$, and should not claim to.} What is established is the structural
contrast: even-and-nonzero versus identically-null. The measurement supplies the rest.

\subsection{The thermal channel fails for a different reason}
The thermal term is not a one-sidedness question --- $k$ is interpolated and nonzero in
both phases, so a correction does exist there. It is wrong because
\code{a2*eps/diff\_sub} assumes latent heat drives $\sigma$ at \emph{unit strength}.
Temperature reaches $\sigma$ only through $\rho_{vs}(T)$, so the channel carries a
Clausius--Clapeyron factor. Reading off $\mathrm{d}\sigma/\mathrm{d}(v_n\epsilon)$ per
channel:
\begin{center}\small\begin{tabular}{llr}\toprule
channel & correct coefficient & value\\ \midrule
vapor & $1/D_v$ & $5.27\times10^{4}$\\
      & \emph{coded}: $1/\texttt{dif\_vap}$ & $4.59\times10^{4}$ \ \checkmark form, but $I=0$\\[2pt]
thermal & $(\mathrm{d}\rho_{vs}/\mathrm{d}T)\,L_{\rm sub}/k$ & $1.0\times10^{2}$ ($k_{\rm ice}$) -- $1.2\times10^{4}$ ($k_{\rm air}$)\\
        & \emph{coded}: $1/\texttt{diff\_sub}$ & $1.29\times10^{5}$ \ $\times$ over-weighted 11--1300$\times$\\ \bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}
\code{ThermalCond} interpolates between the two $k$'s, so \emph{any} reasonable choice
leaves this term negligible. This defect stands independently of everything above.

\subsection{What the measurement bounds --- and what it does not}
\begin{center}\small\begin{tabular}{lll}\toprule
spurious term & form & seen by the ladder?\\ \midrule
kinetic correction & $\propto v_n$ & \textbf{yes} --- $\le0.1\,\%$ of $\bbare$\\
solute trapping & $\propto\epsilon v_n$ (shifts $\sigma_{\rm int}$) & \textbf{yes} --- also $\propto v_n$, absorbed into fitted $\beta$\\
surface diffusion & tangential flux along the interface & \textbf{no} --- different operator\\ \bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}
The first two are dead. The third is invisible to a centerline $v_n$ measurement and is
\emph{not} bounded by anything in this note. One reassurance and one caveat: the surface
excess of the diffusivity weight, $\int[(1-\phi_0)-\Theta(-\eta)]\mathrm{d}\eta$, is
\emph{exactly zero} by the same antisymmetry, which suggests the spurious surface
diffusion is suppressed too --- but that is a suggestion, not the calculation. It matters
because the model has \emph{no} surface-diffusion term by construction, so a spurious one
would appear nowhere in the source, and the Molaro campaign is separately reasoning about
\emph{physical} surface diffusion. The two must not be conflated.
