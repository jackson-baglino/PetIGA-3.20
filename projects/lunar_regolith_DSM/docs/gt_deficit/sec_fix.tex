\section{Form of the required correction}\label{sec:fix}

\subsection{Exact relation}
The realized coefficient obeys, exactly and without further asymptotic analysis,
\begin{equation}
\beta_{\rm realized}=\bsub+\Delta ,\qquad \Delta \text{ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:delta}},
\label{eq:exact}
\end{equation}
verified as $\beta_{\rm fit}/\bbare=1.0009\pm0.0007$ over a range of $40$ in $\bsub$.
Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact} is not a scaling estimate with a fitted $O(1)$ prefactor; it is a
closed-form expression for a known spurious term, obtained from the model definition and
confirmed to within $0.1\,\%$.

\subsection{Inadequacy of a fixed multiplicative factor}
One might attempt to absorb the observed ratio $0.822$ into $\bsub$. This is not
admissible, since $0.822$ is not a constant: it equals $\bsub/(\bsub+\Delta)$, and $\Delta$
does not scale with $\bsub$. Calibrating the factor at the $1.00\times$ arm, corresponding
to $\bsub=0.7835\,\beta_{\rm target}$, and applying it to the remaining arms gives
\begin{center}\small\begin{tabular}{rrrr}\toprule
arm & $\Delta/\beta_{\rm target}$ & realized/target & error\\ \midrule
$0.10\times$ & 2.165 & 2.948 & $+195$\,\%\\
$0.25\times$ & 0.866 & 1.649 & $+65$\,\%\\
$1.00\times$ & 0.217 & 1.000 & $0$\,\%\\
$4.00\times$ & 0.054 & 0.838 & $-16$\,\%\\ \bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}
Applied to the Molaro configuration the same factor gives $-4.4\,\%$ on the
$D_v\!\times\!10$ arm. The sense of such a factor is also ambiguous:
$\Delta/\beta_{\rm target}=0.7835$ and $\bsub/\bbare=0.822$ are distinct quantities, and
since $\beta_{\rm realized}>\bsub$ the model is over-resistive, so any compensating factor
lies below unity rather than above it.

In one regime of interest no such factor exists. At the Molaro value
$\epsilon=1.2\times10^{-7}$\,m with $\alpha_c\to1$, $\beta_{\rm target}=7.94\times10^{3}$
while $\Delta=1.79\times10^{4}$; $\Delta$ exceeds the target by a factor of $2.3$, so
pre-compensation would require a negative \code{-beta\_sub0}, which the option parser
rejects. This is the attachment-limited regime relevant to a replication of Demmenie
et al.

\subsection{Equivalent formulations}
The following two operations are algebraically identical:
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.1em}
\item supply $\bsub=\beta_{\rm target}-\Delta$; or
\item remove the two $a_2$ terms from $\tau_{\rm sub}$ and supply $\beta_{\rm target}$.
\end{itemize}
Both yield $\beta_{\rm realized}=\beta_{\rm target}$. The second is preferable on three
grounds: it is applied once in the solver rather than in every run configuration; it cannot
be invalidated by a subsequent change of $\epsilon$ or $T$; and it remains well defined
when $\Delta>\beta_{\rm target}$, where pre-compensation admits no solution.

The required correction is therefore not a numerical factor but the removal of a
counter-term for a contribution the model does not generate, with \S\ref{sec:inner}
providing the justification and \S\ref{sec:ladder} the verification.

\section{Implementation}\label{sec:impl}

The change is to be made in \code{enceladus\_DSM} and back-ported to
\code{lunar\_regolith\_DSM}. The enceladus implementation is the more current of the two:
it already uses the temperature-corrected $D_v$ in the $a_2$ term, where the lunar code
retains the base $0\,^\circ$C value, and its \code{assembly.c} documents the convention
$W_{\rm KP}=\sqrt2\,\epsilon$.

\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{0.2em}
\item Make the correction explicit and optional in both the scalar path
      (\code{enceladus\_main.c:852}, \code{lunar\_main.c:829}) and the pointwise path
      (\code{SubKinetics}), which carry the same bracket:
\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
PetscReal c\_vap~~= a2 * eps / dv\_T;\\
PetscReal c\_therm = a2 * eps * drhovs\_dT * lat\_sub / k\_eff;\\
if (!user.thin\_iface\_corr) \{ c\_vap = 0.0; c\_therm = 0.0; \}\\
tau\_sub = eps * lambda\_sub * (beta\_sub / a1 + c\_therm + c\_vap);
\end{quote}
\item Set \code{-thin\_iface\_corr} to $0$ by default, documenting at the flag the
      conditions under which it should be re-enabled: a two-sided vapor diffusivity, the
      addition of an anti-trapping current, or a regime in which $\epsilon v_n/D_v$ is not
      negligible. The flag is a model-structure switch and should not be used as a fitting
      parameter.
\item Correct the thermal coefficient in the enabled branch rather than restoring its
      present form. The derivative \code{drhovs\_dT} is already computed and discarded at
      \code{lunar\_main.c:735} ($8.1521\times10^{-5}$\,kg\,m$^{-3}$K$^{-1}$ at
      $-20\,^\circ$C); \code{k\_eff} is obtained from \code{ThermalCond} at $\phi=1/2$.
\item Replace the base $D_v$ in the lunar implementation with the temperature-corrected
      value, for consistency with enceladus.
\item Report $\bbare/\bsub$ and the three bracket terms in the run header alongside
      $\tau_{\rm sub}$, so that each \code{outp.txt} records the separation between
      realized and requested kinetics.
\item Verify by repeating the $0.10\times$ arm of the parameter sweep (5.9\,d simulated,
      3406 steps), for which the predicted ratio changes from $0.32$ to $1.00$.
\item Revise the dependent calibration. \code{comp\_eps.py} sizes $\epsilon$ using
      \code{beta\_uns} in \code{eps\_ceiling} and \code{kin\_over\_curv}; with the
      correction removed this relation ceases to be implicit. Re-derive $\epsilon$ for the
      standard $-20\,^\circ$C geometries afterwards and confirm that the mesh element
      counts are unchanged.
\item Review previous results for dependence on absolute rates. Exponents and
      dimensionless ratios are unaffected; quantities such as times to a given state or
      absolute growth rates are displaced by the factor given in \S\ref{sec:delta} for the
      corresponding $(\epsilon,T)$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Outstanding items}\label{sec:open}

The spurious surface-diffusion contribution is the one $O(\epsilon)$ effect not constrained
by the present work (\S\ref{sec:inner}). Resolving it requires either the one-sided
asymptotic analysis for this model, by mapping onto Echebarria, Folch, Karma \& Plapp,
Phys.\ Rev.\ E \textbf{70}, 061604 (2004), whose weighting $q(\phi)$ corresponds to our
$\phi_a$, or the introduction of the anti-trapping current of Karma, Phys.\ Rev.\ Lett.\
\textbf{87}, 115701 (2001), which cancels it by construction:
\begin{equation}
\vec{\jmath}_{\rm at}=a_t\,\epsilon\,\rho_{\rm ice}\,(\partial_t\phi)\,
\frac{\nabla\phi}{|\nabla\phi|},
\end{equation}
added to the vapor flux, with $a_t$ evaluated for the Moure \& Fu well as $a_1$ and $a_2$
were. Neither is urgent, since the effects cancelled by the anti-trapping current scale as
$\epsilon v_n/D_v$, here $2.7\times10^{-14}$. Both should be completed before any
conclusion that requires distinguishing numerical from physical surface transport.

The dominant discrepancy in the Molaro comparison is not kinetic. Removing the attachment
resistance entirely increases the integrated neck-growth rate by a factor of $1.28$ over
the observed neck range, against a discrepancy of approximately $2$; no adjustment to
$\beta$ accounts for it. The imposed far-field undersaturation is the larger influence: in
arm B, $\sigma_\infty=-2.03\times10^{-3}$ against a curvature driving force at the neck of
$+6.57\times10^{-4}$, larger by a factor of $3.1$ and of opposite sign, so that the neck
surface is in net sublimation. The A/B and dom2/dom3 comparisons already isolate this
effect.
