\textbf{Problem.} At uniform temperature $T$, in the sharp-interface limit of
the model, the vapor density $\rho_v$ in the pore and the ice surface
$\Gamma$ (normal $\mathbf n$ into the pore, normal velocity $v_n > 0$ for
growth, curvature $\chi$) obey
\begin{align}
\pd{\rho_v}{t} &= D_v\,\nabla^{2}\rho_v && \text{in the pore,} \label{eq:diff}\\
\rho_\ice\,v_n &= D_v\,\pd{\rho_v}{n} && \text{on } \Gamma \text{ (conservation of water),} \label{eq:mass}\\
\frac{\rho_v - \rvs}{\rvs} &= d_0\,\chi + \beta\,v_n && \text{on } \Gamma \text{ (Gibbs--Thomson),} \label{eq:gt}
\end{align}
with
\begin{equation}
d_0 = \frac{\gamma\,m}{\rho_\ice\,k_B T},\qquad
\beta = \frac{1+\delta}{\alpha_c}\,\frac{\rho_\ice}{\rvs(T)}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi m}{k_B T}} ,
\label{eq:coef}
\end{equation}
where $\delta = \Delta\beta/\beta_{\mathrm{sub}} = 0.020$ is the
thin-interface term. Temperature enters through $\rvs(T)$, $D_v(T) \propto
T^{1.81}$, $d_0$ and $\beta$.

\textbf{Scales.} With $\Rbar$ the mean grain radius,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf x = \Rbar\,\mathbf x',\qquad
\chi = \frac{\chi'}{\Rbar},\qquad
\frac{\rho_v - \rvs}{\rvs} = \frac{d_0}{\Rbar}\,u,\qquad
v_n = \frac{d_0}{\Rbar\,\beta}\,v',\qquad
t = \tau_{\Rbar}\,t',\quad \tau_{\Rbar} \equiv \frac{\Rbar^{2}\beta}{d_0} .
\label{eq:scales}
\end{equation}
Supersaturation is measured in units of the equilibrium value for a grain of
radius $\Rbar$, velocity in the units that make the kinetic term comparable
to the capillary term, and time in units of $\Rbar$ divided by that velocity.

\textbf{Substitution.} Equation~\eqref{eq:gt} becomes $(d_0/\Rbar)\,u =
(d_0/\Rbar)\,\chi' + (d_0/\Rbar)\,v'$. In Eq.~\eqref{eq:mass},
$\partial\rho_v/\partial n = \rvs\,(d_0/\Rbar^{2})\,\partial u/\partial n'$,
so $v' = \left[D_v\,\beta\,\rvs/(\rho_\ice\Rbar)\right]\partial u/\partial n'$.
In Eq.~\eqref{eq:diff} both sides carry $\rvs d_0/\Rbar$, leaving
$(\Rbar^{2}/D_v\tau_{\Rbar})\,\partial u/\partial t' = \nabla'^{2}u$ with
$\Rbar^{2}/(D_v\tau_{\Rbar}) = d_0/(D_v\beta) \sim 10^{-11}$. The result is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\nabla'^{2}u = 0 \ \text{ in the pore;}\qquad
u = \chi' + v',\quad v' = \Lambda\,\pd{u}{n'} \ \text{ on } \Gamma'\;}
\label{eq:reduced}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\Lambda \equiv \frac{L^{*}}{\Rbar},\qquad
L^{*} \equiv \frac{D_v\,\beta\,\rvs}{\rho_\ice} = \frac{(1+\delta)\,D_v(T)}{\alpha_c}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi m}{k_B T}} .
\label{eq:Lstar}
\end{equation}
The saturation vapor density cancels in $L^{*}$. The problem has one
parameter, $\Lambda$, the ratio of attachment to diffusive resistance over a
distance $\Rbar$.

\textbf{Scaling relation.} For a given initial geometry, the surface evolves
as a function of $t/\tau_{\Rbar}$ and $\Lambda$ only. Hence any quantity $Q$ fixed
by the geometry (specific surface area, neck width,
$k_{\mathrm{eff}}/k_\ice$) satisfies
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;Q(t;\,T) = \widehat Q\!\left(\frac{t}{\tau_{\Rbar}(T)};\ \Lambda(T)\right),\qquad
\tau_{\Rbar}(T) = \frac{(1+\delta)\,\Rbar^{2}\rho_\ice^{2}}{\alpha_c\,\gamma}\;\frac{1}{\rvs(T)}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi k_B T}{m}}\;}
\label{eq:result}
\end{equation}
where $\tau_{\Rbar}$ follows from Eqs.~\eqref{eq:scales} and~\eqref{eq:coef}. Between
two temperatures the time scales are in the ratio
\begin{equation}
a_T \equiv \frac{\tau_{\Rbar}(T)}{\tau_{\Rbar}(T_{\mathrm{ref}})} = \sqrt{\frac{T}{T_{\mathrm{ref}}}}\;\frac{\rvs(T_{\mathrm{ref}})}{\rvs(T)} ,
\label{eq:aT}
\end{equation}
independent of $\Rbar$, $\alpha_c$ and $\gamma$. With $\rvs \propto
\exp(-L_{\mathrm{sub}}M_w/R_gT)$ this is an activation energy $E_a =
L_{\mathrm{sub}}M_w - \tfrac12 R_gT \approx 50$~kJ\,mol$^{-1}$.

From $-40$ to $-5\,^{\circ}$C, $\tau_{\Rbar}$ changes by a factor of 29 while
$\Lambda \propto D_v/\sqrt T \propto T^{1.31}$ changes by 20\%. Neglecting
that weak dependence,
\begin{equation}
Q(t;\,T) = Q\!\left(t/a_T;\ T_{\mathrm{ref}}\right):
\end{equation}
a change of temperature is a change of the unit of time. Eliminating time
between two such quantities gives a relation, such as $k_{\mathrm{eff}}$
against SSA, that contains no time scale and depends on temperature only
through $\Lambda$.

\textbf{Two time scales.} Both have the form $\ell^{2}\beta/d_0$, the time for
a surface of curvature $1/\ell$ to move a distance $\ell$. $\tau_{\Rbar}$
($\ell = \Rbar$) is the grain-scale sintering time; $\tau_{\mathrm{sub}}$
($\ell = \varepsilon$) is the relaxation time of the diffuse interface, the
parameter the model is written with. $\tau_{\mathrm{sub}} =
\varepsilon^{2}\beta/d_0 = (\varepsilon/\Rbar)^{2}\tau_{\Rbar}$. With
$\varepsilon/\Rbar = 1/50$ in every run, $\theta = t/\tau_{\mathrm{sub}} =
2500\,t/\tau_{\Rbar}$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{sub}}(T)/\tau_{\mathrm{sub}}(T_{\mathrm{ref}})
= a_T$.

\textbf{Neglected, with sizes over this range.} Vapor storage,
$d_0/(\xi_v D_v\beta) \le 2\times10^{-8}$. Vapor mass in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mass}, $\rvs/(\xi_v\rho_\ice) \le 4\times10^{-3}$. The effect
of latent heat on the local saturation,
\[
\frac{\rho_\ice L_{\mathrm{sub}}^{2}\,m\,\Rbar}{k_B T^{2}\,k_\ice\,\beta} \le 2.4\times10^{-3} .
\]
The relation requires a uniform temperature, a single transport mechanism
and an $\alpha_c$ independent of temperature. Numbers:
\texttt{check\_groups.py}.
