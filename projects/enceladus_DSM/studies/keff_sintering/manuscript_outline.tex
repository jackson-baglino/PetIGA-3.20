\textbf{Framing.} Sintering of ice by vapor transport, and the bulk
conductivity that results, under conditions a laboratory can reach; Enceladus
is the motivation and the closing implication. Every claim stays inside the
simulated range: $-40$ to $-5\,^{\circ}$C, air at 1~atm, sealed pores,
50~$\mu$m grains, 30~days, two dimensions.

\section{Discussion}
\textbf{State of the draft.} Two subsections, written for the earlier model.
Section~4.1 argues that $\beta_{\mathrm{sub}}(T)$ comes from Libbrecht's
$\sigma_0(T)$, that nothing was retuned, and that $-5\,^{\circ}$C is ``a
genuine prediction''. With a constant $\alpha_c$ none of that holds; the
section has to be rebuilt, not edited. Section~4.2 claims a collapse across
porosity, isotropy, and sintering ``on the order of decades'' near the Tiger
Stripes. About 40 citations print as ``?''.

\begin{itemize}
\item Why the collapse holds: attachment-limited transfer, fixed $\alpha_c$,
  one discretization; what would break it.
