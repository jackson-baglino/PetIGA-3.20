\section*{Summary}

Figure 8 is \textbf{not simulation output}. It is one closed-form expression,
\begin{equation}
  t(T,R) \;=\; \theta^{*}\,\taus(T,R),
  \label{eq:map}
\end{equation}
evaluated on a grid of temperature $T$ and grain radius $R$. One number comes
from the campaign, the anchor $\taus = 7822.3$~s at $-20\degC$ and
$R = 50~\mu$m; one result of the campaign justifies the form, the temperature
collapse; everything else is the model's own definition of $\taus$, written
in the quantities the model uses. Only one grain size and temperatures from
$-40$ to $-5\degC$ were simulated, so every other point of the map is an
extrapolation. Section~\ref{sec:lit} checks the annotations against the two
framing papers and Section~\ref{sec:limits} lists what the extrapolation
assumes. Every number below is recomputed by \texttt{check\_timescale\_map.py}
in this folder (output in \texttt{check\_timescale\_map.txt}).

\section{The phase-change time scale}

The model defines (solver banner; material-properties table of the manuscript)
\begin{align}
  \taus &= \frac{\varepsilon^{2}\,\beta_{\mathrm{sub}}}{d_0}, &
  \beta_{\mathrm{sub}} &= \frac{\rho_{\mathrm{ice}}}{\rvs(T)}\,
      \frac{1}{\alpha_c}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi m}{k_B T}}, &
  d_0 &= \frac{\gamma\,a^{3}}{k_B T},\quad a^{3} = \frac{m}{\rho_{\mathrm{ice}}} ,
\end{align}
with $\varepsilon$ the interface parameter, $\alpha_c$ the condensation
coefficient, $m$ the mass of a water molecule, $\gamma$ the ice--air
interfacial energy and $\rvs$ the saturation vapor density over ice.
Substituting,
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;\taus \;=\; \frac{\varepsilon^{2}\,\rho_{\mathrm{ice}}^{2}}
        {\alpha_c\,\gamma\,m}\;\frac{\sqrt{2\pi m k_B T}}{\rvs(T)}\;}
  \label{eq:tau}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:tau} reproduces the solver's $\taus$ at all five simulated
temperatures to within 0.03\% (60\,389 against 60\,390~s at $-40\degC$; 7822
against 7822~s at $-20\degC$).

\section{Temperature dependence}

In Eq.~\eqref{eq:tau} the temperature enters only as
\begin{equation}
  \taus \;\propto\; \varepsilon^{2}\,\frac{\sqrt{T}}{\rvs(T)} ,
  \label{eq:Tdep}
\end{equation}
and the map evaluates exactly this, with $\rvs(T)$ the model's own function,
the expression the solver uses (\texttt{rho\_vs\_sat} in
\texttt{comp\_eps.py}). No saturation pressure appears: the model has no pressure
variable, and none is needed. At the five simulated temperatures the map's
$\taus$ therefore equals the solver's (Table~\ref{tab:compare}); away from
them it is the same function evaluated at other temperatures.

\paragraph{Effective activation energy.}
$E = -R_g\,\mathrm{d}\ln[\rvs/\sqrt{T}]/\mathrm{d}(1/T) = 50.1$~kJ\,mol$^{-1}$
at 253~K and $50.2$ at 180~K. The same number follows from the solver's values
alone: $\ln(60\,390/2063)$ over the simulated range gives $50.1$~kJ\,mol$^{-1}$.
\emph{This corrects the ``about 48~kJ\,mol$^{-1}$'' quoted earlier in the
project notes}, which came from an ideal-gas form of $\rvs$ and not from the
model's.

\paragraph{Range of $\rvs(T)$.} The model's $\rvs$ rests on a polynomial fit
for the saturation state over ice. As recalled, such fits are stated for about
$-100$ to $0\degC$ (\emph{to verify}); the map uses the function down to 50~K.
It stays smooth and monotonic there, but below roughly 173~K it is the fit
extrapolated, not a tabulated property.

\section{Grain-size dependence}

There is \textbf{no grain-size data} in the figure. The vertical axis is the
variable $R$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:map}; only $R = 50~\mu$m was simulated.

\paragraph{The $R^{2}$ law must not be argued from $\varepsilon \propto R$.}
The campaign sets $\varepsilon = \bar R/50$. That is a numerical choice (it
fixes the mesh at any grain size), and $\taus = \varepsilon^{2}\beta_{\mathrm{sub}}/d_0$
is the relaxation time of the diffuse interface, not a physical time. Reading
``$\taus \propto \varepsilon^{2} \propto R^{2}$'' as the grain-size law would
make the physics follow from a discretization rule. It does not, and the
interface-width runs show it directly: on three packings at fixed
$R = 50~\mu$m, doubling $\varepsilon$ (so $\taus$ goes from 7822 to
31\,289~s, a factor 4.00) leaves the kinetics \emph{in physical time}
unchanged after the early transient. From day 8 to day 30, $k_{\mathrm{eff}}$
rises 8.1, 9.4 and 8.8\% at $\varepsilon = 1~\mu$m and 8.0, 9.6 and 8.7\% at
$2~\mu$m, and SSA falls 10.2--11.2\% against 10.1--10.3\%. If the rate
followed $1/\taus$ the coarser runs would be four times slower; they are not.
So at fixed $R$ the physical rate is independent of $\varepsilon$, as it
should be for a phase-field model near its sharp-interface limit, and $\taus$
by itself carries no grain-size information.

\paragraph{Where $R^{2}$ does come from: the sharp-interface law.} In the
limit the model is built to reproduce (Kaempfer \& Plapp, 2009), the interface
moves with normal velocity
\begin{equation}
  \beta_{\mathrm{sub}}\,v_n \;=\; u \;-\; d_0\,\kappa ,
  \label{eq:sharp}
\end{equation}
with $u$ the dimensionless supersaturation at the interface and $\kappa$ the
curvature. When attachment limits the rate, $u$ is uniform over the pore and
set by conservation of mass, so $v_n = (d_0/\beta_{\mathrm{sub}})\,(\bar\kappa
- \kappa)$: motion by curvature at fixed volume. Measuring lengths in $R$
($\kappa \sim 1/R$, $v_n \sim R/t$) gives the only time scale in the problem,
\begin{equation}
  \tau_R \;\equiv\; \frac{R^{2}\,\beta_{\mathrm{sub}}}{d_0} ,
\end{equation}
and no $\varepsilon$ appears. Two packings that differ only by a uniform
change of scale therefore evolve identically in $t/\tau_R$. This is the
grain-size law of the map. Because the campaign happens to use
$\varepsilon = R/50$, the two times are related by
$\taus = \tau_R/2500$, and that is the \emph{only} reason the map can be
written with $\taus$; the physical statement is $t \propto \tau_R$.

\paragraph{What this rests on.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{$\varepsilon$-converged kinetics.} Tested once, towards a coarser
  interface ($\times 2$, three packings): late-time rates agree as above;
  absolute $k_{\mathrm{eff}}$ differs by 1--3\% and the first day or so is
  $\varepsilon$-dependent. Not tested towards a finer interface
  ($\varepsilon = 0.5~\mu$m is an optional run in \texttt{TODO.md}).
\item \emph{Attachment-limited transfer.} Equation~\eqref{eq:sharp} with
  uniform $u$ needs $R \ll L^{*}$ (Section~\ref{sec:limits}); otherwise vapor
  diffusion adds a second time scale and the law steepens towards $R^{3}$.
\item \emph{Geometric similarity:} the same packing, scaled.
\item \emph{No run at a second grain size.} The law is derived, not measured,
  by us. It agrees with the grain-size dependence in Molaro et al.\ (2019);
  see Section~\ref{sec:lit}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Consequence for the sintering age.} $\theta = t/\taus$ is defined
with an $\varepsilon$-dependent time. It is a valid clock across temperature
because every run uses the same $\varepsilon$ and $\beta_{\mathrm{sub}}/d_0$
carries the temperature. It is not transferable as a number: the same
microstructural state that sits at $\theta = 331$ here sits at $\theta = 83$
in the $\varepsilon = 2~\mu$m runs. The $\varepsilon$-free age is
$t/\tau_R = \theta/2500$.

\section{Why time scales with $\taus$ at all}

Equation~\eqref{eq:map} assumes the microstructure is a function of the
sintering age $\theta = t/\taus$ alone. Across temperature this is the
campaign's main result (Figure~6): for each of the 25 packings, runs at five
temperatures give the same $k_{\mathrm{eff}}$ at matched SSA to within 0.27\%
(100 pairs; median 0.07\%), and the time needed to reach a given state follows
the ratio of $\taus$ to within 4.1\%. So within $-40$ to $-5\degC$ the
statement ``time to a given state $\propto \taus$'' is measured, to 4\%.

\section{The target age $\theta^{*}$}

$\theta^{*} = 331$ is the age the $-20\degC$ runs reach in 30~days:
$30~\mathrm{d}/7822.3~\mathrm{s} = 331.4$. It has no other significance; any
age gives the same map shifted by a constant factor. In the results, at
$\theta = 331$ the 15 well-connected packings (porosity $\le 0.375$) have
\[
  k_{\mathrm{eff}}/k_{\mathrm{eff},r} = 1.171 \pm 0.015, \qquad
  \mathrm{SSA}/\mathrm{SSA}_r = 0.819 \pm 0.007
\]
relative to the reference age $\theta_r = 30$ (mean and standard deviation over
packings). The map is therefore ``time for $k_{\mathrm{eff}}$ to rise 17\% and
SSA to fall 18\% from their values at $\theta_r$''.

\section{The formula as plotted}

\begin{equation}
  t(T,R) = \theta^{*}\,\taus^{\mathrm{ref}}\;
     \frac{\sqrt{T}\,/\,\rvs(T)}{\sqrt{T_{\mathrm{ref}}}\,/\,\rvs(T_{\mathrm{ref}})}
     \left(\frac{R}{R_{\mathrm{ref}}}\right)^{2},
\end{equation}
with $\theta^{*}=331$, $\taus^{\mathrm{ref}}=7822.3$~s,
$T_{\mathrm{ref}}=253.15$~K and $R_{\mathrm{ref}}=50~\mu$m.
It is evaluated for $50 \le T \le 260$~K and
$0.1~\mu\mathrm{m} \le R \le 1$~mm and binned at 1~h, 1~d, 1~yr, 1~kyr, 1~Myr
and 4.5~Gyr (\texttt{fig\_timescales} in \texttt{figures/sample\_figures.py}).

\paragraph{What Choukroun et al.\ say about grain size.} Not a measurement:
they used one grain size. Their statement (Sect.~5, reason 4 for
extrapolating) is that ``the effect of temperature on sintering rates strongly
dominates over that of grain size (Molaro et al., 2019), such that a 1 to 2
order of magnitude difference in the mean grain size between our samples and
actual fresh plume deposits \ldots\ is not anticipated to alter the
conclusions of this study.'' The map agrees on which variable dominates
(temperature moves the time by more than 30 orders of magnitude across the
plot, grain size by 8), but under an $R^{2}$ law one to two orders of magnitude
in grain size is two to four orders of magnitude in time, which is not
negligible for a stated timescale.

\paragraph{What Molaro et al.\ give for grain size.} Their Table~6 fits the
stage-1 sintering timescale as $\tau = f\,T^{g}$ for radii from 0.1 to
100~$\mu$m. From those fits, the local exponent
$\mathrm{d}\ln\tau/\mathrm{d}\ln R$ is 2.00 between 50 and 100~$\mu$m, 2.01
between 30 and 50, 1.95--2.02 between 10 and 30, and 1.7--2.3 between 1 and
10~$\mu$m, at 130--180~K. Their model, which includes surface diffusion and is
independent of ours, therefore also gives $\tau \propto R^{2}$ above about
10~$\mu$m. This is the best available support for the map's grain-size axis.

\paragraph{Their timescales against the map.} At 180~K Molaro's fit gives
2.3~d, 126~d and 8.2~yr for $R$ = 1, 10 and 50~$\mu$m; the map gives 194~d,
53~yr and 1.3~kyr. The two are not the same quantity (theirs is completion of
stage~1 with all mechanisms; ours is a 17\% rise in $k_{\mathrm{eff}}$ by vapor
transport), but the direction is informative: their full model is about 100
times faster than our vapor-only clock at this temperature, as expected if
surface diffusion carries cold sintering.

\section{What the extrapolation assumes}\label{sec:limits}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Temperature range.} Simulated: 233--268~K. Plotted: 50--260~K.
  The collapse is measured over 35~K and applied over 210~K.
\item \textbf{One grain size.} The $R^{2}$ law is a scaling argument,
  supported by Molaro's Table~6 but not by a run of ours.
\item \textbf{Attachment-limited transfer everywhere.} The crossover length
  between attachment and diffusion control is $L^{*} = D_v\,\beta_{HK}$,
  $\beta_{HK} = \alpha_c^{-1}\sqrt{2\pi m/(k_B T)}$: 139~$\mu$m at 253~K and
  89~$\mu$m at 180~K for air at 1~atm and $\alpha_c = 10^{-3}$. The $R^{2}$ law
  needs $R \ll L^{*}$. The simulated $R = 50~\mu$m is already at $R/L^{*} =
  0.36$, and the upper third of the map ($R > 100~\mu$m) is beyond it; there
  diffusion slows growth and the map \emph{under}estimates the time.
\item \textbf{Constant $\alpha_c = 10^{-3}$} at all temperatures. Its value
  near 100~K is not known to us.
\item \textbf{Air-filled pores at 1~atm.} The pore gas enters through $D_v$
  and $L^{*}$. Near the surface of Enceladus the pores are close to vacuum;
  Choukroun et al.\ make the same point about their sealed samples.
\item \textbf{Vapor route only.} Surface diffusion is absent from the model.
  Molaro et al.\ and Choukroun et al.\ both attribute cold sintering to it,
  the latter with $Q = 24.3 \pm 3.3$~kJ\,mol$^{-1}$ against our 50, and
  against the $\sim$51~kJ\,mol$^{-1}$ they quote for vapor diffusion. A
  missing mechanism can only make sintering faster, so the map is an
  \emph{upper bound on the time}: the time the vapor route alone would take.
\item \textbf{$\rvs(T)$ below its fitted range} (Section~2).
\item \textbf{The age--state relation} (SSA linear in $\log\theta$) is itself
  an intermediate-time approximation; the map extrapolates the clock, not
  that relation.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The Choukroun point.} The map gives 19~yr at 180~K and
$R = 6~\mu$m; Choukroun et al.\ give about 15~yr. These are not the same
quantity. Theirs is the time to a strength of 10~MPa by a mechanism they
identify as surface diffusion; ours is the time to an arbitrary sintering age
by vapor transport. The closeness of the two numbers is not evidence that the
model reproduces their experiment.
