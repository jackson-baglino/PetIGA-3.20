# jaumannDistributionIcyParticles2008 — Jaumann et al. 2008, Icarus 193:407–419 (doi 10.1016/j.icarus.2007.09.013)

**Summary.** Cassini VIMS (2005 flybys EN003/004/011, 5–30 km/pixel) water-ice band depths at 1.04, 1.25, 1.5, 2.0 μm compared with model spectra (2 μm–1 mm) to map ice particle diameter across ~30% of Enceladus; correlated with geologic units and crater ages and with CIRS temperatures across the tiger stripes.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- Abstract p.1: "The largest particles (∼0.2 mm) are concentrated in the so called 'tiger stripes' at the south pole"; "particle diameter of water ice increases toward younger tectonically altered surface units"; smallest in old cratered terrain.
- p.6: heavily cratered terrain 15 ± 5 μm; fractured/ridged plains 30 ± 10 μm; tectonically deformed regions 40–120 μm; tiger-stripe inner zone ">100 μm and even reach sizes >150 μm"; ±30% accuracy.
- p.9: "CIRS measurements across the tiger stripes indicate the highest temperatures (Spencer et al., 2006) exactly in those areas where VIMS identifies the largest ice particles"; "Higher temperatures support the growth of large particles (Clark et al., 1983)"; "material was either emplaced recently and/or has been undergoing thermal alteration." Also gravity sorting near vents (large particles fall closer).
- Particle size decreases away from fractures (Fig. 10, p.8).
- Overall size distribution peaks ~20 μm (p.8).

**Claims it supports / contradicts / qualifies**
- Outline L1329–1331 (Discussion 5.6 \Note: "grain size increasing toward the fractures in VIMS data (Jaumann)"): **supported** (p.1, p.8, Fig. 10). Jaumann attribute it to deposition dynamics and possible thermal alteration, not explicitly to sintering — the manuscript must not say Jaumann observed sintering.
- **Outline L1340–1342 (gap: "the grains are micron-sized")**: qualify — plume particles are micron-sized (Choukroun: mean radius 3.1 μm), but VIMS optical particle diameters on the surface are 15–30 μm on most terrain and 60 to >150 μm in the tiger stripes (p.6), i.e., comparable to the 100 μm diameter grains here. Optical "particle size" from band depth is an effective size (like SSA-derived diameters), so the honest statement is: "deposited plume grains are micron-sized, while near-infrared band depths indicate effective particle diameters of 15–150 μm at the surface, largest in the tiger stripes (Jaumann et al., 2008)".
- Draft L259 Tiger Stripes temperatures (Spencer & Nimmo 180 K): Jaumann's link of largest particles to highest CIRS temperatures supports the narrative that the warm stripes are where coarsening/sintering is most plausible (qualitatively).

**Candidate citations / claims**
- Discussion 5.6: cite Jaumann (p.6, p.9) for effective particle diameters up to >150 μm in the tiger stripes, coinciding with the warmest CIRS temperatures — the closest observational analogue in grain size to the simulated aggregates. Keep "no timescales" framing.
