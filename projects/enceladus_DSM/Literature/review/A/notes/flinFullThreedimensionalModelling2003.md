# flinFullThreedimensionalModelling2003 — Flin, Brzoska, Lesaffre, Coléou & Pieritz 2003, J. Phys. D 36:A49–A54 (doi 10.1088/0022-3727/36/10A/310)

PDF p.1 = IOP cover; A49 = PDF p.2.

**Summary.** 3D voxel model of isothermal, curvature-driven snow metamorphism (Kelvin + Langmuir–Knudsen; growth ∝ C_amb − C; volume-conserving; kinetics-limited, no vapor diffusion; extension of Bullard's 2D model), tested on geometric shapes and on μCT subvolumes from a 3-month isothermal experiment at −2 °C (Col de Porte snow, 4.91 μm voxels). Time scale arbitrary (k0 = 1).

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- Mechanism p.3 (A50): "we assume that metamorphism is limited by the evaporation–condensation mechanism. This approximation corresponds to suppose that the vapour diffusion in the air is fast enough so that the vapour partial pressure is homogenous in the air phase. Surface diffusion, which generally occurs at first stages of the metamorphism [16] is neglected"; equivalent to "surface-attachment-limited-kinetics (SALK)".
- Unknown time scale p.4 (A51): "As k0 depends on numerous physical constants whose a part (in particular, α coefficient) is not precisely known, its value was arbitrary set to the unity. It implies that experimental and numerical times differ by an undetermined multiplicative constant."
- **2D vs 3D necks** p.5 (A52): "The evolution of a neck is directly related to its initial size. Note that it is a typical three-dimensional phenomenon: in two dimensions, a neck is always growing, whatever its size is."
- SSA and mean curvature follow a logarithmic law in time over the experimental range at −2 °C (p.6, Fig. 5).
- Real snow differs: grains that disconnect fall, preserving connectivity and densifying (p.6).

**Claims it supports / contradicts / qualifies**
- Draft L262 (sintering examples) and L279 (open questions: literature centered on snow, Flin 2003/2004): Flin 2003 is a curvature-driven metamorphism model of snow, not grain-pair sintering — outline \tC (L392–394) confirmed. Fine as an example of isothermal metamorphism modelling.
- Outline L1050 / L1317 ("SSA falls linearly in log time" in isothermal snow): Flin 2003 Fig. 5 shows logarithmic SSA decay at −2 °C (functional form only) — supports the form, from experiment and model.
- **Limitations (2D)**: Flin 2003 p.5 is a direct, closely matching statement that in 2D a neck always grows whereas in 3D small necks can be removed. This bears on the manuscript's 2D aggregates (all point contacts become necks; outline L991–994 says point contacts become necks). Add to Section 5.6 "Numerics and geometry": in two dimensions every contact neck grows, while in three dimensions small necks can shrink and break (Flin et al., 2003), so 2D likely overstates neck formation and the conductivity gain.
- Section 2.2 / 5.1 (α_c rescales time in an attachment-limited model): Flin p.4 supports (time differs by an undetermined multiplicative constant set by α).
