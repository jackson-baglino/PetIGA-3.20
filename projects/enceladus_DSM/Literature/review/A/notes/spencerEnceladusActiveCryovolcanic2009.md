# spencerEnceladusActiveCryovolcanic2009 — Spencer et al. 2009, "Enceladus: An active cryovolcanic satellite", in Saturn from Cassini-Huygens (Springer), ch. 21
Read (book chapter, 200 kB; interior/tectonics sections read for relevance, surface/thermal/plume sections closely).
- Abstract p.1: tiger stripes "radiate heat at temperatures up to at least 167 K"; plumes eject "micron-sized H2O ice grains".
- Sect 21.5.2.2: CIRS fit ~133 K over ~345 km² (strip ~700 m wide); "if the plume gases ... were in vapor pressure equilibrium with surface ice ... a temperature of at least 180K could be inferred for the gas source ... upper limit to the width of the plume source is 50 m" (citing Spencer 2006).
- Thermal inertia: average 15 MKS, "increasing thermal inertia towards the active south pole"; "A highly unconsolidated particulate surface at least a few centimeters deep"; equatorial peak ~75 K at noon, 50–60 K night; north pole unconsolidated to ≥1 m.
- Grain size: larger grains (up to ~100 μm) near tiger stripes (ISS, Porco 2006); possible causes include "growth of grains resulting from deposition of plume gases or from the enhanced temperatures near the tiger stripes"; VIMS: grain size increases with decreasing surface age (Jaumann 2008).
- Most near-surface plume particles fall back (scale height 30 km).
Claims:
- "Tiger Stripe temperatures up to about 180 K" (draft L259 via Spencer&Nimmo 2013; outline L1330): PARTIAL — measured surface emission ≥167 K (abstract); 180 K is an inferred lower limit for the vent/gas source, ≤50 m wide, not the broad deposit surface. Say "fracture walls reach at least 167–180 K; surfaces nearby ~130 K over a few hundred m".
- Thermal inertia increasing toward the active south pole + larger grains near stripes: consistent with (not proof of) sintering/coarsening raising conductivity — candidate citation for Sec 5.4, worded as consistency only.
- Draft L259 "unconsolidated granular packings": SUPPORTED by Spencer 2009 (thermal inertia, unconsolidated ≥ few cm; ≥1 m at north pole) — better citation than Molaro.
