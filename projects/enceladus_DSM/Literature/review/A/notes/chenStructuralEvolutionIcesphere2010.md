# chenStructuralEvolutionIcesphere2010 — Chen & Baker 2010, Hydrol. Process. 24:2034–2040 (doi 10.1002/hyp.7787)

**Summary.** Experimental. 2D single-layer arrays of ~4 mm ice spheres (water dropped into liquid N2; polycrystalline, with air bubbles) in a cold room (text layer drops minus signs; "10 °C" cold room = −10 °C), one array quasi-isothermal for 18 days, one under ~500 °C m^-1 (plates at −20/−10 °C) for 8 h; optical microscopy time series + LT-SEM/EBSD. Steady conduction model of two spheres with wide vs. narrow neck (x/D = 0.5 vs 0.05).

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- Framing p.1: 2D ice-sphere arrays "as a geometrically simplified model of dry snow aggregates" (abstract) — precedent for idealized sphere/disc packings as snow analogues.
- Classical sintering exponents p.1: Kuczynski power law x^n/R^m ∝ f(T) t; Kingery (1960) surface diffusion "requires a very high surface diffusion coefficient"; Kuroiwa volume diffusion; "Hobbs and Mason (1964) proposed sublimation–condensation and water vapour transport as the dominant mechanisms, a proposal that has since received wide acceptance (Blackford, 2007; Adams et al., 2008)." No exponent values given.
- Isothermal necks grew by "growth of ice crystals in the pore spaces" over 18 days (p.3); a pre-existing solid neck showed "no significant evolution" (p.4–5); protrusions sometimes retreated.
- Narrow necks concentrate the temperature gradient in a conducting two-sphere structure (p.5, Fig. 10): "in the narrow-neck case, the temperature difference was greatly concentrated."
- Hobbs & Mason 1964 reference with DOI 10.1080/14786436408229184 (p.7).
