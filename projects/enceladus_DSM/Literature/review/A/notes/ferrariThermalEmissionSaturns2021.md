# ferrariThermalEmissionSaturns2021 — Ferrari, Lucas & Jacquemoud 2021, A&A 655, A8 (doi 10.1051/0004-6361/202141223)

**Summary.** Thermophysical model of icy regolith (Ferrari & Lucas 2016 conductivity formalism: radiative + contact conduction, grain size, porosity, ice phase, contact type; Hapke albedo/emissivity) coupled with Dione shape models (flat, tessellated ellipsoid, Gaskell GTM at 1–8 km) to reproduce one Cassini CIRS FP1 latitudinal scan of Dione's leading hemisphere. Not about Enceladus specifically; Saturn icy-moon temperatures ~80 ± 30 K.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- Low thermal inertia of Saturn's icy moons p.2: "Thermal inertia were found to be very weak, around 10–20 J m^−2 K^−1 s^−1/2"; explained by high porosity, amorphous ice, or rough grains with poor contacts (Ferrari & Lucas 2016).
- Conductivity value p.6: "A constant thermal conductivity kE0 = 10^−3 W m^−1 K^−1 yields similar thermal inertia" (Dione Γ ≈ 10 MKS) — the source of Byrne's lower bound 0.001.
- **Sintering and thermal inertia** p.2: "If the observed lens-shaped patterns can be explained by an alteration of the surface by MeV electrons, the mechanism of grain sintering and the reason for thermal inertia increases in the icy regolith remain poorly understood (Ferrari & Lucas 2016; Schaible et al. 2017)." p.10: regional grain-size variation "may originate from sintering after bombardment by MeV electrons in the lens-shaped region".
- Contact conduction "may be indeed very limited due to grain roughness" (p.3); contact conductivity scales with 1/R for loose contacts (p.10).
- Vacuum: radiative conductivity in pores ∝ R T³ (p.3) — no gas conduction (airless).
