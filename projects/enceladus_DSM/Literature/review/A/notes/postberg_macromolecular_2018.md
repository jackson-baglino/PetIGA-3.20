# postberg_macromolecular_2018 — Postberg, Khawaja et al. 2018, Nature 558:564–568 (doi 10.1038/s41586-018-0246-4)

Main text and Methods read in full; Extended Data captions read; long organic-chemistry/precursor Methods sections read for relevance (nothing on grain physics beyond sizes).

**Summary.** Cassini CDA impact-ionization spectra (7,353 E-ring spectra 2004–2008) and INMS fast-vs-slow flyby residuals show high-mass organic cations (HMOC, >200 u) in ~3% of type-2 (organic-bearing) grains, ≥4% of grains near Enceladus' orbit at optimal speeds. Interpreted as macromolecular aromatic/aliphatic organics from an organic film on the ocean water table, dispersed by bubble bursting and coated by condensing ice.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- p.565: "Ion abundances are indicative of an organic fraction in ice grains up to the per cent level ... Particle radii are mostly between 0.2 μm and 2 μm."
- p.564: "about 25% of the ice grain spectra of the E ring, the so-called type-2 spectra, exhibit the presence of organic material".
- Methods: "the actual fraction of ice grains carrying substantial amounts of complex organic material ejected into the E ring is at least 4% of the grains in the size range covered by the CDA (grain radius r ≈ 0.2–2 μm)".
- p.567: type-1 nearly pure water ice from vapour condensation; type-3 salt-rich frozen ocean spray (citing Postberg 2009, 2011); HMOC grains are salt-poor.

**Claims it supports / contradicts / qualifies**
- Draft L259 "The ice grains that form this plume are micron-sized, composed primarily of water ice, and contain percent level NaCl (Postberg 2009) and complex organic materials (Postberg 2018)": **supported** for the organics (macromolecular organics, up to per-cent level in a few % of grains) and for micron size (radii 0.2–2 μm, CDA range). Precision: organics are in a minority population (~25% organic-bearing; ≥4% with macromolecules), not in all grains — "some grains carry complex organic material" is the accurate wording.
- These are E-ring/plume grains in space; grains on the surface are larger (Jaumann 2008: 15–150 μm diameter) — the "micron-sized" statement must stay about the plume, as the draft has it.

**Candidate citations / claims**
- None beyond current use; suggested wording "a few per cent of the grains carry macromolecular organics (Postberg et al., 2018)".
