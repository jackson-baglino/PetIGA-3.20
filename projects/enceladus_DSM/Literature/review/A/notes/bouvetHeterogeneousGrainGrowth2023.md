# bouvetHeterogeneousGrainGrowth2023 — Bouvet, Calonne, Flin & Geindreau 2023, The Cryosphere 17:3553–3573 (doi 10.5194/tc-17-3553-2023)

**Summary.** Purely experimental. Two cold-room temperature-gradient-metamorphism (TGM) experiments at ~100 K m^-1 on sieved natural snow (A: DF, 210 kg m^-3, −3.1 to −15.6 °C, 20 d; B: RG, 287 kg m^-3, −6.5 to −14.5 °C, 28 d), sampled and imaged by X-ray tomography (8–21 μm voxels), plus T and RH sensors. Reports vertical heterogeneity of grain growth (linked to Nakaya basal/prismatic regimes), basal mass loss (3.6 mm air gap), and hard depth hoar with increasing SSA. **No model, no condensation coefficient, no time rescaling.**

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- Exp. A SSA 44.5 → 17.5 m² kg^-1 in 20 d, density ~constant after first 2 d (p.10, printed 3562): "After the first 2 d, the density remains stable to the end of the experiment. The SSA decreases overall with time".
- Exp. B: SSA fell then rose (39.6 → 27.5 → 33.3 m² kg^-1) with hard depth hoar (p.12, 3564); "opposed to the typical logarithmic decrease in SSA reported in most of the metamorphism studies" (p.16, 3568).
- Conductivity depends on microstructure, not only density: p.15 (3567) "the thermal conductivity is at first order driven by the density, but Calonne et al. (2011) have shown that it is also dependent on the microstructural arrangement."
- SSA computed by voxel projection (Flin et al. 2011) (p.7, 3559).
