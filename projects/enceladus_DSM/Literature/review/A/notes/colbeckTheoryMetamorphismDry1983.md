# colbeckTheoryMetamorphismDry1983 — Colbeck 1983, JGR 88(C9):5475–5482 (doi 10.1029/JC088iC09p05475)

**Summary.** Theory of temperature-gradient (TG) metamorphism: interparticle vapor diffusion between warmer source and colder sink grains (electrostatic capacitance solutions, geometric enhancement factor g, log-normal distribution of g), with a surface-kinetics law (from Lamb & Hobbs 1971 data) and a critical supersaturation (excess vapor density 5–6×10^-10 g cm^-3, from Colbeck 1983 J. Appl. Phys.) separating rounded from faceted growth. Explains depth hoar near the warm base and below crusts. Not about isothermal sintering.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- Curvature effects small vs. imposed gradients p.1 (5475): "If no temperature gradient were imposed on a snow cover, small chemical potential differences due to differences in curvature and stress would cause small temperature and vapor pressure differences ... These small differences would cause slow grain and neck growth by vapor diffusion and heat flow. However, in nature these self-imposed temperature gradients are small"; conclusions p.7: "the Kelvin effect on vapor pressure has not been included in this theory."
- Diffusion vs kinetics p.4 (5478): "in the snow cover, β >> 1 and the growth rate is limited by vapor diffusion rather than surface kinetics" (under TG).
- **Isothermal case kinetics-limited and strongly T-sensitive** p.5 (5479): for Bader et al. (1939) "destructive metamorphism of a snowflake held in an isolated environment with very small, self-imposed vapor pressure and temperature differences ... the value of β was very small, indicating that surface kinetic restrictions on crystal growth were important. Accordingly, the growth should be quite temperature sensitive, and ... there was a large difference in growth rate between −3.8° and −11.5 °C. The difference was much too large to be explained by the temperature dependence of D and/or dp/dT."
- Density constant during TG recrystallization; old bonds lost faster than new ones form, "a layer of snow undergoing rapid grain growth will lose its strength unless the new crystals sinter" (p.2).
- Critical TG 0.1–0.2 deg/cm (10–20 K m^-1) for faceting (p.1).
