# demmenieIceSinteringSublimation2025 — Demmenie, Woutersen & Bonn 2025, J. Phys. Chem. Lett. 16:2104–2109 (doi 10.1021/acs.jpclett.5c00050)

**Summary.** Experiments on pairs of ~1 mm diameter ice spheres (frozen droplets on syringes) in contact horizontally, no external pressure, at −3 °C in a humidity-controlled box set to ice saturation (Murphy & Koop 2005), imaged from above for 2.5 h; plus healing of a ~4 μm deep, ~280 μm long scratch in an ice layer, compared with Mullins' linear evolution equations for n = 1–4. Concludes sintering at vapor-pressure equilibrium proceeds by sublimation–condensation.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- Exponent table p.2 (2105), Eq. 1 (r/R0)^m = C_n(T) t/R0^n and Table 1: viscous flow m = 1 (they argue the classical m = 2 is a mistake), sublimation–condensation m = 3, bulk diffusion m = 5, surface diffusion m = 7; so α = 1/m (Eq. 2).
- Sublimation–condensation derivation p.2: "In classical gas theory, the rate of molecule emission is directly related to the vapor pressure and, consequently, the surface curvature ... one would find t ∼ r^3" — i.e., the emission/attachment (Hertz–Knudsen) picture with no gas-diffusion resistance, small-neck approximation.
- Measured exponents p.3 (2106), Fig. 3: "values for exponent α of 0.29 ± 0.01, 0.33 ± 0.01, 0.30 ± 0.01, and 0.26 ± 0.01", fit r = (t − t0)^α with "t0 represents an experimental delay between the initial contact of the spheres and the first image"; "Errors were determined by adjusting t0 by one frame (±15 s)."
- Undersaturated p.3: "When we deliberately set the humidity too low and let the ice sublimate within 2.5 h, the competition between neck growth and sublimation resulted in an exponent of ∼1/7 (see Figure S2)." (SI not in library.)
- Kingery's 1/7 attributed to humidity: p.2 "A notable example is the work of Kingery, who reported an exponent α of ∼1/7 ... this outcome resulted from an underestimation of the relative humidity."
- Range in literature p.1: "different experiments reported values of α ranging between 1/3 and 1/7."
- Snow vs ice p.1–2: snow densifies on similar time scales because "the open structure of snowflakes allows gravity to exert sufficient external pressure"; growth-rate laws "should not be applied to snow sintering".
- Maeno & Ebinuma exception p.4 (2107): surface diffusion predominant only "for neck sizes that were <8% of the initial sphere diameter (r/R0 < 0.08)".
- Mass/volume constant at equilibrium (p.3) — no shrinkage.
- Uses neck *radius* r normalized by R0 (Fig. 1b).
