# postberg_sodium_2009 — Postberg, Kempf, Schmidt, Brilliantov, Beinsen, Abel, Buck & Srama 2009, Nature 459:1098–1101 (doi 10.1038/nature08046)

Whole letter read (pp. 1098–1101). Text layer drops μ ("radii of 0.1–1 mm" = 0.1–1 μm from context) and flattens exponents/superscripts.

**Summary.** CDA spectra of E-ring grains (Oct 2004–Dec 2005): ~93% show Na; ~6% ("type III") are Na-rich with NaCl, NaHCO3/Na2CO3 (salts 0.5–2% by mass), reproduced by laser-dispersion of 0.05–0.2 mol/kg NaCl solutions at pH 8.5–9; the Na-poor majority (types I, II; >90%) have Na/H2O 10^-8–10^-5. Implies a salty liquid reservoir (ocean); Na-rich grains are frozen spray, Na-poor grains condense from vapour.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- p.1098: "The Cosmic Dust Analyser ... recorded thousands of mass spectra of E-ring particles, predominantly with radii of 0.1–1 [μm]."
- p.1098: "About 6% of these spectra (hereafter 'type III') show a unique mass line pattern which we now attribute to ice particles with particularly high sodium contents"; abstract: "rich in sodium salts (0.5–2% by mass)".
- p.1098: "The low Na content of type I and II grains (which amount to more than 90% of the recorded spectra)".
- p.1100: "most of the total ejected grain mass falls back to the moon's surface (the cryo-volcanically active regions should be covered with Na-bearing plume particles)".
- p.1100: plume sources from fractures; cites Spitale & Porco 2007.

Fix: "a few per cent of them are salt-rich, with 0.5–2% sodium salts by mass".
- Plume deposits: p.1100 statement that most grain mass falls back and covers the active regions supports the premise "plume deposits blanket the south polar terrain" — candidate citation beside Kempf 2010/Southworth 2019.
- Molaro 2019 Sect. 5.4 "up to 2% NaCl by mass (Postberg et al., 2009)" — consistent with Postberg's 0.5–2% for type III.
