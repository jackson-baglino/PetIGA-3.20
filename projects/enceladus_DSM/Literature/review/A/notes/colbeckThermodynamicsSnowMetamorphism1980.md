# colbeckThermodynamicsSnowMetamorphism1980 — Colbeck 1980, J. Glaciol. 26(94):291–301 (doi 10.3189/S0022143000010832)

**Summary.** Thermodynamic analysis (Gibbs–Duhem, Laplace, Kelvin) of curvature-driven ("equi-temperature" / "radius-of-curvature") metamorphism of dry snow, plus liquid-saturated and low-liquid-content wet snow. Shows that in dry snow without imposed gradients the relative change of vapor pressure greatly exceeds the relative change of temperature, so the isothermal Kelvin equation applies and vapor diffusion is rate-limiting; argues curvature-driven metamorphism is slow and matters only for fresh snow in a seasonal cover.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- Metamorphism changes bulk properties p.1 (Intro): "The metamorphism of snow causes large changes in snow properties, especially strength, permeability, reflectivity, and thermal conductivity."
- Driving force p.1 abstract: "In the absence of imposed temperature gradients, the metamorphism of dry snow is dominated by the slow process of vapor diffusion between surfaces of different radii of curvature."
- Vapor transport route p.3 (293): "Hobbs and Mason (1964) and Hobbs and Radke (1967), examined the sintering of ice spheres and found that mass transfer through the vapor is more important than transfer by surface or volume diffusion."
- Nearly isothermal p.6 (296): (dp_g/p_g) ≈ −560 (dT/T); "the relative change in vapor pressure is much larger than the relative change in temperature and therefore the constant-temperature Kelvin's equation ... is a good approximation" — independent support for the outline's statement that latent heat is not a candidate (neck 0.002 mK warmer than grain surface).
- Curvature-driven temperature gradient ~10^-2 deg m^-1 for 0.1 mm radii (p.6); imposed gradients usually dominate in seasonal snow (p.6–7, conclusion p.9–10). "Radius of curvature is important in firnification of cold ice sheets" (p.3).
- Snow metamorphism simulates sintering of "high-temperature metal and ceramic powders, but at temperatures more convenient" (p.1).
