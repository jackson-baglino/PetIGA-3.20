# choukroun_strength_2020 — Choukroun, Molaro, Hodyss, Marteau, Backes, Carey, Dhaouadi, Moreland & Schulson 2020, GRL 47, e2020GL088953 (doi 10.1029/2020GL088953)

**Summary.** Laboratory: fine-grained crystalline ice (12 μm mean diameter, air-atomized into liquid N2), unpacked, porosity 51.5 ± 1.6%, 0.66–2.14 kg samples, sintered isothermally in sealed containers "at water vapor saturation pressure" at 193, 223, 233, 243 K for months up to 14 months; cone penetration resistance measured repeatedly. Strength grows linearly in time; Arrhenius fit of rates → Q = 24.3 ± 3.3 kJ mol^-1; extrapolated to Enceladus and Europa.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- Linear strengthening p.3: "At each temperature, strengthening is observed over time and forms seemingly linear trends"; 193 K: 2–3 MPa after ~400 days; 243 K: 7–10 MPa in 60 days.
- Activation energy p.3: "Q = 24.3 ± 3.3 kJ/mol (95% confidence interval)", "comparable to ... the ~23 kJ/mol activation energy of H2O self-diffusion on the surface of ice grains (Nasello et al., 2007)".
- **Not vapor**: p.4 "It is not consistent with the activation energy associated with ice recrystallization (~50 kJ/mol) nor with the volume (~60 kJ/mol) or vapor (~51 kJ/mol) diffusion mechanisms that contribute to the sintering process (Molaro et al., 2019). This suggests that the strengthening ... is primarily due to the evolution of a mesoscale network between individual grains, or agglomerates thereof."
- Conclusions p.8: "This value is consistent with a process dominated by self-diffusion of H2O molecules on the surface of ice grains."
- No densification: p.6 "The highest resistance recorded in our experiments was 14 MPa, but it was still increasing linearly at the time and no densification had yet occurred."
- Enceladus p.7: "Under typical Enceladus surface conditions, little to no strengthening is expected for plume deposits. In the south polar region, it would take at least the age of the solar system for the deposits to develop a resistance on order of 1 MPa ... Under Enceladus equatorial noontime temperatures, plume deposits would still take about 100 Myr to reach 1 MPa ... anticipated to remain poorly consolidated". South polar mean < 50 K, equatorial noon > 80 K (Howett 2010), Tiger Stripes ~180 K (Spencer & Nimmo 2013) (p.6).
- Tiger Stripes p.7: "plume deposit materials would develop a cone penetration resistance around 10 MPa in ~15 years ... very consolidated."
- Burial p.7: "these regions close to the Tiger Stripes are also areas where the deposition of new plume deposits would be most intense, on order of 0.01 to 1 mm/yr (Kempf et al., 2010; Southworth et al., 2019). There would be competition between strengthening ... and covering by new fresh and loose particles".
- Plume context p.1–2: "Cassini observed over a hundred jets converging into a plume (Porco et al., 2006), which originates from a set of four rectilinear surface fractures dubbed Tiger Stripes (Spitale & Porco, 2007)"; "micron-sized particles mostly composed of water ice ... percent-level NaCl (Postberg et al., 2009, 2011) and complex organic materials (Postberg et al., 2018)"; mean radius 3.1 ± 0.5 μm (Ingersoll & Ewald 2011); radii 0.1–5 μm dominate deposits; deposition ~1 μm/yr average, up to 1 mm/yr near jets.
- Snow is "a relatively poor analog" because of melt–refreeze (p.2).
- Assumption (4) p.6: "The effect of temperature on sintering rates strongly dominates over that of grain size (Molaro et al., 2019)".
