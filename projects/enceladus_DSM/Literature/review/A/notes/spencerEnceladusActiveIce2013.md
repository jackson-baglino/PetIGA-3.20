# spencerEnceladusActiveIce2013 — Spencer & Nimmo 2013, Annu. Rev. Earth Planet. Sci. 41:693–717 (doi 10.1146/annurev-earth-050212-124025)
Read (review; geology/thermal/surface sections closely, interior/orbital sections for relevance).
- p.~700: tiger-stripe troughs (~2 km wide, 0.5 km deep) are the source of thermal emission and jets; "surface temperatures within the troughs reach at least 180 K over regions tens of meters wide along the length of the tiger stripes (Spencer et al. 2009, 2011)"; total 15.8 ± 3.1 GW.
- Surface: "largest [grain] sizes are found near the tiger stripes (Jaumann 2008; Porco 2006)"; low-latitude T ~50 K night to ~80 K day (Howett 2010); "very low surface thermal conductivity and a fluffy unconsolidated surface, as expected for plume particle fallout".
- Plume fallout pattern matches surface colour patterns (Kempf 2010; Schenk 2011).
- Fracture walls >200 K (Abramov & Spencer 2009); condensation on walls closes cracks up to ~1 m/yr.
Claims:
- Draft L259 "Temperatures near Tiger Stripes may reach up to 180 K": SUPPORTED but must say "within the trough, over tens of metres" (not the whole deposit); "at least 180 K", so "up to" is the wrong direction — reword "reach at least 180 K in strips tens of metres wide".
- "Much of Enceladus' surface ... unconsolidated" (draft L259): SUPPORTED here ("fluffy unconsolidated surface ... plume particle fallout") — cite Spencer & Nimmo 2013 instead of Molaro.
- Outline Sec 5.4 grain size increasing toward fractures: supported.
