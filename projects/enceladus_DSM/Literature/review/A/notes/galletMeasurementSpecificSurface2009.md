# galletMeasurementSpecificSurface2009 — Gallet, Domine, Zender & Picard 2009, The Cryosphere 3:167–182 (doi 10.5194/tc-3-167-2009)

**Summary.** Instrument paper: DUFISSS integrating sphere measuring hemispherical IR reflectance of snow samples at 1310 nm (SSA < 60 m² kg^-1, accuracy 10%) and 1550 nm (SSA > 60 m² kg^-1, accuracy 12%), calibrated against CH4 adsorption at 77 K; DISORT and ray-tracing (SNOWRAT) modelling; field use in Alpine/Arctic/Antarctic; ~1 min per SSA value.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- SSA definition p.1: "Snow SSA is defined as the surface area per unit mass ... SSA = S/M = S/(ρ_ice V)"; SSA = 6/(ρ_ice d_eff) (Eq. 1). Typical range 2 (melt-freeze) to 156 m² kg^-1 (fresh dendrites).
- Methods reviewed p.2: CH4 adsorption (reproducibility 6%, 3 h), stereology, X-ray tomography (insufficient for SSA > 70 m² kg^-1), NIR photography (Matzl & Schneebeli 2006), contact spectroscopy (Painter 2007).
- "rapid and accurate method ... in the field ... in about one minute" (p.4); conclusion: "accuracy better than 12%" (p.10).
- SSA changes with metamorphism, rate "not elucidated" (p.2).

**Claims it supports / contradicts / qualifies**
- Outline L561–564 ("SSA is measured in the field and laboratory (Matzl & Schneebeli 2006; Gallet et al. 2009; Proksch et al. 2015), so a law in SSA can be applied"): **supported** (field and lab SSA by IR reflectance; one value per minute; 10–12%).
- Outline L1317–1319 (Section 5.4: "SSA is measured by near-infrared reflectance, gas adsorption and tomography"): supported — Gallet p.2 reviews all three.

**Candidate citations / claims**
- Note for 5.4: the ice samples there would be sintered ice-sphere packings; Gallet's 1310 nm calibration (DISORT spheres) assumes ρ > 200 kg m^-3 or a density correction (Eq. 8) — sphere packings at φ 0.3–0.5 (ρ ≈ 460–640 kg m^-3) are within the "dense" case. Fine as a citation for feasibility.
