# molaroMicrostructuralEvolutionWater2019 — Molaro, Choukroun, Phillips, Phelps, Hodyss, Mitchell, Lora & Meirion-Griffith 2019, JGR Planets 124:243–276 (doi 10.1029/2018JE005773; corrected 10 Aug 2020)

Whole paper read (pp. 243–273 + reference list start), in four parts. Numbers taken from text layer; superscripts are flattened in the text (e.g. "103 years" = 10^3 yr) — interpreted from context.

**Summary.** Implements Swinkels & Ashby (1981) (SA81) for water ice; Stage 1 neck growth by six mechanisms (Table 3); compares with historical data (Hobbs & Mason 1964, Kingery 1960, Thomas 1994; Fig. 8) and with new grain-pair (−20, −5 °C) and bulk-aggregate (−20, −80 °C, 10 weeks) experiments; Stage 1 timescale maps vs T and grain radius; implications for planetary surfaces (strength, conductivity, Enceladus deposition, salts).

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- Abstract p.243: sintering "through surface and volume diffusion ... resulting in the evolution of its strength, porosity, thermal conductivity".
- p.244: "Later studies found both vapor transport and surface diffusion to be important, the latter being primarily dominant at temperatures below −15 °C when neck sizes are small (e.g., Löwe et al., 2011; Maeno & Ebinuma, 1983; Vetter et al., 2010)."
- p.254–255: "Stage 1 in ice is strongly dominated by surface V1 and vapor diffusion V3, both nondensifying mechanisms (Figure 10)"; almost no densification during neck growth.
- App. B2 p.271–272: "Neck growth during sintering of water ice is dominated by vapor diffusion (V3)"; "surface diffusion dominates over vapor diffusion during the early stages of the process (Figure 10)"; "Grains 100 μm in radius showed no significant effects from neglecting surface diffusion on any timescales".
- App. A p.269–271: vapor term from Hertz–Knudsen with α = 1 ("Kingery and Berg (1955) assumed that sublimation occurs with perfect efficiency and thus set (α = 1)"); "assumes that sintering occurs in a vacuum"; "ignores the presence of an atmosphere".
- Fig. 8 (p.256): model matches Hobbs & Mason best; "significantly slower than (b) Kingery (1960) and significantly faster than (c) Thomas et al. (1994)".
- **Grain-pair experiment (Sect. 4.1, p.257–258):** "Peltier‐cooled Instec TS102V/G cryostage at −5 and −20 °C. The vacuum ports of the stage were capped in order to seal the chamber ... The clearance between the slide and cover is ~1 mm. Though this area does not seal off from the rest of the chamber, both the cap and the plate are thermoelectrically cooled"; slide coated with ice to saturate; grains sprayed into LN2, 212 μm sieve. Fig. 11 caption: −20 °C "first observation (left) and after 57 min (right), showing growth of the neck ... from 33 to 65 μm. ... starting radii of 101 and 73 μm, shrinking by 3% and 4%". −5 °C: radii 99 and 78 μm, neck 32→44 μm, 9% shrinkage. "we anchored the first measurement to the time at which the model shows the same neck size"; "The grains at −20 °C show a good match to the model ... The grains at −5 °C show a slower sintering rate than predicted". Fig. 12 caption: "−20 °C over a period of 100 min"; "relative neck sizes ... equal to the shared neck size between grains divided by each of their diameters" (i.e. neck WIDTH / grain diameter).
- **Table B1 (p.273):** −20 °C neck sizes at t = 0, 2, 5, 9, 18, 29, 40, 57, 78 min: 32.81, 33.60, 37.16, 44.68, 46.84, 54.18, 58.00, 60.54, 64.78 μm (nine points); −5 °C at 0, 26, 48 min: 32.51, 41.57, 44.00 μm. → The text's "33 to 65 μm after 57 min" (Fig. 11) is inconsistent with its own Table B1 (60.5 μm at 57 min; 64.8 μm at 78 min). The 78-min series is Table B1.
- App. B5: neck uncertainty = min/max of five measurements; grain radius ±1 μm; −5 °C images fogged, "very high humidity that day".
- Bulk experiment (Sect. 4.2): Warm (−20 °C) mean radius 7→38 μm in 2 weeks, "no change in density"; recrystallization after 6 weeks.
- p.264 (Sect. 5.2): "As neck growth occurs, the surface area of solid‐to‐solid contact between ice grains will increase, causing an increase in the thermal conductivity of the aggregate ... the thermal behavior of icy surfaces is not only a function of density and grain size ... but also of age"; models of conductivity vs neck size exist (Ferrari & Lucas 2016; Sirono & Yamamoto 1997; Steiner & Kömle 1991) "but incorporation of this into SA81 is beyond the scope".
- p.265–266 (Sect. 5.3): Enceladus "fine (0.25–2.5‐μm radius) grained plume fallout and E‐ring material is being deposited (Kempf et al., 2010) where particles tens to hundreds of meters [sic] are observed (Jaumann et al., 2008)".
- p.266 (Sect. 5.4): "Enceladus has a maximum equatorial surface temperature of ~80 K ... For a 0.1‐μm grain deposited by plume fallout, the sintering timescale at this temperature is ~10^3 years"; 2% NaCl → ~10^2 yr.
- Conclusions p.268: neck growth agrees with data "within approximately 1 order of magnitude"; densification much slower → "cohesive, but porous, sintered crusts".

- Outline L340 (sintering changes thermal conductivity): **supported** by Molaro p.264 — cite Molaro here; it is the closest prior statement of this paper's motivation in a planetary context.
- Outline L1331–1333 (Enceladus implications): Molaro p.266 "maximum equatorial surface temperature ~80 K" and fine plume grains 0.25–2.5 μm radius (via Kempf) usable.
