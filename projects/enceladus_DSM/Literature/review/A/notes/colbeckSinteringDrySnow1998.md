# colbeckSinteringDrySnow1998 — Colbeck 1998, J. Appl. Phys. 84:4585–4589 (doi 10.1063/1.368684)

PDF p.1 = AIP cover; journal p.4585 = PDF p.2.

**Summary.** Theory paper with a few micrographs. Argues bonds in snow are grain-boundary grooves (equilibrium dihedral angle ~145°), not concave necks, and models bond growth by grain-boundary diffusion out of the boundary under a stress gradient (extending Zhang & Schneibel 1995 to 3D with a growing dihedral angle). Predicts sin(A/2) = Y/R ∝ t^0.25 and t ∝ R^4. Isothermal, two spheres; no temperature-gradient model.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- p.2 (4585) "all of the earlier work on the theory of sintering in snow was based on an incorrect assumption about the geometry".
- Neck growth exponent p.4–5 (4587–4588): "sin(A/2), or Y/R, increases as t^0.25 during the initial phase ... the highest value of the exponents found by Kuroiwa"; conclusions "The growth rate is predicted to vary as the one-fourth power of time, a dependence which is a little stronger than observed."
- Vapor route p.5 (4588): "Hobbs and Mason believed that sublimation, transfer through the vapor phase, had to be the dominant mechanism. The vapor transfer mechanism, which depends on the assumption of the reverse geometry of the neck, has since received wide acceptance and was supported by the strength tests of Ramseier and Keeler."
- Temperature gradients p.5: "Keeler found a higher rate of bond growth in natural snow than expected from laboratory experiments. This is almost certainly due to the macroscopic temperature gradients".
- **No content on depth hoar, weak layers or avalanche initiation** (whole paper read).
