# kempfHowEnceladusDust2010 — Kempf, Beckmann & Schmidt 2010, Icarus 206:446–457 (doi 10.1016/j.icarus.2009.09.016)

Deposition numbers verified on the rendered page 10 (journal p.455).

**Summary.** Dynamical simulations (Saturn + Enceladus gravity, Lorentz force, self-consistent grain charging) of plume grains (5 nm–5 μm; jets from Spitale & Porco 2007) leaving the south polar terrain; explains E-ring vertical profile; computes where grains fall back and the surface deposition rate.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- Deposition rate p.10 (455): "The deposition rate ranges between 0.5 mm per year in the immediate vicinity of the jet sources in the tiger stripe region and about 10^−5 mm per year at the equator ... the growth rate of the regolith layer can be as high as 0.5 mm per year." (Fig. 8b colour scale 10^-5 to 1 mm/yr.)
- Grain size mismatch and possible sintering p.10: "the largest jet particles (Hedman et al., 2009) of about 5 μm are much smaller than the 75 μm grains inferred by Jaumann et al. (2008) ... there may be other physical processes, taking place on the surface, that determine the grain sizes in the south polar terrain. This could be re-crystallisation and compactification of small grains, possibly related to the local temperature of the ice (annealing), which might explain the decrease of grain sizes with distance from the tiger stripes."
- Boulder burial: 10 m boulder at 100 m from a source covered in 10^5–10^6 yr (abstract, p.10).
- Plume dust production ~5 kg s^-1 (Schmidt et al. 2008) (p.8); grain composition summary (Postberg 2008, 2009): types I–III, type III salt-rich (p.2).

**Claims it supports / contradicts / qualifies**
- Outline L337–339 / Choukroun p.7 ("heaviest fallout, 0.01–1 mm yr^-1 (Kempf et al., 2010; Southworth et al., 2019)"): Kempf's own maximum is **0.5 mm yr^-1** near the jet sources; the 0.01–1 range is Choukroun's summary of Kempf + Southworth. Outline already says "cited as Choukroun et al. cite them" — fine, but if Kempf is cited directly the number to quote is up to 0.5 mm yr^-1 (Southworth to be checked for the upper value).
- **Strong candidate for Discussion 5.6**: Kempf p.10 explicitly proposes that surface grains near the tiger stripes grow by "re-crystallisation and compactification of small grains, possibly related to the local temperature of the ice (annealing)", to explain the 5 μm vs 75 μm mismatch and the size decrease away from the stripes. This is a closely matching published suggestion that thermally driven grain coarsening/sintering operates in the warm south-polar terrain; it pairs with Jaumann 2008. Use as motivation only (no timescale).
- Outline Intro P3a: "The plume's grains are micron-sized" — Kempf: largest jet particles ~5 μm (Hedman 2009) — supports.
