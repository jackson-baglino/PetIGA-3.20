# calonneThermalConductivitySnow2019 — Calonne, Milliancourt, Burr, Philip, Martin, Flin & Geindreau 2019, GRL 46:13079–13089 (doi 10.1029/2019GL085228)

PDF p.1 = HAL cover; journal page 13,079 = PDF p.2.

**Summary.** k_eff tensors computed (GeoDict homogenization, Calonne 2011 method) on 64 μCT images: 37 seasonal snow + 27 Antarctic firn/porous ice (Dome C, Lock In, Vostok; 23–133 m), 100–888 kg m^-3, at −3, −20, −60 °C (k_i 2.107/2.330/2.900; k_a 0.024/0.023/0.019). Firn/porous ice: linear in density, ~isotropic, T-sensitive; snow: quadratic, anisotropic. New combined density–temperature formula (Eq. 5).

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- Motivation p.2 abstract: "Estimating thermal conductivity of snow, firn, and porous ice is key for modeling the thermal regime of alpine and polar glaciers." p.2 Intro: "How temperature fluctuations are propagated through snow, firn, and porous ice is therefore crucial to understand".
- **Bonds as heat paths** p.5 (13,082): "At low densities, ice grains are insulated by the predominant surrounding pore space. The few bonds between ice grains play then a critical role in the overall heat conduction as they act as heat paths. Hence, during a change in density, the portion of ice added or removed elsewhere than at ice bonds has little impact on the overall heat conduction." Quadratic → linear transition ~450 kg m^-3.
- Firn regression Eq. 1: k_firn = 2.107 + 0.003618(ρ − ρ_i), R² 0.99 (p.5).
- Anisotropy p.6–7: "k ranges from 0.66 for a rounded grains sample with a lc of 0.67, to 1.57 for a depth hoar sample with a lc of 1.34"; vanishes for firn: "differences ... tend to vanish above 600 kg/m3" (p.6).
- Temperature p.5: conductivity increases with decreasing T following k_ice; firn >600 kg m^-3 +37% from −3 to −60 °C and +10% to −20 °C; "about 20% and 5% at 300 kg/m3"; negligible at low density; lightest sample 4% larger at −3 than −60 °C.
- Self-consistent ellipsoid model reproduces anisotropy ratios "remarkably well" (p.9).
- Snow formulas underestimate firn by up to −30% (p.7–8, p.10).
- Off-diagonal negligible; k_x ≈ k_y (p.4).

**Claims it supports / contradicts / qualifies**
- Outline L294–300 (proposed Intro sentence: "The effective thermal conductivity of snow, firn and porous ice sets how fast heat crosses them and so the temperature profile within them (Calonne et al., 2011, 2019)"): **supported** by Calonne 2019 p.2 (thermal regime of glaciers; propagation of temperature fluctuations). Calonne 2011 supports only "heat transfer through the snowpack ... climatic and hydrological role" — fine.
- Outline L551–553 (Calonne 2019 Figs. 1c, 2b: k_z/k_xy tracks correlation-length ratio, 0.66 at 0.67, 1.57 at 1.34; vanishes in firn above ~600 kg m^-3): **supported**.
- Outline L1288 (contrast: conductivity against density): supported (quadratic/linear regressions).
- Outline L1353–1357 (Limitations: constant k_ice, k_air; "Calonne et al. (2019) on temperature-dependent conductivities"): **supported** — over −40 to −5 °C our constant k_ice = 2.29 omits a k_ice change of roughly 10–15% (2.107 at −3 °C, 2.330 at −20 °C per Paterson values used here, p.4), which would shift absolute k_eff but not the normalized curves; at φ ≈ 0.3–0.5 the bulk effect is a few percent (≈5% at 300 kg m^-3 for −3 → −20 °C, p.5).

**Candidate citations / claims**
- **Strong candidate for Discussion 5.3 / Intro**: Calonne 2019 p.5 mechanism — at low density the few bonds between grains carry the heat, so ice added at bonds matters and ice elsewhere does not. This is the closest published statement of why neck growth (sintering) raises k at constant porosity, and why SSA loss (from necks) correlates with k. Cite as supporting the mechanism, not the magnitude.
- Note Calonne 2019 k_ice values (2.107 at −3 °C, 2.330 at −20 °C) vs. manuscript Table 1 k_ice = 2.29 (constant): state the value's temperature and source in Table 1.
