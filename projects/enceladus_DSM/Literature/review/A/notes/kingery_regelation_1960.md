# kingery_regelation_1960 — Kingery 1960, J. Appl. Phys. 31:833–838 (doi 10.1063/1.1735704 on PDF p.1)

PDF p.1 = AIP cover; p.2 begins with the end of the previous article. Table I verified on the rendered page 3 (journal p.834). Not in the draft .bib (Zotero only); named in outline prose.

**Summary.** Neck growth and shrinkage between ice spheres (0.1–3 mm radius, sprayed into liquid oxygen) on a glass slide in a constant-temperature room, −2.2 to −25.1 °C (most at −17.8 °C); not in a saturated enclosure. Concludes surface diffusion dominates (time exponent ~1/7, t ∝ r^4, no shrinkage); activation energy 27.5 kcal/mol.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- **Table I (p.3, rendered)**: viscous flow (x/r)² = A t/r; evaporation–condensation (x/r)³ = B t/r²; volume diffusion (x/r)⁵ = C t/r³; surface diffusion (x/r)⁷ = D t/r⁴; shrinkage none for evaporation–condensation and surface diffusion.
- Evaporation–condensation rate p.6 (837), Eq. 6: "derived from the Langmuir equation assuming that vaporization and condensation take place in vacuo; under a positive pressure, the rate of vaporization is decreased as a result of diffusion through a boundary layer. Consequently, Eq. (6) gives a result an order of magnitude larger than that expected under atmospheric pressure." (Eq. 6 from Kingery & Berg 1955.)
- Measured: "(x/r) ∼ t^{1/6.9}" at −17.8 °C (p.5); Table II mean inverse slopes 7.1 (−2.2 °C), 6.2 (−9.5), 6.9 (−17.8), 6.1 (−25.1); time to x/r = 0.1 ∝ r^4; activation energy 27.5 kcal/mol (≈115 kJ/mol); D_s implausibly large (p.7).
- Range of exponents: "the time dependence of neck growth varies, depending on the process taking place, from the one-half to the one-seventh power of time" (p.4).

**Claims it supports / contradicts / qualifies**
- Outline L395–404 (classical exponents: "1/3 for evaporation–condensation, smaller exponents (1/5, 1/7) for volume and surface diffusion (Kuczynski 1949; Kingery & Berg 1955; for ice, Hobbs & Mason 1964)"): **supported by Kingery 1960 Table I** (local, checkable). Kingery adds the key qualification: the 1/3 law is for evaporation–condensation limited by molecular exchange (Langmuir/Hertz–Knudsen, "in vacuo"); under atmospheric pressure diffusion through the gas slows it. This supports the outline's \tC that t^{1/3} describes attachment-limited sublimation/desublimation. Cite Kingery 1960 (in Zotero; add to .bib) instead of the missing Kingery & Berg 1955.
- Demmenie 2025 attribute Kingery's ~1/7 to underestimated humidity (unsaturated, open slide) — consistent with Blackford's remark that his spheres were not in an ice-saturated enclosure. Relevant to the Molaro unsaturated comparison (exponent 0.135 ≈ 1/7).
- Activation energy 27.5 kcal/mol (115 kJ/mol) — differs from Choukroun 24.3 kJ/mol and vapor ~51 kJ/mol; not needed.
