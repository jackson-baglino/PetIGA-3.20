**Summary.** 1D steady-state conductive thermal models of Enceladus' ice shell (5–35 km; T_surf 60 K, test 85 K; k_ice = 651/T) with a porous surface layer of constant conductivity 0.001–0.1 W m^-1 K^-1 and thickness 0–700 m; two-way radar attenuation (low-loss pure ice, high-loss 300 μM Cl) with a 100 dB limit; global maps with two layer-thickness distributions (layer k = 0.025).

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- Porous layer treated "specifically as a thermal insulator" p.2: "we consider the effects of a porous ice layer specifically as a thermal insulator. This porous ice layer may considerably affect the thermal state of the ice shell due to its low thermal conductivity."
- Conductivity range p.2–3: "measured for porous icy layers in laboratory experiments (Seiferlin et al., 1996) and derived from thermophysical modeling (Ferrari et al., 2021), yielding values between 0.025 and 0.001 W m^−1 K^−1. Therefore ... we vary the thermal conductivity of this porous surface layer between 0.001 and 0.1 W m^−1 K^−1." Constant within the layer (p.3).
- 2%: abstract p.1 "In regions covered by thick and strongly insulating porous surface layers, however, as little as 2% of the ice shell can be penetrated."
- p.9 (Discussion) "Another important factor that will affect the thermal state of the ice shell and hence the two-way attenuation is the thermal conductivity of the porous surface layer."
- p.9 Conclusions "a porous surface layer built up by the deposition of material from Enceladus's south pole plume or generated by impacts has a first order effect on the two-way radar attenuation."
- Shallow brines: abstract "a thick porous surface layer leads to high subsurface temperatures promoting the formation of brines at shallow depth that can be detected".
- Stability: p.2 "such a porous surface layer could be stable on most of Enceladus' surface due to the low temperatures (Gundlach et al., 2018)"; thickness up to 700 m from pit chains (Martin et al. 2023).
- Tiger-stripe surface: p.8 "surface temperature reaches about 85 K on average at the south polar region, with values higher than 145 K in localized areas close to the tiger stripes."
- No mention of sintering anywhere (whole paper read). High porosity → low conductivity via Jabaud et al. 2024 (p.9).
