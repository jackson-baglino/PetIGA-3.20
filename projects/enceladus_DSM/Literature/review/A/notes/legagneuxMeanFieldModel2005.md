# legagneuxMeanFieldModel2005 — Legagneux & Domine 2005, JGR 110, F04011 (doi 10.1029/2004JF000181)

Minus signs dropped in the text layer ("2°C" = −2 °C, Flin 2004 data; "233 K to 271 K" unaffected).

**Summary.** Mean-field model of isothermal snow metamorphism as transient Ostwald ripening: snow as a distribution of spheres (1 μm–1 cm) each in a spherical air cavity sized to the snow density; growth law combining Hertz–Knudsen surface kinetics (α) and steady vapor diffusion (Eq. 10), mass conservation sets the cavity-wall vapor pressure; isothermal, infinite thermal conductivity, latent heat neglected; necks/concavities neglected. Compared with Flin et al. (2004) tomography at −2 °C; parameter study of T (233–271 K), density, α (10^-5–1).

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- SSA law p.2–3: empirical SSA = B − A ln(t + Δt) (Legagneux et al. 2004); steady-state LSW gives SSA = SSA0 (τ/(t+τ))^{1/n} (Eq. 2), n = 3 diffusion-limited, n = 2 surface-limited (Wagner).
- DRC evolution reproduced with α = 1 (p.6): "the somewhat arbitrary choice of the condensation coefficient a = 1, yields correct values ... the surface processes are not rate limiting"; conclusions p.10: "diffusion is rate limiting".
- Transient regime lasts >10^6 days for Flin's snow (p.7) — steady-state ripening not reached.
- **Temperature = shift of time axis** p.9–10 (Sect. 3.5.1): "The effect of temperature T is visible in log-log coordinates as a translation of the curve along the time axis (Figure 10). This means that the value of n is constant and equal to 3 and that τ decreases with T. The dependence of τ with T ... is exponential ... since Pav depends on Psat, and since Psat has an exponential T dependence".
- α p.10: "No effect is detectable for values of a between 10^−2 and 1 ... n finally stabilizes at 2 for a values below 10^−4 ... For intermediate values of a, diffusion and surface kinetics both contribute"; "the impact of surface processes should not be detected in the snowpack unless the condensation coefficient is lower than 0.01". Fig. 12 caption: slope 1/3 for high α and 1/2 for low α.
- Density effect weaker, power law (p.10).
- Necks cannot be represented in the model (p.4).

**Claims it supports / contradicts / qualifies**
- **Section 5.2 (Temperature as a rescaling of time) — strong prior result to cite**: Legagneux & Domine's isothermal model already predicts that temperature only translates the SSA(t) curve along the (log) time axis, through P_sat(T) (p.9–10). This is a closely matching published statement of the manuscript's result 1 (for SSA; ours extends it to conductivity on sintering packings with necks). The manuscript should cite it, both as support and to frame novelty honestly (ours: explicit microstructure with necks and vapor diffusion; conductivity, not only SSA; quantified 0.3% collapse).
- Section 5.2 Step 2/3 (Λ and diffusivity as weak correction): in Legagneux the shift is "exponential" via P_sat; they do not separate D_v's temperature dependence. Consistent.
- Outline L1050 / L1320 (SSA linear in log time, functional form): supported (p.2, Eq. 13).
- Outline Section 2.2 regime (α = 10^-3 with 50 μm grains → mixed regime, Λ ≈ 2.8): consistent with Legagneux's intermediate regime "between 10^−2 and 10^−4" where both resistances matter.
- Note a disagreement in the literature: Legagneux find isothermal snow diffusion-limited (α ≈ 1 works), Bouvet 2022 fit α ≈ 10^-3 with a kinetics-limited model, Fourteau 2021 cite Krol & Löwe for slow kinetics in isothermal snow. Worth one sentence in the α_c paragraph ("the regime of isothermal snow metamorphism is itself debated").
