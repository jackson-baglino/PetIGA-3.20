# fukuzawaDepthhoarCrystalGrowth1993 — Fukuzawa & Akitaya 1993, Ann. Glaciol. 18:39–45 (doi 10.3189/S026030550001123X)

**Summary.** Field observations (Hokkaido, winter 1989–90) of depth hoar forming within one or two nights just below the snow surface under >100 K m^-1 gradients, and lab experiments (low-density 80–100 kg m^-3 snow at about −16 °C, 100–300 K m^-1, ~50 h): mean crystal size grows linearly in time, rate 0.98–2.56×10^-9 m s^-1 increasing with gradient.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- p.1 Intro: "A layer with relatively low strength plays an important role in a slab-avalanche release. The kinetic growth metamorphism produces a depth-hoar layer, which is typically weak because of thin bonds between particles."
- Conclusion p.7: "Avalanche danger will be high if the metamorphosed layer is covered by heavy snow in a later storm."
- Field: new snow → depth hoar in one or two nights (p.4).

**Claims it supports / contradicts / qualifies**
- Draft L256 "Depth hoar formation under strong temperature gradients creates a mechanically weak layer within snowpacks and drives slab avalanche initiation (Colbeck 1998; Fukuzawa 1993; Schweizer 2003)": **supported** by Fukuzawa for the weak-layer part (p.1) and for depth hoar under strong gradients; "drives slab avalanche initiation" is stated as a role in slab release (p.1) — fine with Schweizer 2003.
