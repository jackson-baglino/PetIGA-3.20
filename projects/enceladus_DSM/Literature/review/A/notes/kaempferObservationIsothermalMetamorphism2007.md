# kaempferObservationIsothermalMetamorphism2007 — Kaempfer & Schneebeli 2007, JGR 112, D24101 (doi 10.1029/2007JD009047)

Text layer drops minus signs and μ (CHECK_COLLECTION, confirmed there on p.1): temperatures are −1.6, −8.3, −19.1 and −54 °C; pixel 10 μm.

**Summary.** Four sealed samples of the same sieved fresh snow (18.5 mm × 65 mm cylinders) held isothermally at −1.6, −8.3, −19.1, −54 °C for ~11 months; μCT (10 μm) monthly; density, SSA, structure model index, trabecular thickness/number.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- Kuczynski exponents p.2: "n = 7 and m = 4 would indicate surface diffusion, n = 5 and m = 3 volume diffusion, and n = 3 and m = 2 ... evaporation-condensation"; "The vapor transfer mechanism has since received wide acceptance" (citing Hobbs & Mason as 1963).
- Isothermal in nature p.1: "Conditions for long-term isothermal metamorphism occur in nature only in the center of the polar ice shields".
- **Densification without load** p.8: "We observe a significant and continuous densification for all the three samples ... stresses due to the weight are very low ... There has thus to occur a mechanism during the isothermal sintering which leads to densification ... this has to be a volume process"; abstract "The continuous densification implies that volume processes like grain boundary diffusion have to occur."
- SSA p.6–7: fit SSA = SSA0 (τ/(t+τ))^{1/n}, n = 3.00, 3.55, 2.20 (R² > 0.99) for the three warmer samples; described as "logarithmic decrease"; at −54 °C SSA fell only 19% in a year.
- Two stages: rapid shape change in the first month, then constant SMI/trabecular number (p.7–8).
- Thermal conductivity "strongly dependent on the microstructure" (p.1); bond-sensitive properties (p.1): "Other properties are most sensitive to the bonds between grains".
- Mass loss by sublimation 0.4–11.1% (p.5).

**Claims it supports / contradicts / qualifies**
- Outline L1320 (cite for isothermal SSA decay in the lab, forms only): supported — but note their fitted form is a power law in (t+τ) that they call logarithmic; their samples also densified, so "forms only" is right.
- **Outline "Scope" (vapor-transport sintering does not densify; porosity constant) — qualify**: unloaded isothermal snow did densify in this experiment (p.8), attributed to volume (grain-boundary) diffusion. The model excludes this; the manuscript should say real isothermal samples can densify slowly without load through processes the model omits (Kaempfer & Schneebeli 2007), so constant porosity is a property of the model's single mechanism, not a general property of isothermal sintering.
- Outline classical exponents (Intro P4): Kaempfer p.2 gives the same n = 3, 5, 7 table — another local, checkable citation (alongside Blackford 2007, Demmenie 2025).
- Outline Section 5.4 ("SSA falls linearly in log time"): Kaempfer's fits support a slow, log-like decay; quote as functional form only.

**Candidate citations / claims**
- Limitations: "isothermal snow densifies slowly even without load (Kaempfer & Schneebeli, 2007), through volume processes absent from the model."
- Intro P4 classical exponents: cite Kaempfer & Schneebeli 2007 p.2 (or Blackford 2007) since Kuczynski/Kingery & Berg/Hobbs & Mason are not in the library.
