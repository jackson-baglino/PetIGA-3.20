# matzlMeasuringSpecificSurface2006 — Matzl & Schneebeli 2006, J. Glaciol. 52(179):558–564 (doi 10.3189/172756506781828412)

Whole paper read (pp. 558–564).

**Summary.** Calibrated near-infrared (840–940 nm) digital photography of snow-pit walls; reflectance correlated with SSA measured by stereology on phthalate-cast surface sections (29 samples). SSA = A exp(r/t), R² = 90.8%; uncertainty 4% (SSA 5 mm^-1) to ~15% (SSA ~25 mm^-1); ~1 mm spatial resolution over 0.5–1 m².

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- p.558: "SSA (mm–1) is defined as the ratio between surface area and the volume of the ice phase." (per ice volume, same as the model's SSA if normalised by ice area/volume).
- p.558: "Calibrated NIR photography allows quantitative determination of SSA and its spatial variation in a snow profile in two dimensions within an uncertainty of 15%."
- p.561: stereology "underestimation ... for SSA values higher than approximately 55 mm–1".
- p.562: "Because of the widespread use of SSA to parameterize air permeability and thermal conductivity of sintered materials (German, 1996), it can be expected that this should also be possible for snow." — direct, if speculative, support for the idea that SSA can parameterize thermal conductivity.
- p.558: "The SSA of snow changes during isothermal and temperature-gradient metamorphism (Schneebeli and Sokratov, 2004; Legagneux and Domine, 2005)".

**Claims it supports / contradicts / qualifies**
- Outline L1318–1320 ("SSA is measured by near-infrared reflectance, gas adsorption and tomography (Gallet, Matzl, Proksch)"): **supported** for NIR reflectance (photography of pit walls). Caveat: method is calibrated on natural snowpack layers at field scale; for small laboratory ice-sphere samples the contact-probe NIR (Gallet 2009) or tomography are more appropriate; Matzl is fine as a general citation.
- Sec 5 claim "SSA is a strong predictor of k_eff": Matzl p.562 offers a prior suggestion (via German 1996 for sintered materials) that SSA can parameterize thermal conductivity — candidate supporting/priority citation, but it is a speculation, not a result; cite only as "suggested".

**Candidate citations / claims**
- Sec 5.3 (lab tests): "SSA of snow layers can be mapped by near-infrared photography with ~15% uncertainty (Matzl & Schneebeli, 2006)".
- Discussion (SSA–conductivity link): "Matzl & Schneebeli (2006) suggested that SSA, used for sintered materials to parameterize permeability and conductivity, could do the same for snow" — closely matches the manuscript's claim; optional.
