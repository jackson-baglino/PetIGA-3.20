# schneebeliTomographyTemperatureGradient2004 — Schneebeli & Sokratov 2004, Hydrol. Process. 18:3655–3665 (doi 10.1002/hyp.5800)
Read in full.
- Snow breeder, micro-CT (36 μm) + heat-flux EHC, TG 25–100 K/m, density 150–500 kg/m3; "density in the observed volume remained constant" (abstract).
- Abstract: "heat conductivity changed by as much as twice its initial value, caused by changes in structure and texture, but not due to changes in density."
- p.3664: "EHC increased by about 60% from the initial value at a constant snow density ... parameterization of EHC by snow density alone (Sturm 1997) is insufficient"; LDS shows an early minimum in EHC (recrystallization), then two-stage rise; HDS reaches a limiting value.
- p.3663: "SSA decreases only for LDS, not for HDS"; anisotropy switches horizontal→vertical; thicker vertical ice structures raise EHC.
- p.3664: Lehning et al. (2002) used an EHC correction based on simulated bond/neck growth (Part II).
- SSA defined per ice volume "the parameter required for thermophysical modelling".
Claims:
- Outline L510–513 (gradient studies followed conductivity at constant density; isothermal fixed-porosity case not quantified): SUPPORTED.
- Outline L554–558 ("under a gradient, losing surface area did not raise conductivity ... redistribution between directions"): CONTRADICTED/partial — EHC rose ~60% (up to 2x) at constant density; in LDS SSA fell while EHC first dipped then rose; in HDS EHC rose with no SSA loss. Correct: "conductivity rose by up to a factor 2 at constant density, mostly through vertical structures, and was not tied to SSA".
- Supports Sec 5 "density is not enough" (p.3664 quote).
