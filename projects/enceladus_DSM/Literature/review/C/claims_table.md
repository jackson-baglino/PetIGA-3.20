## Suggested additions

1. **Bouvet et al. (2022)** (`bouvetSnowEquiTemperatureMetamorphism2022`, in .bib) — closest prior work: keff computed by homogenization on phase-field-simulated isothermal metamorphism of µCT snow at nearly constant density (Sect. 3.2.2: 0.078 → 0.081 and 0.37 → 0.42 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹). Must appear in the gap paragraph (Intro para 7) and Section 5 so the novelty is stated against it (packings sintering from contacts, k–SSA relation, temperature and porosity).
2. **Gundlach et al. (2018)** (`gundlachSinteringSublimationMicrometresized2018`, in .bib) — for draft L259: "the low temperature of ∼80 K prevents any sintering … Enceladus possesses a powder-like surface consisting of unsintered micrometre-sized particles" (Sect. 5); and sintering raises network conductivity ×4–8, λ ∝ r_neck^{1/2} (Sect. 4.3, vacuum).
3. **Maeno & Ebinuma (1983)** and **Kaempfer & Schneebeli (2007)** (both in .bib) — in-library sources for the classical exponents (q = 3/5/7) and for Hobbs & Mason's own q = 4.2–6.2 in air (maeno1983 Table I p.2; kaempfer2007 p.2). Replace the missing-PDF citations (Kuczynski, Kingery & Berg, Hobbs & Mason) or accompany them.
4. **Molaro et al. (2019) p.23/265** — they predict that neck growth raises aggregate conductivity so thermal inertia depends on age; directly matching motivation for Intro para 3b.
5. **Mergny et al. (2024)** (`mergnyLunaIcyExploringEuropas2024`) — coupled vapor-sintering/heat model with bond-dependent conductivity for icy moons (prior art for "sintering changes conductivity"); also an in-library description of Hobbs & Mason's conditions (1 atm air, −3 to −20 °C, 50–700 µm; fitted α = 0.03, ~17× lower due to air).
