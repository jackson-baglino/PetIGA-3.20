# byrneRadarAttenuationSubsurface2024 — Byrne et al., JGR Planets 129, e2024JE008615 (2024)
DOI (PDF p.1): 10.1029/2024JE008615. Read in full (11 pp).

## Summary
1D steady conductive ice-shell models of Enceladus with a porous insulating surface layer (0–700 m, k = 0.001–0.1 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹), surface 60 K (85 K test); two-way radar attenuation (low/high loss), penetration maps.

## Findings
- Porous layer from plume fall-back (Kempf 2010), "could be stable on most of Enceladus' surface due to the low temperatures (Gundlach et al., 2018)"; up to 700 m locally (Martin 2023) (p.2).
- "we consider the effects of a porous ice layer specifically as a thermal insulator"; conductivities "measured ... (Seiferlin et al., 1996) and derived from thermophysical modeling (Ferrari et al., 2021), yielding values between 0.025 and 0.001"; varied 0.001–0.1, constant within layer (p.2–3).
- Thick insulating layer: "as little as 2% of the ice shell can be penetrated" (abstract); brines likely (p.1, p.8).
- "has a first order effect on the two-way radar attenuation" (conclusions); conductivity "another important factor that will affect the thermal state of the ice shell" (p.8).
- Surface 85 K average in south polar region, ">145 K in localized areas close to the tiger stripes" (p.8). No discussion of sintering.
