# chenStructuralEvolutionIcesphere2010 — Chen & Baker, Hydrol. Process. 24, 2034–2040 (2010)
DOI (PDF p.1): 10.1002/hyp.7787. Read in full (7 pp). Temperatures: −10 °C cold room (minus dropped, per CHECK_COLLECTION).

## Summary
Single-layer (2D) arrays of ~4 mm ice spheres (water drops frozen in LN2), quasi-isothermal at −10 °C for 18 days and under ~500 K m⁻¹ for 8 h; optical microscopy + SEM/EBSD; steady conduction model of a two-sphere structure.

## Findings
- Sintering history: Kuczynski power law; Kingery surface diffusion; Kuroiwa volume diffusion; "Hobbs and Mason (1964) proposed sublimation–condensation and water vapour transport as the dominant mechanisms, a proposal that has since received wide acceptance (Blackford, 2007...)" (p.1–2).
- Isothermal necks grew by "growth of ice crystals in the pore spaces" and protrusions bridging spheres — porous necks, not smooth concave necks (Figs 3–5, p.3–4); some protrusions retreated; solid necks showed no significant evolution (p.4).
- Narrow necks concentrate the temperature gradient locally (Fig. 10, p.5).
- Spheres polycrystalline (grains 50–800 µm) with trapped air bubbles (p.2).

## Claims
- Draft L262 (sintering studied extensively; cites Chen 2010): supported — an ice-sphere sintering study.
- Qualifies model geometry: mm-sphere necks form by vapor-grown crystals/protrusions (Molaro also notes this, p.16–17/258–259); the phase-field model's smooth isotropic necks do not represent faceted protrusion growth. Limitation-level.
