# legagneuxMeanFieldModel2005 — Legagneux & Domine, JGR 110, F04011 (2005)
DOI (PDF p.1): 10.1029/2004JF000181. Read in full (12 pp). Section 3.5 and Figs. 10–12 checked on rendered PDF p.10. Minus signs lost in text: the Flin et al. (2004) experiment is at −2 °C; temperature range simulated 233–271 K; α from 10⁻⁵ to 1.

## Summary
Mean-field model of isothermal snow metamorphism as transient Ostwald ripening: snow as disconnected spheres (each in a spherical air cavity set by snow density), vapor transport through air (Fick, steady Laplace) in series with Hertz–Knudsen surface kinetics (condensation coefficient α, default 1), Kelvin effect. Compares simulated distribution of radii of curvature (DRC) with Flin et al. (2004) tomography; SSA via sphere geometry; sensitivity to T, density, α.

## Findings relevant to the manuscript
- Temperature acts as a time shift in a vapor-only model: "The effect of temperature T is visible in log-log coordinates as a translation of the curve along the time axis (Figure 10). This means that the value of n is constant and equal to 3 and that τ decreases with T ... since Pav depends on Psat, and since Psat has an exponential T dependence, we expect the growth rate to depend exponentially on T" (p.9–10). Closely matching published precedent for the paper's Section 5.2 result (temperature rescales time, through the saturation vapor pressure), in a different (sphere, Ostwald) model.
- Condensation coefficient: "No effect is detectable for values of α between 10⁻² and 1 ... This slope further changes with decreasing values of α between 10⁻² and 10⁻⁴. The parameter n finally stabilizes at 2 for α values below 10⁻⁴ while τ increases" (p.10). "The impact of surface processes should not be detected in the snowpack unless the condensation coefficient is lower than 0.01. Even for values of α between 10⁻² and 10⁻⁴, the effect of diffusion can be felt" (p.10). → α = 10⁻³ lies in their mixed regime where both attachment and diffusion matter — consistent with the outline's Λ ≈ 2.8 and with "a smaller α_c only rescales time; a larger one brings diffusion in" (L1191–1199). Note their exponents (n = 3 diffusion-limited, 2 attachment-limited) are Ostwald coarsening exponents, not neck-growth exponents.
- Their conclusion from data: α = 1 works and "diffusion is rate limiting" in isothermal snow (p.10–11) — i.e., they find large α for rounded natural snow at −2 °C; contrasts with the manuscript's α_c = 10⁻³ in the aggregates (an effective/choice value; the outline already frames α_c as uncertain).
- "the condensation coefficient ... strongly depends on the nature of the ice-air interface and on the growth mechanism. If the interface is rough ... a = 1 ... no general expression of a is presently available" (p.3).
- Necks cannot be represented in their sphere model (p.4–5) — the topological change our phase-field resolves.
- Other mechanisms (surface/volume diffusion) can be neglected for sintering of seasonal snow (p.10–11).
- SSA decay with log time (Eq. 13 from Legagneux et al. 2003) (p.7).

## Claims supported / contradicted / qualified
- Outline L1050 (cite box: Legagneux & Domine 2005 for SSA against log time in isothermal snow, functional form): partially — the log law (Eq. 13) is quoted from Legagneux et al. 2003/2004 experiments; this 2005 paper is a model; its own simulated curves are not log-linear (Figs. 10–12 log-log, transient). For the empirical log law cite Taillandier 2007 or Flin 2004 (verified Eq. 8) rather than Legagneux & Domine 2005.
- Section 5.2 (temperature only rescales time): supported as published precedent (p.9–10, Fig. 10) — cite as "a mean-field vapor-transport model also finds temperature to act as a time shift (Legagneux & Domine, 2005)".
- Section 5.1 (α_c effective, uncertain; α_c = 1e-3 in a mixed regime): supported (p.3, p.10).
- Taillandier 2007 says this model found "diffusion of water vapor in air was the rate-limiting step" — consistent.

## Candidate additions
- Section 5.2: Legagneux & Domine (2005, p.9–10, Fig. 10) — closely matching precedent for temperature as a pure time shift in a vapor-only model.
- Section 5.1/2.2: their Fig. 12 — α between 1e-2 and 1e-4 is the mixed attachment/diffusion regime; supports stating that α_c = 1e-3 is not purely attachment-limited at the aggregate scale (contrast with the saturated pair, where gas diffusion is 0.4–2% of resistance).
