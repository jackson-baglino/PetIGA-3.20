# choukroun_strength_2020 — Choukroun, Molaro, Hodyss, Marteau, et al., GRL 47, e2020GL088953 (2020)
DOI (PDF p.1): 10.1029/2020GL088953. Read in full (10 pp). SI not in library.

## Summary
Laboratory sintering of plume-deposit analogs: crystalline ice of 12 µm mean diameter (air atomization into liquid N2), unpacked aggregates of porosity 51.5 ± 1.6%, 0.66–2.14 kg samples in sealed containers, isothermal at 193, 223, 233, 243 K, "at water vapor saturation pressure", for months to 14 months. Strength by cone penetration resistance. Strength grows linearly in time; Arrhenius rate with Q = 24.3 ± 3.3 kJ/mol; extrapolated to Enceladus and Europa.

## Findings relevant to the manuscript
- Linear strengthening, Q: "A linear regression ... yields an activation energy Q = 24.3 ± 3.3 kJ/mol (95% confidence interval)" (p.3).
- Interpretation: "comparable to ... the ~23 kJ/mol activation energy of H2O self-diffusion on the surface of ice grains (Nasello et al., 2007). It is not consistent with the activation energy associated with ice recrystallization (~50 kJ/mol) nor with the volume (~60 kJ/mol) or vapor (~51 kJ/mol) diffusion mechanisms ... (Molaro et al., 2019). This suggests that the strengthening ... is primarily due to the evolution of a mesoscale network between individual grains, or agglomerates thereof" (p.3–4). Conclusion: "consistent with a process dominated by self-diffusion of H2O molecules on the surface of ice grains" (p.8).
  → They themselves state the vapor-route activation energy is ~51 kJ/mol and that their 24.3 is inconsistent with it. This directly supports the outline's Section 5.2 argument (our clock ≈ 50 kJ/mol, theirs 24.3: points to a second route).
- Temperature ranges: 193–243 K (p.2). Our runs 233–268 K; overlap 233–243 K — outline L1261–1263 correct.
- No densification: "The highest resistance recorded ... was 14 MPa, but it was still increasing linearly at the time and no densification had yet occurred" (p.6).
- Early strengthening off the linear trend "perhaps associated to a different process" (p.3).
- Enceladus extrapolation: "Under typical Enceladus surface conditions, little to no strengthening is expected for plume deposits. In the south polar region, it would take at least the age of the solar system for the deposits to develop a resistance on order of 1 MPa ... Under Enceladus equatorial noontime temperatures, plume deposits would still take about 100 Myr to reach 1 MPa ... plume deposits in these areas are anticipated to remain poorly consolidated" (p.7). South polar mean "colder than 50 K", equatorial noon "over 80 K" (Howett 2010) (p.6).
- Tiger Stripes: "Close to the Tiger Stripes, where temperatures up to 180 K have been estimated (Spencer & Nimmo, 2013), plume deposit materials would develop a cone penetration resistance around 10 MPa in ~15 years ... our laboratory samples with a cone penetration resistance on order of 10 MPa are very consolidated" (p.7).
- Fallout: "regions close to the Tiger Stripes are also areas where the deposition of new plume deposits would be most intense, on order of 0.01 to 1 mm/yr (Kempf et al., 2010; Southworth et al., 2019). There would be competition between strengthening ... and covering by new fresh and loose particles" (p.7). Also p.2: "The deposition rate averages on order of 1 µm/yr across the entire Enceladus surface but can be up to 1 mm/yr in locations close to jet sources." Mean plume particle radius 3.1 ± 0.5 µm (Ingersoll & Ewald 2011); 0.1–5 µm dominate deposits (p.2).
- Plume description (p.1): "Cassini observed over a hundred jets converging into a plume (Porco et al., 2006), which originates from a set of four rectilinear surface fractures dubbed Tiger Stripes (Spitale & Porco, 2007). Enceladus' plume consists of micron-sized particles mostly composed of water ice that feed Saturn's E ring (Kempf et al., 2010). These particles also contain percent-level NaCl (Postberg et al., 2009, 2011) and complex organic materials (Postberg et al., 2018)." The draft's Intro para 3 follows this nearly verbatim (and drops Porco 2006 and Kempf 2010).
- Extrapolation caveats (p.6): (3) "Low H2O vapor pressure and airless conditions at the surface ... would ease release and escape of H2O, which suggests that ice could redistribute less efficiently within plume deposits than in our experiments"; (4) "The effect of temperature on sintering rates strongly dominates over that of grain size (Molaro et al., 2019)".
- Snow as analog: "melting and refreezing processes play an important role in the evolution of snow, making it a relatively poor analog" (p.2). Molaro: "a high bulk porosity could be retained over long timescales" (p.2).

## Claims supported / contradicted / qualified
- Draft L259 "Much of Enceladus' surface experiences conditions that cause little to no sintering resulting in unconsolidated granular packings (Choukroun; Molaro)": partially — Choukroun's quantity is strength, wording "little to no strengthening" and "poorly consolidated", not "unconsolidated" and not "no sintering". Supports outline L347–353.
- Draft L259 "Temperatures near Tiger Stripes may reach up to 180 K (Spencer & Nimmo 2013), warm enough cause in significant sintering ... well-consolidated (Choukroun)": supported by p.7 ("very consolidated" in ~15 yr). Typo "enough cause in".
- Draft L262 "Other modes of diffusion tend to have limited effects or operate over planetary timescales (Molaro 2019; Choukroun 2020)": CONTRADICTED for Choukroun — they attribute strengthening to surface self-diffusion and say vapor's ~51 kJ/mol does not fit. Supports outline L376–381.
- Outline L330–332 (Arrhenius, 24.3 ± 3.3 kJ/mol, linear): supported (p.3).
- Outline L338–339 "heaviest fallout, 0.01–1 mm/yr (Kempf 2010; Southworth 2019)": supported as Choukroun's own statement (p.7). Note Kempf alone gives ≤0.5 mm/yr; cite Choukroun too, or check Southworth for the bounds.
- Outline L1259–1264 (Choukroun attribute 24.3 to surface self-diffusion; vapor ~50): supported, and stronger than the outline says — Choukroun explicitly give ~51 kJ/mol for vapor and reject it.
- Outline "Comparisons" decision (unloaded ice-powder samples as analogue): supported — Choukroun samples are unloaded, sealed, isothermal, vapor-saturated, no densification.
- Draft L262 "The strength evolution of ice plume deposit simulants was studied experimentally by Choukroun ... to better understand the surface structure of the icy shells on Enceladus and Europa": fine; their stated motivation is lander sampling (p.1–2).
