# ferrariThermalEmissionSaturns2021 — Ferrari, Lucas & Jacquemoud, A&A 655, A8 (2021)
DOI (PDF p.1): 10.1051/0004-6361/202141223. Read in full (11 pp; references skimmed).

## Summary
Thermophysical model of the regolith of Saturn's icy moons (porosity, grain size, ice phase, grain-contact type, radiative + contact conductivity after Ferrari & Lucas 2016; Hapke albedo/emissivity) coupled to Dione shape models, compared with one Cassini CIRS FP1 latitudinal scan of Dione's leading hemisphere. Airless surfaces, 50–110 K.

## Findings relevant to the manuscript
- Thermal inertia of Saturn's icy moons "very weak, around 10–20 J m−2 K−1 s−1/2" (p.2); "A constant thermal conductivity kE0 = 10−3 W m−1 K−1 yields similar thermal inertia" (p.6) — this is the 0.001 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹ value Byrne et al. take as their lower end.
- Sintering motivation: "the mechanism of grain sintering and the reason for thermal inertia increases in the icy regolith remain poorly understood (Ferrari & Lucas 2016; Schaible et al. 2017)" (p.2). Thermal-inertia anomalies (lens-shaped, "PacMan") interpreted as higher thermal inertia from MeV-electron alteration (p.1–2).
- Contact conductance: low values attributed to amorphous ice or "rough grains with irregular faces that are likely to reduce the solid conduction through grain contact" (p.2); contact conductivity "scales with 1/R" (p.10).
- Model conductivity = radiative + contact (kE = kC + kR), for vacuum; no gas conduction (p.3).
- Body studied is Dione, not Enceladus; no Enceladus result.
