# demmenieIceSinteringSublimation2025 — Demmenie, Woutersen & Bonn, J. Phys. Chem. Lett. 16, 2104–2109 (2025)
DOI (PDF p.1): 10.1021/acs.jpclett.5c00050. Read in full (6 pp; Table 1 and Eqs. 1–2 checked on the rendered PDF p.2). SI (Figs S1–S3) not in library.

## Summary
Experiments: pairs of ~1 mm-diameter ice spheres (frozen water droplets from 800 µm needles), horizontal, no external load, at −3 °C, in a box whose humidity was set to ice saturation from Murphy & Koop (2005). Neck growth imaged from above for ~2.5 h; four runs fitted to r = (t − t0)^α. Plus healing of a ~4 µm-deep, ~280 µm-long scratch analysed with Mullins' linear theory (n = 1–4).

## Findings relevant to the manuscript
- Classical exponents, Table 1 + Eq. 2 (p.2/2105): r ∝ t^α with α = 1/(2n−1)... in their table m = 1 (viscous, they correct the literature's 2), 3 (sublimation–condensation), 5 (bulk diffusion), 7 (surface diffusion). I.e. α = 1, 1/3, 1/5, 1/7.
- Sublimation–condensation prefactor C2 = 3 P0 γsv Ω / (sqrt(2π m) (kT)^{3/2}) (Table 1, p.2/2105): this is a pure Hertz–Knudsen (kinetic emission) rate with no gas-diffusion term and no condensation coefficient — i.e. the t^{1/3} law is the attachment(emission)-limited vapor law. Supports outline L877–878/L937–940 ("the t^{1/3} law is for sublimation and desublimation limited by attachment").
- Measured exponents at saturation: "values for exponent α of 0.29 ± 0.01, 0.33 ± 0.01, 0.30 ± 0.01, and 0.26 ± 0.01" (p.3/2106, Fig. 3). Range 0.26–0.33 — matches outline L950 "0.26–0.33".
- t0 is fitted as "an experimental delay between the initial contact of the spheres and the first image"; "Errors were determined by adjusting t0 by one frame (±15 s)" (p.3/2106). Relevant to Appendix B's sensitivity to t0 — Demmenie also fit with a free t0.
- Undersaturated: "When we deliberately set the humidity too low and let the ice sublimate within 2.5 h, the competition between neck growth and sublimation resulted in an exponent of ∼1/7 (see Figure S2)" (p.3/2106). So 1/7 under undersaturation is an observation, from one SI figure; they attribute it to sublimation competing, not to surface diffusion.
- Kingery's 1/7: "this outcome resulted from an underestimation of the relative humidity" (p.2/2105).
- Literature range: "different experiments reported values of α ranging between 1/3 and 1/7" (p.1/2104).
- Vapor dominance with a small-neck exception (citing Maeno & Ebinuma 1983): "Maeno and Ebinuma predicted that vapor phase mass transport would dominate ice sintering ... An exception was identified for neck sizes that were <8% of the initial sphere diameter (r/R0 < 0.08), where surface diffusion becomes predominant within certain temperature ranges" (p.4/2107). Directly relevant to the Introduction's "Mass moves mainly by vapor diffusion" and outline's "for necks larger than a few micrometres".
- Snow vs ice sintering: snow densifies under gravity; growth-rate laws "should not be applied to snow sintering" (p.1–2). Supports the outline's decision that the analogues are unloaded ice-sphere samples, not natural snow.
- Convention: r is called neck radius in Eq. 1, but the Fig. 3 caption says "normalized neck diameter, with respect to the initial sphere diameter (r/R0)" (p.3) — ambiguous; the exponent is unaffected (ratio of same-type quantities).
- Chen et al. protrusions attributed possibly to a temperature gradient (p.4/2107).

## Claims supported / contradicted / qualified
- Draft L434 "theorized trends from Demmenie et al., stating for ice growth dominated by vapor transport, the neck width scales with t^{1/3}": partially — they measured 0.26–0.33 and the law (theirs, Table 1) is the kinetic (attachment) vapor law. "Theorized" understates; "dominated by vapor transport" is fine in their sense (mechanism n = 2) but in our model language it is attachment-limited.
- Draft L452 "neck growth rate as proposed by Demmenie, stating w(t) ∝ t^{1/3}": they derive and measure it; the derivation is classical (small-neck approx).
- Draft L499 "w(t) ∝ t^{1/3} (Demmenie)" when "kinetics limited (e.g., small α_c)": Demmenie do not discuss α_c; their law is pure Hertz–Knudsen with α = 1 implicitly. Saturated → their experiments; kinetics-limited is implied by the form, not stated.
- Draft L499 "the theorized scaling for unsaturated sintering of w(t) ∝ t^{1/7}": in Demmenie, 1/7 under undersaturation is an observation (SI Fig S2) and the classical 1/7 is the surface-diffusion exponent. Calling it "theorized scaling for unsaturated sintering" is wrong — there is no theory of 1/7 for undersaturated vapor sintering in this paper. Supports outline L1168–1170.
- Outline L395–404 (classical exponents 1/3, 1/5, 1/7): Table 1 here gives exactly these, so Demmenie can carry the classical exponents while Kuczynski/Kingery & Berg/Hobbs & Mason are missing locally.
- Outline L950 "Demmenie measure 0.26–0.33 at saturation and about 1/7 when undersaturated": supported (p.3/2106).
