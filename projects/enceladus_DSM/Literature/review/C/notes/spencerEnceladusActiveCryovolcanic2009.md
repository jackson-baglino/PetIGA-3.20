## Summary
Book-chapter review of Enceladus as of early 2009: interior and tidal heating, geology, surface composition and processes, plume observations (ISS, CIRS, UVIS, INMS, CDA, VIMS, MAG/CAPS), plume models, astrobiology, future missions.

## Findings relevant to the manuscript
- Tiger-stripe temperatures (2009 state): tiger stripes "radiate heat at temperatures up to at least 167 K" (abstract, p.1/683); "places on the surface as warm as 167 K" (p.25/707); a gas source temperature "of at least 180 K could be inferred" from vapor-pressure equilibrium with UVIS flux (p.25/707). The chapter's directly measured surface value is 167 K; 180 K is an inference for the gas source. (Spencer & Nimmo 2013 later give surface ≥180 K.)
- Grain size near the tiger stripes and its possible causes: "The tectonically deformed regions exhibit particles ranging from 40 µm to about 120 µm with the largest ones (about 2% reaching values of about 0.2 mm) located close to the center of the south polar tiger stripes ... Possible causes for the large grain sizes near the tiger stripes include preferential fallout of larger, slower, plume grains near their sources (Jaumann et al. 2008, Hedman et al. 2009 ...), or sintering and growth of grains resulting from deposition of plume gases or from the enhanced temperatures near the tiger stripes" (p.21–22/703). Directly relevant to the outline's Section 5.5 ("grain size increasing toward the fractures in VIMS data (Jaumann)") and to the motivation (sintering near warm fractures).
- Surface alteration: VIMS interpretation is complicated by "radiation damage, sintering, loss of volatiles, and change in crystal size" (p.29/711).
- Thermal inertia / unconsolidated surface: "very low average thermal inertia of 15 W m⁻² s⁻¹ᐟ² K⁻¹ ... A highly unconsolidated particulate surface at least a few centimeters deep is implied. Equatorial temperatures reach a peak of about 75 K at noon and drop to 50–60 K at night" (p.23/705); north pole <33 K, unconsolidated to ≥1 m (p.23/705).
- Plume particles ~1 µm radius (Hedman et al. 2009; p.30/712); most fall back (p.24/706).
- Jets located on the tiger stripes near the warmest regions (Spitale & Porco 2007) (p.24/706).
- Surface plume fallout coats much of the surface (p.22–23/704–705).

## Claims supported / contradicted / qualified
- Outline L1333 (Section 5.5 cite box lists Spencer et al. 2009 with Spencer & Nimmo 2013 for Tiger-Stripe temperatures ~180 K): the 2009 chapter gives ≥167 K measured, ≥180 K inferred for the vent gas source. For "temperatures may reach 180 K" cite Spencer & Nimmo 2013 (measured, ≥180 K); the 2009 chapter is weaker for this number.
- Outline L1330–1331 (grain size increasing toward fractures; Jaumann): supported; and Spencer 2009 explicitly lists sintering at enhanced temperatures as a possible cause (p.21–22). Good closely matching support for Section 5.5's qualitative link.
- Intro 3b "a porous surface layer insulates": supported qualitatively by the very low thermal inertia and unconsolidated surface (p.23/705).
