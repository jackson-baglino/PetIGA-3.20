# postberg_macromolecular_2018 — Postberg, Khawaja, et al., Nature 558, 564–568 (2018)
DOI (PDF p.1): 10.1038/s41586-018-0246-4. Read in full (26 pp incl. Methods and Extended Data captions).

## Summary
Cassini CDA spectra of E-ring and plume ice grains (7,353 spectra, 2004–2008) and INMS fast/slow plume flyby spectra reveal high-mass organic cations (HMOCs) from macromolecular organics (>200 u), aromatic and aliphatic, with O/N groups; proposed origin: an organic film on the water table dispersed by bursting bubbles, organics coated by condensing ice.

## Findings relevant to the manuscript
- Complex organics in emitted ice grains: "Here we report observations of emitted ice grains containing concentrated and complex macromolecular organic material with molecular masses above 200 atomic mass units" (abstract, p.1/564).
- Grains: "Particle radii are mostly between 0.2 µm and 2 µm" (p.2/565); "an ice–organic mixture constitutes the bulk composition of these particles"; organic fraction "up to the per cent level" (p.2/565).
- Frequency: HMOC-type are ~1.1% of all spectra (≈3% of organic type-2), up to ~4% near Enceladus' orbit at optimal impact speeds (Methods, p.6). One HMOC grain recorded directly in the plume (E17), radius ~2 µm (Ext. Data Fig. 6, p.19).
