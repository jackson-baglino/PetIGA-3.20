## Summary
SNOWPACK Part III: input data control at IMIS stations, statistical new-snow density and albedo models, Monin–Obukhov surface fluxes, wind pumping, surface hoar and crust formation, evaluation by an objective profile-comparison score (>0.8).

## Findings relevant to the manuscript
- Conductivity in SNOWPACK comes from "our microstructure model (Part II)"; wind pumping adds an effective diffusivity Kp to "the effective thermal conductivity (Part II)" (p.6/174). So the conductivity parameterization itself is in Part II (Lehning et al. 2002b, CRST 35, 147–167), not in this paper.
- Wind pumping "improved simulations of the thermal regime in the snow cover" (p.6/174); temperature score improved from 0.81 to 0.96 (p.14/182).
- Snow-cover models used for avalanche warning; detailed layering (p.1–2).

If the citation is meant to support "conductivity is an input of the model", Part II (Lehning et al. 2002, "Part II: Snow microstructure", CRST 35, 147–167) is the matching paper; Part III is acceptable only for "detailed snowpack models" generally.
