# spencerEnceladusActiveIce2013 — Spencer & Nimmo, Annu. Rev. Earth Planet. Sci. 41, 693–717 (2013)
DOI (PDF p.1): 10.1146/annurev-earth-050212-124025. Read in full (27 pp; pp. 21–27 are references and journal contents).

## Summary
Review of Enceladus after Cassini: bulk properties, geology, the active south polar terrain (SPT) and tiger stripes, plume structure and composition, surface properties, interior, tidal heating, plume sources, habitability.

## Findings relevant to the manuscript
- Tiger-stripe temperatures: "Measurements of the thermal emission show that surface temperatures within the troughs reach at least 180 K over regions tens of meters wide along the length of the tiger stripes (Spencer et al. 2009, 2011)" (p.8, printed 700). So 180 K is a lower bound, confined to narrow strips tens of metres wide inside the ~2 km-wide troughs.
- Four tiger stripes "approximately 130 km long and spaced 35 km apart" (p.7/699); jets erupt from the tiger stripes (Spitale & Porco 2007) and merge into a plume (p.8/700).
- Plume particles: "predominantly water ice, but most contain approximately 1% salt, mostly NaCl (Postberg et al. 2011), and have typical radii of a few micrometers" (p.9/701). Larger, salt-rich grains mostly fall back (p.9/701). E ring "comprising ∼6% salt-rich particles (Postberg et al. 2009)" but preferentially salt-poor small particles (p.10–11).
- Surface coated by re-impacted plume particles (Kempf et al. 2010) (p.9–10/701–702); grain size largest near the tiger stripes (Jaumann et al. 2008; Porco 2006) (p.10/702).
- Surface conductivity: "Surface temperatures at low latitudes vary from ∼50 K at night to ∼80 K during the day (Howett et al. 2010). The diurnal temperature variation implies a very low surface thermal conductivity and a fluffy unconsolidated surface, as expected for plume particle fallout" (p.10/702).
- SPT age < a few Myr (p.7/699). Endogenic power 15.8 ± 3.1 GW (p.8/700).

## Claims supported / contradicted / qualified
- Draft L259 / outline L335–336 "Near the Tiger Stripes, where temperatures may reach 180 K (Spencer & Nimmo 2013)": supported, with nuance: "at least 180 K" and only over regions tens of metres wide within the troughs (p.8/700). The outline wording "may reach 180 K" is fine; "up to 180 K" (draft) slightly misstates ("at least").
- Draft L259 "micron-sized, composed primarily of water ice, and contain percent level NaCl (Postberg 2009)": Spencer & Nimmo (p.9/701) give the cleaner statement for the plume grains ("most contain approximately 1% salt, mostly NaCl", citing Postberg 2011). Postberg 2009 is about E-ring grains (6% Na-rich). Suggest citing Spencer & Nimmo 2013 (or Postberg 2011, not in library) for the plume-grain salt content.
- Outline L1330 / Section 5.5 "Tiger Stripe temperatures up to about 180 K (Spencer)": supported as above.
