# auriaultHeterogeneousMediumEquivalent1991 — Auriault, Int. J. Eng. Sci. 29(7), 785–795 (1991)
DOI: 10.1016/0020-7225(91)90001-J (from the reference list of Fourteau 2021; this PDF's first page shows no DOI). Read in full (11 pp).

## Summary
Methodological paper: when does a finely heterogeneous medium under some excitation admit an equivalent (intrinsic) macroscopic description? Answer: a good separation of scales ε = l/L ≪ 1 for both geometry and excitation; method = double-scale asymptotic expansions with cell problems and compatibility conditions. Examples: Stokes flow → Darcy's law; elastodynamics of composites (homogenizable only if wavelength ≫ cell). No heat conduction example; no corrector vector named t.

## Findings relevant to the manuscript
- Separation of scales: "l/L = ε ≪ 1" (Eq. 1, p.2/786); applies to geometry and to the excitation ("The right L is the smallest one").
- Terminology: "elementary representative volume (ERV)" (p.2/786) — the draft writes "representative element volume (REV)"; outline suggests "elementary". Auriault uses ERV.
- Periodicity: "If we limit ourselves to the discovery of the structure of the macroscopic description, periodic and random media are equivalent, if homogenization is possible. It is therefore convenient to assume that the medium is periodic" (p.4/788). "The ERV reduces to the periodic cell when the medium is periodic" (p.2/786).
- Self-consistency: "if the unknowns can be found in the form (6), where the Φ(i) are y-stationary or y-periodic, then homogenization is possible" (p.4/788).
- Average theorem warning: "A frequent snare in this field is constituted by the average theorem, which is valid whether or not homogenization is possible" (p.1/785).

## Claims supported / contradicted / qualified
- Draft L353 "two length scales ... l ≪ L ... we further assume, without less of generality, that the medium is periodic ... as described in Calonne 2011 and Auriault 1991": the scale-separation part is supported (p.2). On periodicity, Auriault says periodic and random media give the same *structure* of the macroscopic law, so assuming periodicity is a convenience for deriving the form; it is not without loss of generality for the *values* of K_eff of a random packing (the cell must be an ERV). The outline's correction (L748–749: "periodicity is an assumption") is right for the values; suggested wording: "We assume the medium periodic; for a random medium this gives the same form of the macroscopic law provided the cell is a representative volume (Auriault, 1991)". "less" → "loss".
- Draft L362 "As is shown in Auriault 1991 and Calonne 2011, the corresponding macroscopic description is ∇·(K_eff∇T) = 0" with K_eff defined through t: Auriault 1991 does NOT treat heat conduction or define t; it gives the general method and the Darcy and elastic examples. Partially supported (method only). For the heat-conduction cell problem and the name t, cite Auriault, Boutin & Geindreau 2009 book (check chapter) and/or Calonne 2011.
- Draft L282 "a diffuse interface adapted homogenization technique initially outlined by Auriault 1991": acceptable for the asymptotic method's general statement, but "initially outlined" overstates — the method is from Bensoussan–Lions–Papanicolaou (1978) and Sanchez-Palencia (1980), refs [1,2] in this paper (p.11). Suggest "the homogenization method of multiple-scale expansions (e.g., Auriault, 1991; Auriault et al., 2009)".
