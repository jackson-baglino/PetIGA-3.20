# maenoPressureSinteringIce1983 — Maeno & Ebinuma, J. Phys. Chem. 87, 4103–4110 (1983)
Read (8 pp; pressure-sintering diagram sections skimmed).
## Summary
Review of two-sphere ice sintering experiments with pressureless sintering diagrams (six mechanisms, 35 and 350 µm spheres), and pressure-sintering diagrams for polar snow densification.
## Findings
- Exponents x^q/r^p = Bt: "surface diffusion, p = 4, q = 7; lattice diffusion, p = 3, q = 5; vapor transport, p = 2, q = 3" (Kuczynski) (p.1).
- Table I (p.2): Kingery q = 6.1–7.1 (radius 100–3000 µm); Kuroiwa 4.0–7.7; **Hobbs and Mason q = 4.2–6.2** (radius 15–350 µm, −3 to −20 °C, x/r 0.1–0.6).
- Hobbs & Mason "reevaluated Kuczynski's theory for the vapor transport mechanism ... taking into account that the mean free path of water molecules in air at atmospheric pressure is not large compared with the radius of the neck, and the growth rate ... is controlled ... by the rate at which the latent heat of sublimation escapes" and concluded vapor transport dominates; they faulted Kingery's experiment for lacking an ice-saturated enclosure (p.1–2).
- "The vapor transport mechanism was concluded to be dominant in most natural and laboratory conditions, but in the very initial stage surface diffusion is also an important mechanism" (abstract).
