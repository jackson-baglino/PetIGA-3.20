DOI (PDF p.2): 10.1088/0022-3727/40/21/R02. Read in full (32 pp; PDF page N = article page R(353+N), IOP cover page first). Key passages (pp.14, 16) checked on rendered page images.

## Summary
Topical review of ice sintering from a materials-science viewpoint: driving forces, transport mechanisms, sintering maps, experimental techniques (light microscopy, LTSEM, XMT, gas adsorption), early ice-sphere experiments (Kingery, Kuroiwa, Hobbs & Mason, Maeno & Ebinuma), pressure sintering/firn densification, grain-boundary grooves (Colbeck), recent SSA work, modelling approaches (including phase field), liquid-phase sintering, mechanics.

## Findings relevant to the manuscript
- Surface transport does not densify: "Bulk transport mechanisms lead to shrinkage and the mutual approach of the centres of two particles whereas surface transport mechanisms [evaporation–condensation and surface diffusion] do not lead to shrinkage" (p.4/R357). Conclusion: "Vapour diffusion does not lead to densification" (p.28/R381). Directly supports the outline's Scope bullet (L649–651).
- SSA decreases also for non-densifying sintering: "for surface transport (although there is no densification the structure coarsens and there is a decrease in SSA)" (p.14/R367).
- Classical (Kuczynski) exponents, rendered p.14/R367: (X/D)^n = Bt/D^m with "surface diffusion, n = 7, m = 4; lattice diffusion, n = 5, m = 3; vapour transport, n = 3, m = 2; viscous or plastic flow, n = 2, m = 1".
- Hobbs & Mason's vapor model gives a DIFFERENT exponent: "Hobbs and Mason's modified model gave values of n = 5 and m = 3 for vapour transport; these are the same values as Kucynzski determined for volume diffusion" (rendered p.16/R369) — because they treated vapor supply by diffusion through air (MFP 0.1 µm ≪ neck). They distinguished mechanisms by absolute rates (vapor ~10⁴ × faster than volume diffusion); their measurements were ~5× below their vapor prediction but with the same temperature dependence (p.16).
  → The n = 3 (t^{1/3}) law is the kinetics(attachment)-limited vapor law; diffusion-limited vapor transport gives t^{1/5}. This matches the outline's Appendix B ("Attachment alone gives x³ ∝ t; diffusion alone x⁵ ∝ t").
- Hobbs & Mason (1964) experiments: particles 50–700 µm diameter, −3 to −20 °C; in unsaturated air "neck growth was observed for about the first 0.3 h of contact, then it appeared to cease; after about 1 h a reduction in diameter of the particles due to sublimation was apparent" (p.15–16/R368–369) — directly relevant to Molaro's unsaturated pair and Demmenie's ~1/7 under undersaturation.
- Kingery 1960 (0.1–3 mm radius, −2 to −25 °C) concluded surface diffusion; rejected because it required an implausibly large Ds and activation energy "twice the value for the latent heat of sublimation"; "the ice spheres were not contained within an ice saturated enclosure" (p.14/R367).
- Maeno & Ebinuma (1983) sintering map: vapor transport dominant "under most natural and laboratory conditions", with surface diffusion dominant "at small neck radii (ca <2 µm) at homologous temperatures corresponding to 0 to −6 °C, and (for neck radii of ca <1 µm) below ∼−40 °C" (p.17/R370). Strongly supports the outline's "vapor dominates for necks larger than a few micrometres".
- Why vapor dominates for ice: unusually high vapor pressure (vapor pressure 133 Pa at homologous temperature 0.94 for ice) (p.17/R370).
- Grain-boundary grooves: equilibrium dihedral angle 145°; γsv = 109 mJ/m², γss = 65 mJ/m² (Ketcham & Hobbs 1969) (Fig. 5, p.7/R360). γsv = 0.109 J/m² matches Table 1.
- Phase-field simulation named as promising for many sintering particles (p.24/R377).
- Miller et al. (2003) model reproduces Hobbs & Mason's isothermal results; "a factor of eight decrease in bond growth rate from 0 to −20 °C" (p.21/R374).

## Claims supported / contradicted / qualified
- Draft L262 (Blackford 2007 as example of sintering studies): appropriate — it IS a sintering review.
- Outline L395–404 (classical exponents "1/3 for evaporation–condensation, 1/5, 1/7 for volume and surface diffusion (Kuczynski 1949; Kingery & Berg 1955; for ice, Hobbs & Mason 1964)"): PARTIALLY CONTRADICTED for the Hobbs & Mason attribution. Per Blackford (p.16), Hobbs & Mason's vapor model gives n = 5 (1/5), not 1/3; 1/3 is Kuczynski's kinetics-limited evaporation–condensation. Citing Hobbs & Mason for "1/3 for ice" would be wrong (Hobbs & Mason itself is missing locally — not-checkable directly, but Blackford's review states this). Suggest: "1/3 for evaporation–condensation limited by attachment, 1/5 when vapor diffusion through the gas limits it (Hobbs & Mason 1964, as summarized by Blackford 2007), 1/5 and 1/7 for volume and surface diffusion (Kuczynski 1949)" — and cite Blackford 2007 (local, verified) for the set.
- Draft L262 "Other modes of diffusion tend to have limited effects or operate over planetary timescales": Blackford/Maeno & Ebinuma support vapor dominance with the small-neck caveat (<~2 µm near 0 to −6 °C).
- Outline Scope (vapor-transport sintering does not densify): supported (p.4, p.28).
