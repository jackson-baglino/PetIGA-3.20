DOI (PDF p.1): 10.1002/hyp.5800. Read in full (11 pp). Numbers checked on rendered pp. 4 and 9.

## Summary
Laboratory "snow breeder" (48 mm × 25 mm) inside a µCT (36 µm voxels, scans every 8 h): natural rounded snow (0.3–0.5 mm), densities 147–514 kg m⁻³, temperature gradients 25–100 K m⁻¹, mean temperature about −8 °C, ~1 week per run (p.4/3658, Table II). Effective heat conductivity (EHC) from measured heat fluxes and ΔT (Eq. 1) alongside SSA, structure size, SMI and anisotropy.

## Findings relevant to the manuscript
- EHC is a measured, apparent quantity that includes latent heat: "the aggregated coefficient of (i) conductivity through the ice matrix and the pore space, (ii) the latent heat release and gain by recrystallization and (iii) the convective heat exchange" (p.1/3655).
- Density stayed constant; "The heat conductivity changed by as much as twice its initial value, caused by changes in structure and texture, but not due to changes in density" (abstract p.1/3655).
- Two different behaviours (Fig. 7, p.9/3663, rendered): low-density snow (LDS, 147–302) "decreased as soon as a temperature gradient was applied and then increased or remained constant" (two stages marked only in Fig. 7a); high-density snow (HDS, 490–514) "increased immediately, almost doubled from the initial value and reached a plateau" (p.5/3659). Fig. 7b: sample 4 ~0.33 → ~0.56 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹.
- SSA: "SSA decreases only for LDS, not for HDS" (p.9/3663) — in HDS conductivity nearly doubled with no SSA decrease; the increase came from "thicker vertical ice structures" (p.10/3664). Rate of SSA change increased exponentially with decreasing density (p.5/3659, Fig. 5).
- "The parameterization of EHC by snow density alone (Sturm et al., 1997) is insufficient, especially when TGM exists" (p.10/3664). "Empirically, the age of snow changes the EHC at a similar magnitude to the density (Sturm et al., 2002)" (p.10/3664).
- "The concept of grains and bonds is probably too simple" (p.9/3663).

## Claims supported / contradicted / qualified
- Outline L508–512 (gap paragraph: Schneebeli & Sokratov and Calonne 2014 followed conductivity at constant density under a gradient): supported (p.1/3655, p.5/3659).
- Outline L554–556 "conductivity changed at nearly constant density, in two stages (as described by Calonne et al. 2014; not yet read)": partially. Two stages (drop then rise) only for low-density snow (Fig. 7a); high-density snow rose immediately and plateaued (Fig. 7b). Also their k is an apparent conductivity (includes latent heat), unlike the conductive-only k here.
- Outline L556–559 "under a gradient, losing surface area did not raise conductivity ... a redistribution between directions": qualified. In HDS conductivity nearly doubled while SSA did not decrease (p.9/3663); in LDS SSA fell and k first fell then rose. Rephrase: "under a gradient conductivity changed with little or no change in SSA, through vertical structures".
- Draft L279 "still unquantified": contradicted (as for Calonne 2014): conductivity at constant density measured here.
- Outline L1281 (Section 5: "Density is not enough"): supported — "parameterization of EHC by snow density alone ... is insufficient" (p.10/3664).
