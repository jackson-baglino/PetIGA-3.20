# fourteauImpactWaterVapor2021 — Fourteau, Domine & Hagenmuller, The Cryosphere 15, 2739–2755 (2021)
DOI: 10.5194/tc-15-2739-2021. Read (17 pp; appendix skimmed).

## Summary
Homogenized heat–vapor model for snow; simulations on 3D µCT microstructures comparing fast kinetics (saturated vapor at ice surface, α large) and slow kinetics (latent heat negligible, α small) for keff = Kcond + Kvap.

## Findings
- "the effective thermal conductivity of snow can be significantly larger, by up to about 50 % for low-density snow, than if water vapor transport is neglected" (abstract).
- Near melting fast-kinetics keff is 10–50% above slow kinetics; at 248 K 1–10% (Fig. 6, p. ~10).
- Latent heat also modifies the conductive part Kcond: "Kcond corresponds to the pure conduction ... in response to the actual microscopic thermal gradients that are influenced by the latent heat effects" (p. ~14); "one should therefore be careful when treating heat conduction as decoupled from vapor transport (e.g., Calonne et al., 2011...)".
- Unclear which kinetic limit applies in real snow (p. ~12).

## Claims
- Outline L565–568 "vapor transport under a gradient adds to the apparent conductivity; the conductivity here is conductive only, which is exact for isothermal sintering": partially. Correct that this is the paper to cite (not fourteauMacroscopic2021). But "exact for isothermal sintering" conflates two things: the microstructure evolves isothermally, yet keff is the response to an imposed gradient, under which vapor and latent heat add 1–50% depending on temperature, density and α. Suggested: "the conductivity here is the conductive part (slow-kinetics limit); under a gradient, vapor transport can add 1–10% at −25 °C and up to ~50% near melting in low-density snow (Fourteau et al., 2021)".
- Outline L1353–1357 ("check the figure quoted"): figures verified: up to ~50%; 10–50% near 0 °C; 1–10% at 248 K.

## Candidate additions
- Limitations/Section 2.3: as above. Note α_c = 10⁻³ is closer to the slow-kinetics limit, where the conductive keff is appropriate.
