# colbeckThermodynamicsSnowMetamorphism1980 — Colbeck, J. Glaciol. 26(94), 291–301 (1980)
DOI (PDF footer): 10.3189/S0022143000010832. Read in full (theory paper, OCR text).

## Summary
Thermodynamic treatment of curvature-driven ("equi-temperature", better "radius-of-curvature") metamorphism in dry snow, plus liquid-saturated and low-water-content wet snow. Derives Kelvin equation; shows vapor-pressure gradients dominate over temperature gradients in isothermal dry snow.

## Findings
- Dry-snow isothermal metamorphism is "dominated by the slow process of vapor diffusion between surfaces of different radii of curvature" and is so slow that imposed temperature gradients usually dominate (abstract).
- "Hobbs and Mason (1964) and Hobbs and Radke (1967) ... found that mass transfer through the vapor is more important than transfer by surface or volume diffusion" (p.3, printed ~293).
- Metamorphism "causes large changes in snow properties, especially strength, permeability, reflectivity, and thermal conductivity" (p.1).
- Curvature effects negligible for grains ≥ 0.1 mm compared with temperature gradients > 10⁻² K m⁻¹ (p.6).
- Theory only; no sintering experiment.
