# southworthSurfaceDepositionEnceladus2019 — Southworth, Kempf & Spitale, Icarus 319, 33–42 (2019)
DOI (PDF p.1): 10.1016/j.icarus.2018.08.024. Read in full (10 pp); Fig. 1–3 colourbars checked on the rendered PDF p.4 (printed 36).

## Summary
Updated surface-deposition maps of Enceladus plume fallout (particles 0.6–15 µm, M+ = 25 kg/s, size slope α = 3.1, deep-source speed distribution of Schmidt et al. 2008) for the 8 Spitale & Porco (2007) jets, the ~100 Porco et al. (2014) jets and a curtain plume (Spitale et al. 2015); argues long-term emissions are near-orthogonal to the surface.

## Findings relevant to the manuscript
- Deposition maps in mm/yr: Fig. 1 colourbar spans 10⁻⁶ to 1 mm/yr, brightest at the sources near the south pole (p.4/36); Fig. 2–3 colourbars 10⁻⁶ to 10⁻¹ mm/yr by size bin. The text gives no explicit numerical range like "0.01–1 mm/yr"; the maximum near the jets in Fig. 1 approaches ~1 mm/yr (read from the colour scale).
- Perfect compaction assumed — a lower bound on thickness: "assuming perfect compaction and water-ice-density provides a lower bound on deposition"; porous deposits scale by 1/(1−ψ) (p.3/35).
- Plume is "the dominant source of the resurfacing of Enceladus" (p.1/33).
- Most deposited mass is in large particles; most particles >1 µm do not escape (p.3/35).

## Claims supported / contradicted / qualified
- Outline L338–339 ("regions of heaviest fallout, 0.01–1 mm/yr (Kempf 2010; Southworth 2019)"): partially supported. Southworth's map reaches ~1 mm/yr near sources (Fig. 1); Kempf gives up to 0.5 mm/yr. The "0.01–1 mm/yr" range is Choukroun et al.'s (2020, p.7) summary of these two papers, not a number stated in either. Suggest citing Choukroun 2020 for the range as well, or "up to ~0.5–1 mm/yr near the vents (Kempf et al., 2010; Southworth et al., 2019)". Also note these are compacted-ice-equivalent rates; a porous deposit grows faster by 1/(1−φ).

## Candidate additions
- The 1/(1−ψ) point (p.3/35): a deposit of porosity ~0.5 accumulates about twice as thick as the quoted ice-equivalent rate — relevant if the manuscript compares burial with sintering. Optional.
