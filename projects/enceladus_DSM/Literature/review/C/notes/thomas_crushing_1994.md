# thomas_crushing_1994 — Thomas, Ratke & Kochan, Adv. Space Res. 14(12), (12)207–(12)216 (1994)

## Summary
DLR/KOSI comet-simulation lab work. (i) Two ice particles (r ≈ 120 µm) sintered under a microscope in a cold lab at −20 °C (253 K): neck growth x/r ∝ t^{1/3}. (ii) Porous ice and ice–olivine powders (sprayed into liquid N2), held isothermal at −20 °C in copper cans; crushing strength by spherical indentor rises with time and mineral content. (iii) Strength theory (Hall–Petch, notch factor, neck area (x/r)², porosity), and extrapolation to comets (<100 K: only surface diffusion can act).

## Findings relevant to the manuscript
- Two-sphere measurement at −20 °C: "We find a neck growth proportional to t^{1/3}, i.e., the sublimation and recondensation process predominate at a temperature of −20 °C" (p.4, printed (12)210); Fig. 3: "sintering of two spheres T = 253 K, r ≈ 120 µm" (p.4).
- Exponents (from Exner 1978): "x ∼ t^{1/n} {n = 3 for vapour transport, 4 < n ≤ 5 for surface diffusion, n = 6 for grain boundary diffusion, and 4 < n ≤ 5 for lattice, volume diffusion}" (p.3/(12)209). Note this differs from Demmenie's table (surface diffusion 1/7, volume 1/5) — the classical exponent assignments vary by source/geometry.
- Validity: "these power laws hold only for small ratios of the neck radius x to the particle radius r, i.e. x/r < 0.5" (p.3/(12)209).
- Mechanism via Kelvin equation: vapor pressure higher over convex surfaces, mass moves from convex to concave (p.2/(12)208) — supports the Figure 5 colour description in the outline (L1001–1003).
- Below 100 K vapor transport "can be excluded, since the vapour pressure of ice at temperature below 100 K is negligibly small" — only surface diffusion remains (p.8/(12)214). Surface-diffusion activation energy (Gjostein scaling) 14.8 kJ/mol (p.9).
- Strength rises with sintering time in isothermal porous ice at −20 °C (p.5–6).

## Claims supported / contradicted / qualified
- Outline "Two-grain argument ... no Thomas or Kingery runs" (L98–99): consistent; Thomas is an independent −20 °C two-sphere measurement that found t^{1/3} (vapor) — directly relevant to Section 3.2/5.1 as a second saturated-ish measurement at our benchmark temperature. Humidity control is not described.
- Outline L395–404 (classical exponents 1/3 vapor; 1/5, 1/7 volume and surface): partially — Thomas (via Exner) gives n = 4–5 for surface and volume diffusion and 6 for grain-boundary. Recommend wording "smaller exponents (between about 1/4 and 1/7, depending on the route and on the derivation)" or cite one source consistently (Demmenie Table 1).
- Draft L262 "Other modes ... operate over planetary timescales": Thomas p.8–9 argue the reverse at very low T — surface diffusion is the one route that still operates when vapor transport is frozen out (<100 K). Relevant to the Enceladus gap: at Enceladus temperatures our vapor-only model would not apply. Supports outline's "no extrapolation to Enceladus temperatures".
