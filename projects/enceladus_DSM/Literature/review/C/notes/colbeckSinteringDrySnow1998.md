# colbeckSinteringDrySnow1998 — Colbeck, J. Appl. Phys. 84, 4585–4589 (1998)
DOI (PDF p.1): 10.1063/1.368684. Read in full (6 pp incl. cover page).

## Summary
Theory paper. Argues that bonds between snow grains are grain-boundary grooves (equilibrium dihedral angle ~145°), not concave necks, and models bond growth by grain-boundary diffusion driven by a stress gradient, for two spherical grains with a growing dihedral angle. Predicts Y/R ∝ t^0.25 and time ∝ R^4. Observations of laboratory snow at about −3 °C (text layer shows "23 °C"; minus sign lost) and snow stored for years.

## Findings relevant to the manuscript
- History of mechanisms: "Kingery, who first applied this approach to ice grains, concluded that the bonding was due to surface diffusion ... Kuroiwa concluded that volume diffusion was the dominant process, but Hobbs and Mason believed that sublimation, transfer through the vapor phase, had to be the dominant mechanism. The vapor transfer mechanism ... has since received wide acceptance" (p.5, printed 4588).
- Mechanism identification by exponent: "the dominant mechanisms were identified by the observed dependence on time" (p.5/4588).
- Temperature gradients speed bond growth in nature: Keeler found faster bond growth in natural snow, "almost certainly due to the macroscopic temperature gradients ... These gradients cause vapor movement at a much greater rate than could occur just due to gradients in curvature or stress" (p.5/4588).
- Humidity affects strength development (Hosler et al.) (p.5/4588).
- Predicts exponent 1/4 (grain-boundary diffusion), "a little stronger than observed" (p.5/4588).
- Nothing on depth hoar, weak layers or avalanches.
