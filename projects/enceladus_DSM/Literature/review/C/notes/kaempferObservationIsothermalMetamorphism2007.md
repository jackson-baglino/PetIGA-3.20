# kaempferObservationIsothermalMetamorphism2007 — Kaempfer & Schneebeli, JGR 112, D24101 (2007)
DOI (PDF p.1): 10.1029/2007JD009047. Read in full (10 pp). Temperatures are −1.6, −8.3, −19.1, −54 °C (minus signs lost in text; confirmed in CHECK_COLLECTION against p.1).

## Summary
Four sealed samples of the same fresh snow (sieved 1 mm, holders 18.5 mm × 65 mm) held isothermally at −1.6, −8.3, −19.1 and −54 °C for ~1 year; µCT (10 µm voxels) about monthly; density, SSA, SMI, trabecular thickness/number from distance transforms.

## Findings relevant to the manuscript
- Classical exponents in X^n/D^m = Bt: "values of n = 7 and m = 4 would indicate surface diffusion, n = 5 and m = 3 volume diffusion, and n = 3 and m = 2 would suggest that the dominant process is evaporation-condensation" (p.2). History: Kingery surface diffusion, Kuroiwa volume diffusion, "Hobbs and Mason [1963] concluded that sublimation-condensation and transport through the vapor phase had to be the dominant process. The vapor transfer mechanism has since received wide acceptance" (p.2).
- Grain-boundary grooves: models with concave bond geometry "cannot be valid as soon as the grain boundary groove has formed" (Colbeck) (p.2); grooves visible in their tomography (Fig. 9, p.9).
- Continuous densification at isothermal conditions in samples 1–3: "The continuous densification implies that volume processes like grain boundary diffusion have to occur" (abstract p.1; p.8). Density and trabecular thickness relate linearly, slope independent of temperature (p.8).
- SSA: fit SSA = SSA0 (τ/(t+τ))^{1/n} "very well with R² > 0.99", n = 3.00, 3.55, 2.20 for samples 1–3 (p.7); interpreted as "the SSA decreases indeed logarithmically with time" (p.8). Exponents differ with temperature (Table 4, p.9), so the curves are not simple time shifts of one another.
- −54 °C: "SSA of the ice matrix decreased by only 19% during 1 year" (p.9); attributed possibly to temperature below homogeneous nucleation / absence of quasi-liquid layer (p.9).
- Two-stage evolution: SMI and trabecular number change quickly in the first month, then constant (p.7–8).

## Claims supported / contradicted / qualified
- Outline L398–401 (classical exponents, 1/3 for evaporation–condensation, 1/5 and 1/7 for volume and surface diffusion): supported, and this paper is already in the .bib — it can serve as an in-bib secondary source for the exponents (p.2), alongside Blackford 2007 and Kingery 1960, while Kuczynski / Kingery & Berg / Hobbs & Mason are missing PDFs.
- Outline L1320 (isothermal SSA decay in the laboratory, "forms only"): supported — the fit form is their Eq. 4 (p.6–7).
- Outline L1316–1317 test (i) "curves at different temperatures superpose under a time shift": qualified — in sealed isothermal snow the fitted exponents differ with temperature (n = 3.00, 3.55, 2.20; Table 4), so superposition by a pure time shift is not what snow shows; snow also densifies. The test is a model prediction; say the experiment should be on unloaded ice spheres at fixed porosity.
- Outline Section 6 "SSA falls and conductivity rises together, without densification": qualified — isothermal snow densified continuously (p.8). The paper's model holds porosity fixed by construction; say so where the claim is made.

## Candidate additions
- Intro para 4 (classical exponents): cite Kaempfer & Schneebeli (2007, p.2) for the 3/5/7 exponents and for vapor transport having "received wide acceptance" (in .bib).
- Limitations: snow sintering also densifies by volume/grain-boundary diffusion (p.8), which the model omits.
