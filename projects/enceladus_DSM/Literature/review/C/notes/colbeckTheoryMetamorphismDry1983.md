# colbeckTheoryMetamorphismDry1983 — Colbeck, JGR 88(C9), 5475–5482 (1983)
DOI: 10.1029/JC088iC09p05475 (from the Wiley footer in the PDF). Read in full (8 pp).

## Summary
Theory of temperature-gradient (TG) metamorphism of dry seasonal snow: interparticle vapor diffusion between warmer source and colder sink grains computed with electrostatic capacitance solutions, a geometric "enhancement factor" g with a log-normal distribution, and a surface-kinetics law with a critical excess vapor density (5–6e-10 g/cc, from Colbeck 1983 J. Appl. Phys.) separating rounded and faceted growth. Explains depth-hoar growth near the warm base and below crusts.

## Findings relevant to the manuscript
- Isothermal (curvature-driven) metamorphism is slow vapor sintering: "If no temperature gradient were imposed on a snow cover, small chemical potential differences due to differences in curvature and stress would cause small temperature and vapor pressure differences ... These small differences would cause slow grain and neck growth by vapor diffusion and heat flow" (p.1/5475).
- In natural snow covers curvature effects are negligible compared with imposed gradients: "Temperature differences due to the curvature of the rounded grains are very small when compared with the temperature differences arising between neighboring grains in any dry snow cover in nature, and can be ignored" (p.7–8/5481–5482).
- Growth in snow is diffusion-limited: "in the snow cover, β ≫ 1 and the growth rate is limited by vapor diffusion rather than surface kinetics" (p.4/5478).
- Recrystallization at constant density: "the density is often observed to remain constant while the snow cover recrystallizes into faceted crystals" (p.2/5476); bonds lost during rapid faceted growth (p.2).
- The kinetics law (Eq. 8) uses a temperature dependence from Lamb & Hobbs (1971) data; the critical supersaturation is from the author's separate laboratory paper (Colbeck 1983, J. Appl. Phys.) — this JGR paper is theory, not an experiment.
