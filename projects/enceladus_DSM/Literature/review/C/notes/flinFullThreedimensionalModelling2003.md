# flinFullThreedimensionalModelling2003 — Flin, Brzoska, Lesaffre, Coléou & Pieritz, J. Phys. D 36, A49–A54 (2003)
DOI not printed (PII S0022-3727(03)60117-8). Read in full (7 pp incl. IOP cover).

## Summary
3D voxel model of isothermal, curvature-driven snow metamorphism near 0 °C: Kelvin + Langmuir–Knudsen gives local deposition flux j = k(C_amb − C) (attachment-limited, vapor uniform in the pore, "equivalent to the surface-attachment-limited-kinetics (SALK) formulation"), mass conserved via C_amb; time in arbitrary units (k0 = 1). Applied to test shapes and µCT images from the −2 °C Col de Porte experiment.

## Findings relevant to the manuscript
- Assumptions: "we assume that metamorphism is limited by the evaporation–condensation mechanism ... vapour diffusion in the air is fast enough so that the vapour partial pressure is homogenous in the air phase. Surface diffusion, which generally occurs at first stages of the metamorphism is neglected here" (p.3/A50).
- Neck growth is dimension-dependent: "The evolution of a neck is directly related to its initial size. Note that it is a typical three-dimensional phenomenon: in two dimensions, a neck is always growing, whatever its size is" (p.5/A52). Directly relevant to the manuscript's 2D aggregates: in 2D the neck lacks the convex azimuthal curvature, so neck growth is favoured relative to 3D.
- Time scale undetermined: "experimental and numerical times differ by an undetermined multiplicative constant" because α is not known (p.4/A51) — parallels the paper's τ_sub ∝ 1/α_c uncertainty.
- SSA and mean curvature follow log laws in the −2 °C experiment (Fig. 5, p.6/A53).
- Real snow densifies by grain packing under gravity, which the model lacks (p.6/A53).

## Claims supported / contradicted / qualified
- Draft L262 (Flin 2003 among "Sintering has been studied extensively"): it is a model of isothermal snow metamorphism (curvature-driven coarsening and neck evolution), described by its authors as "curvature-driven sintering" (p.6) — acceptable for metamorphism/sintering in snow, but not a grain-pair sintering study (outline L392–394 fair).
- Draft L279 (Flin 2003 as snow-community literature on how DSM affects macroscopic properties): it does not compute any macroscopic property; weak support.
- Outline L1358–1361 (Limitations: two dimensions): Flin 2003 p.5 gives a concrete, citable reason why 2D can bias neck evolution — supports adding a sentence.

## Candidate additions
- Limitations (Section 5.6): "In two dimensions every neck grows, whereas in three dimensions small necks can shrink (Flin et al., 2003, p.A52)" — closely matching caveat for the 2D aggregates and for comparing 2D rates with 3D experiments.
- Section 2 lineage: Flin 2003 is an attachment-limited (SALK) curvature model with undetermined time scale — a predecessor to diffuse-interface vapor models; optional.
