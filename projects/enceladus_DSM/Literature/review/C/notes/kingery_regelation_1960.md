# kingery_regelation_1960 — Kingery, J. Appl. Phys. 31, 833–838 (1960)
DOI (PDF p.1): 10.1063/1.1735704. Read in full (7 pp incl. AIP cover; PDF p.2 opens with the end of the previous article). Table I checked on rendered PDF p.3 (printed 834). Not in the manuscript .bib (Zotero key only).

## Summary
Ice spheres (sprayed into liquid oxygen; radii 0.1–3 mm) brought into light contact on a microscope slide in a constant-temperature room (no separate enclosure) at −2.2, −9.5, −17.8, −25.1 °C. Neck growth (x/r) ∝ t^{1/n} with mean n ≈ 6.1–7.1; no shrinkage; time to x/r = 0.1 ∝ r⁴; activation energy 27.5 kcal/mol. Concludes surface diffusion.

## Findings relevant to the manuscript
- Table I (rendered p.3/834): (x/r)² = A(T)t/r viscous flow; (x/r)³ = B(T)t/r² evaporation–condensation, shrinkage none; (x/r)⁵ = C(T)t/r³ volume diffusion; (x/r)⁷ = D(T)t/r⁴ surface diffusion, shrinkage none. "the time dependence of neck growth varies, depending on the process taking place, from the one-half to the one-seventh power of time" (p.4/835). Cites Kuczynski 1949 and Kingery & Berg 1955 for these.
- Measured "(x/r) ∼ t^{1/6.9}" at −17.8 °C (p.5/836); Table II means 6.2 (−2.2 °C), 6.1/7.1 (other T), 6.9 (−17.8 °C).
- Set-up: the "entire apparatus in a constant temperature room so that no separate enclosure for the particles" (p.4/835) — i.e., humidity uncontrolled; Demmenie (2025) attribute his ~1/7 to underestimated (too low) humidity; Blackford (2007) also notes the spheres "were not contained within an ice saturated enclosure".
- Evaporation–condensation rate formula (Eq. 6) is the Langmuir (Hertz–Knudsen, α = 1, in vacuo) form; under atmospheric pressure "the rate of vaporization is decreased as a result of diffusion through a boundary layer", an order of magnitude lower (p.6/837).
