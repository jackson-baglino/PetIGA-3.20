# flinThreedimensionalGeometricMeasurements2004 — Flin, Brzoska, Lesaffre, Coléou & Pieritz, Ann. Glaciol. 38, 39–44 (2004)
DOI (PDF p.1): 10.3189/172756404781814942. Read in full (6 pp).

## Summary
3-month isothermal metamorphism experiment on natural fresh snow at −2 °C (closed box, cold room), µCT (4.91 µm voxels, analyses at 9.82 µm on 2.5 mm cubes) of ten samples; algorithms for porosity, SSA (voxel projection), normal-vector anisotropy and mean curvature; time series of each.

## Findings relevant to the manuscript
- SSA declines logarithmically in time under isothermal conditions: "By fitting this evolution with a logarithmic function, we obtain ... SSA(t) = −157.23 log(t) + 681.52 ... correlation coefficient of 0.986. This law is in fairly good agreement with the results based on CH4 adsorption" (p.4/42, Eq. 8; Fig. 3).
- Mechanism near 0 °C: "Near 0 °C, one can expect a reduced influence of possible surface or bulk diffusion at grain boundaries ... owing to the large saturation vapour pressure of water at these temperatures" (p.1/39). Isothermal metamorphism "mainly governed by the distribution of mean curvature ... like many sintering processes" (p.1/39).
- Porosity decreased (packing) during the experiment, following "classical dry-snow packing laws" (p.4/42) — natural snow densifies; contrast with our non-densifying model.
- Slight vertical anisotropy develops ("suggests gravity effects", p.4–5/42–43).
- SSA defined per unit mass (p.2/40).

## Claims supported / contradicted / qualified
- Draft L279 (Flin 2004 among snow-community literature on how metamorphism affects macroscopic properties): this paper reports microstructure (SSA, porosity, curvature) only, no macroscopic property like conductivity. Weakly relevant for "macroscopic properties".
- Outline L1050 / L1317 (SSA falls linearly in log time in isothermal snow; Legagneux & Domine 2005, Taillandier 2007 as functional form): Flin 2004 is an additional, closely matching µCT source for the log-time form (p.4/42, R = 0.986). Note snow densified here, so functional form only.
- Introduction para 4 ("Mass moves mainly by vapor diffusion"): Flin 2004 p.1 offers support near 0 °C.

## Verification (rendered PDF p.4, printed 42)
- Eq. 8 confirmed: SSA(t) = −157.23 log(t) + 681.52 (cm² g⁻¹, t in h), R = 0.986. Fig. 2a: porosity fell from ~0.89 to ~0.71 over 2000 h (densification), Fig. 2b layer thickness to ~40%.
