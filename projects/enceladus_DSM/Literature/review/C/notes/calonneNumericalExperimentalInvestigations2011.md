# calonneNumericalExperimentalInvestigations2011 — Calonne et al., GRL 38, L23501 (2011)
DOI (PDF p.1): 10.1029/2011GL049234. Read in full (6 pp).

## Summary
Full keff tensor from 30 µCT snow images (all seasonal types) by periodic homogenization (Auriault 1991; Auriault et al. 2009; Geodict), conduction in ice and air only, steady state; compared with needle-probe and flux/gradient data.

## Findings
- Homogenization: separation of scales and REV; cell problem for periodic t_ice, t_air with flux continuity on Γ (Eqs. 1–11, p.2). REV for conductivity 2.5 mm (PP) to 5.5 mm (MF) (p.2).
- kice = 2.107, kair = 0.024 at 271 K; 2.900 and 0.019 at 203 K (p.2; text typo "kair = 2.107").
- keff strongly correlated with density, quadratic fit Eq. 12, r = 0.985, residual s.d. 0.025 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹ (p.3); agrees with Yen (1981).
- Anisotropy: kz/kxy as low as 0.7 (deep RG) and as high as 1.5 (FC, DH) (p.3).
- keff increases with decreasing temperature, mostly following kice (p.3).
- Neglecting air conduction underestimates kz by ×10 (PP), up to ×2 for denser snow (p.3–4).
- Needle-probe values systematically lower; apparent vs effective conductivity differ (p.4).

## Claims
- Draft L256 "effective thermal conductivity of snowpacks and firn determines how heat moves ... affecting the stability of glacial ice, snowpacks, and permafrost (Calonne 2011)": partially — supports heat transfer being critical "to assess the climatic and hydrological role of snow" and mechanical stability via morphology (p.1); firn, glacial ice and permafrost are not discussed.
- Draft L353, L362 (homogenization following Auriault and Calonne): supported (p.2).
- Outline L515–520 (Eq. 12, r = 0.985; kz/kxy 0.7–1.5; Fig. 1, Sect. 3.2): supported.
- Paper's temperature collapse: Calonne shows keff rises as T falls because kice rises (2.107 → 2.900 from 271 → 203 K). If the paper holds kice and kair fixed across its temperature sweep, the conductivity–SSA collapse across temperature excludes this effect; say so (Calonne 2019 gives 2.107/2.330 at −3/−20 °C).

## Candidate additions
- Methods/limitations: k_ice is temperature dependent (p.2–3); state the values used and that they are held fixed.
