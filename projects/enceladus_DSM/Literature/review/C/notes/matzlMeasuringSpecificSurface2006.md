# matzlMeasuringSpecificSurface2006 — Matzl & Schneebeli, J. Glaciol. 52(179), 558–564 (2006)
DOI (PDF p.1): 10.3189/172756506781828412. Read in full (7 pp).

## Summary
Calibrated near-infrared (840–940 nm) photography of snow-pit walls, with SSA of 29 cast samples measured by stereology on surface sections; exponential reflectance–SSA calibration (R² = 0.908); maps SSA in 2D at ~1 mm resolution with ≤15% uncertainty.

## Findings relevant to the manuscript
- SSA is measurable in the field: "Calibrated NIR photography allows quantitative determination of SSA and its spatial variation in a snow profile in two dimensions within an uncertainty of 15%" (abstract, p.1/558).
- Other SSA methods listed: adsorption, microtomography, stereology, "restricted to relatively small snow samples" (p.1/558).
- SSA definition here: "SSA (mm⁻¹) is defined as the ratio between surface area and the volume of the ice phase" (p.1/558) — per ice volume, not per mass (others use m²/kg). The manuscript must state its definition and units.
- SSA as the state variable of sintered materials: "The specific surface area (SSA) is an essential microstructural parameter for the characterization of sintered materials such as snow (German, 1996). The SSA of snow changes during isothermal and temperature-gradient metamorphism" (p.1/558).
- Directly motivating a conductivity–SSA law: "Because of the widespread use of SSA to parameterize air permeability and thermal conductivity of sintered materials (German, 1996), it can be expected that this should also be possible for snow" (p.5–6/562–563).
- SSA–density only loosely correlated, especially 150–300 kg/m³ (Fig. 4, p.4–5/561–562).
