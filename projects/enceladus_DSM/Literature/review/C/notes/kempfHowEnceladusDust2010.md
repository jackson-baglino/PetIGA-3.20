# kempfHowEnceladusDust2010 — Kempf, Beckmann & Schmidt, Icarus 206, 446–457 (2010)
DOI (PDF p.1): 10.1016/j.icarus.2009.09.016. Read in full (12 pp).

## Summary
Dynamical simulations (gravity, Lorentz force, self-consistent grain charging) of grains (5 nm–5 µm; jets 10 nm–50 µm) launched from the eight Spitale & Porco (2007) jets, with the Schmidt et al. (2008) plume model (~5 kg/s dust). Reproduces the vertical E-ring profile (CDA) and computes surface deposition rates of plume fallout on Enceladus.

## Findings relevant to the manuscript
- Deposition rates: "The deposition rate ranges between 0.5 mm per year in the immediate vicinity of the jet sources in the tiger stripe region and about 10^-5 mm per year at the equator" (p.10, printed 455). "Here, the growth rate of the regolith layer can be as high as 0.5 mm per year" (p.10/455). Map Fig. 8 (grains 0.5–5 µm).
- So Kempf's maximum is 0.5 mm/yr, not 1 mm/yr. The manuscript's "0.01–1 mm yr−1 (Kempf et al., 2010; Southworth et al., 2019)" must come from Southworth for the upper/lower bounds or from Choukroun quoting both. Check Southworth.
- Grain sizes: plume grains up to ~5 µm ("the largest jet particles ... of about 5 µm") vs regolith near tiger stripes 30±10 to 50±15 µm, up to 75 µm (citing Jaumann 2008) (p.10/455; p.9/454).
- Sintering-relevant hypothesis: the large surface grains may result from "re-crystallisation and compactification of small grains, possibly related to the local temperature of the ice (annealing), which might explain the decrease of grain sizes with distance from the tiger stripes" (p.10/455). Directly relevant to the Enceladus implications (Section 5.5) — a published suggestion that warm-region surface processing coarsens deposits.
- Plume composition summary: E-ring grains dominated by water ice; type III (~6%) salt-rich (Na/H2O ~1e-3) (p.2/447), citing Postberg 2008/2009.
- Plume jets located by Spitale & Porco (2007) at thermal hotspots (p.1/446).
- Burial time: a 10 m boulder 100 m from a jet covered in 1e5–1e6 yr (abstract, p.1; p.10/455).

## Claims supported / contradicted / qualified
- Outline L338–339 / draft L259-context ("heaviest fallout, 0.01–1 mm yr−1 (Kempf et al., 2010; Southworth et al., 2019)"): partially — Kempf supports "heaviest fallout near the tiger stripes" and gives up to 0.5 mm/yr there; the 0.01 and 1 bounds are not from Kempf. Verify against Southworth and Choukroun.
- Outline L1330–1331 ("grain size increasing toward the fractures in VIMS data (Jaumann)"): Kempf restates Jaumann's numbers (p.9–10) — supporting context; cite Jaumann directly.
- "plume's grains are micron-sized" (draft L259): Kempf supports (largest ~5 µm; sizes simulated 0.1–5 µm) — but the draft cites Postberg 2009 there, fine.

## Candidate additions
- Section 5.5: Kempf et al. (2010, p.10/455) explicitly propose that recrystallisation/annealing of small deposited grains, tied to local ice temperature, may explain larger grains near the tiger stripes. This is a closely matching citation for "warm deposits near the tiger stripes coarsen" — usable as context (qualitative, no timescale).
