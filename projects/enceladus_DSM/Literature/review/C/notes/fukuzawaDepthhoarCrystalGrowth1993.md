# fukuzawaDepthhoarCrystalGrowth1993 — Fukuzawa & Akitaya, Ann. Glaciol. 18, 39–45 (1993)
DOI (PDF footer): 10.3189/S026030550001123X. Read in full (7 pp). Note: the bib key says "Depthhoar" and the PDF header says "Annals of Glaciology 18 1993".

## Summary
Field observations (Hokkaido, 50 days, winter 1989–90) of rapid depth-hoar growth within one or two nights in a thin low-density new-snow layer just below the surface under gradients >100 K/m; laboratory experiments on 80–100 kg/m³ snow at −16 °C under 100–300 K/m for ~50 h: mean crystal size grows linearly with time, rate increasing with gradient (~1–2.6e-9 m/s).

## Findings relevant to the manuscript
- Depth hoar forms weak layers relevant to slab avalanches: "A layer with relatively low strength plays an important role in a slab-avalanche release. The kinetic growth metamorphism produces a depth-hoar layer, which is typically weak because of thin bonds between particles" (p.1/39). Conclusion: "Avalanche danger will be high if the metamorphosed layer is covered by heavy snow in a later storm" (p.7/45).
- Mechanism: high temperature gradient and higher temperature increase vapor-density gradient and diffusivity (p.4/42).

## Claims supported / contradicted / qualified
- Draft L256 ("Depth hoar formation under strong temperature gradients creates a mechanically weak layer within snowpacks and drives slab avalanche initiation (Colbeck 1998; Fukuzawa 1993; Schweizer 2003)"): supported by Fukuzawa for "weak layer ... slab-avalanche release" (p.1/39).
