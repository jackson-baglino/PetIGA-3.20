# spitale_association_2007 — Spitale & Porco, Nature 449, 695–697 (2007)
DOI (PDF p.1): 10.1038/nature06217. Read in full (3 pp).

## Summary
Triangulates the sources of the most prominent Enceladus jets from Cassini ISS images over two years and compares them with CIRS hotspots and a shear-heating model.

## Findings relevant to the manuscript
- "We find that the jets emanate from the four tiger stripes, with the strongest sources on Baghdad and Damascus" (p.1/695); eight sources, "each on one of the four tiger stripes" (p.1/695).
- Plume vs jets: "'plumes' (that is, observed features, as opposed to the individual 'jets' of material that can compose a plume, depending on the viewing geometry)" (p.1/695).
- Hottest CIRS temperatures coincide with the tiger stripes (p.1/695); Table 1 CIRS hotspot temperatures 114–157 K (p.3/697; ± values garbled in text layer, not needed).
- Nothing on fallout, grain size or composition.

## Claims supported / contradicted / qualified
- Draft L259 / outline L312–314 "Cassini ... observed numerous jets converging into a plume, emitted from four fractures along the moon's south pole, known as the Tiger Stripes (Spitale 2007)": supported (p.1/695). The bib key is old style (`spitale_association_2007`).
