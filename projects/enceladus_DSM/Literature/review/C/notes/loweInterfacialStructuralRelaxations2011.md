# loweInterfacialStructuralRelaxations2011 — Löwe, Spiegel & Schneebeli, J. Glaciol. 57(203), 499–510 (2011)
DOI: 10.3189/002214311796905569. Read in full (12 pp; equations not needed).

## Summary
Sealed laboratory new snow (φi = 0.188) held isothermally at −3, −9, −19 °C for ~1 year, µCT, analysed with the anisotropic two-point correlation function; interfacial lengths (incl. 1/SSA) vs structural lengths (first zero crossing).

## Findings
- Coarsening theory: l ∼ t^{1/z}, z = 3 for diffusion-limited and z = 2 for kinetics-limited evaporation/condensation (LSW; Cahn–Hilliard/Allen–Cahn) (p.2–3). "The kinetics-limited case requires a small condensation coefficient" (p. ~9).
- At −3 °C, l_ssa and thickness grow with 1/z ≈ 0.33 (diffusion-limited, z = 3); at −9 and −19 °C lower exponents, interpreted as "surface diffusion with z = 4 at low temperatures", or transients (conclusions; abstract: "crossover between T = −9 and −19 °C from evaporation/condensation to surface diffusion").
- Dendritic structures persist a year at low T; SSA "only describes spatial correlations of snow on the smallest scales" (p. ~10).
- Densification observed (gap above sample grows) (p.3).

## Claims
- Draft L276 (DSM research list): supported as metamorphism study.
- Outline L1316–1320 tests (i) temperature superposition by a time shift, (iii) "SSA falls linearly in log time": qualified — in snow the SSA length follows a power law with a temperature-dependent exponent (z = 3 at −3 °C, ~4 at −9/−19 °C), so neither a pure time shift nor a universal log law is what snow shows. Present them as model predictions to test on ice spheres; cite Löwe for the power-law form ("forms only" is correct).
- Limitations (model has no surface diffusion): Löwe interpret low-T coarsening in snow as surface-diffusion dominated — the model's temperature collapse could fail in experiments for that reason. Worth one clause.
- Note z = 2 (kinetics-limited, small α) vs z = 3 (diffusion-limited): the paper's α_c = 10⁻³ aggregates sit in the kinetics-limited class.
