# gundlachSinteringSublimationMicrometresized2018 — Gundlach, Ratte, Blum, Oesert & Gorb, MNRAS 479, 5272–5287 (2018)
Read (16 pp; appendix runs skimmed).
## Summary
Cryo-SEM (10⁻⁶ mbar) observations of sintering and sublimation of micrometre-sized water-ice particles at 160–170 K; sinter model (vapor transport dominant at low T and pressure) applied to strength and network thermal conductivity of icy surfaces; extrapolated to comets, Enceladus (80 K), Europa (120 K).
## Findings
- "For water ice at low temperatures and pressures, the vapour transport process is the dominant material transport mechanism" (p.2).
- Network conductivity in vacuum λ ∝ r_neck^{1/2}; "The sintering process can increase the thermal conductivity by a factor of ∼4−8, depending on the particle size" (Sect. 4.3, Fig. 11).
- Enceladus: "the low temperature of ∼80 K prevents any sintering; i.e., the sinter time-scale is much longer than the age of the Solar System ... we conclude that Enceladus possesses a powder-like surface consisting of unsintered micrometre-sized particles" (Sect. 5); vacuum sublimation assumed.
## Claims
- Draft L259 "Much of Enceladus' surface experiences conditions that cause little to no sintering resulting in unconsolidated granular packings (Choukroun; Molaro)": supported directly by Gundlach (Sect. 5) — better citation than Molaro (whose 0.1 µm grains sinter in ~10³ yr).
- Draft L259 "changes in other bulk properties, namely thermal conductivity": supported (Sect. 4.3).
