# choukroun_strength_2020 — Choukroun, Molaro, Hodyss et al. (2020), GRL 47, e2020GL088953, doi:10.1029/2020GL088953 (printed)

**Summary.** Fine-grained crystalline ice (12 µm mean diameter, made by air atomization into liquid nitrogen) was poured loose (porosity 51.5 ± 1.6 %), sealed in containers, and held isothermal at 193, 223, 233 and 243 K, "at water vapor saturation pressure", for months up to 14 months. Cone-penetration resistance was measured repeatedly. The Arrhenius fit of strengthening rate is extrapolated to Enceladus and Europa.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- p.3: strength rises linearly in time at each T; 193 K reaches 2–3 MPa after ~400 d, and 243 K reaches 7–10 MPa in 60 d. Q = 24.3 ± 3.3 kJ/mol, "comparable to ... the ~23 kJ/mol activation energy of H2O self-diffusion on the surface of ice grains (Nasello et al., 2007)". It is "not consistent with ... the volume (~60 kJ/mol) or vapor (~51 kJ/mol) diffusion mechanisms", which suggests that strengthening "is primarily due to the evolution of a mesoscale network". The conclusion (p.8) says the rate "is consistent with a process dominated by self-diffusion of H2O molecules on the surface of ice grains."
- p.2: plume particle mean radius 3.1 ± 0.5 µm (Ingersoll & Ewald); radii 0.1–5 µm dominate deposits; deposition "averages on order of 1 μm/yr across the entire Enceladus surface but can be up to 1 mm/yr in locations close to jet sources". Snow is "a relatively poor analog" because of melt–refreeze.
- p.6: "no densification had yet occurred" (14 MPa, still rising linearly).
- p.7: "Under typical Enceladus surface conditions, little to no strengthening is expected." The south polar region (mean <50 K) needs "at least the age of the solar system" to reach ~1 MPa; equatorial noon (>80 K) needs "about 100 Myr". 1 MPa samples are "poorly consolidated and friable by hand".
- p.7: "Close to the Tiger Stripes, where temperatures up to 180 K have been estimated (Spencer & Nimmo, 2013), plume deposit materials would develop a cone penetration resistance around 10 MPa in ~15 years." Deposition near the stripes is "on order of 0.01 to 1 mm/yr (Kempf et al., 2010; Southworth et al., 2019)".
- p.6: Arrhenius extrapolation assumed to give "at least an upper bound"; airless, low-vapour conditions would redistribute ice less efficiently.

**Claims supported / qualified**
- Draft L259 (much of Enceladus' surface sees "little to no sintering resulting in unconsolidated granular packings"; Choukroun, Molaro): **partially**. Choukroun supports "little to no strengthening" (p.7), but its word is "poorly consolidated", and its quantity is strength rather than sintering. Molaro is contradicted (see the Molaro notes). Suggest: "little strengthening is expected over geologic time, leaving deposits poorly consolidated (Choukroun et al. 2020)".
- Draft L260 ("up to 180 K", citing Spencer 2013): the phrase "up to 180 K" is Choukroun's paraphrase of Spencer & Nimmo. Spencer & Nimmo say "at least 180 K", so the draft should follow the primary source (see the spencer2013 notes).
- Draft L260 ("warm enough to cause significant sintering ... well-consolidated"; Choukroun): **supported** (p.7: ~10 MPa in ~15 years, "very consolidated"), as an extrapolation by Choukroun.
- Draft L262 ("Other modes of diffusion tend to have limited effects or operate over planetary timescales"; Molaro, Choukroun): **contradicted** by Choukroun, who attributes the strengthening to surface self-diffusion (Q matches surface diffusion, not vapour) (p.3, p.8). The Intro must acknowledge this and cannot cite Choukroun for vapour dominance. Note: their containers were vapour-saturated, so a saturated-vapour lab experiment pointed to surface diffusion.
- Outline L355–359 and L1338–1340 (Choukroun fallout 0.01–1 mm/yr; south polar "below 50 K" takes the age of the solar system to reach 1 MPa; equatorial >80 K about 100 Myr; "poorly consolidated"): **supported** verbatim (p.7). The 0.01 lower bound is Choukroun's, citing Kempf/Southworth. Neither of those sources states it (my Kempf notes give 1e-5 at the equator and ≤0.5 mm/yr near the vents).
- Draft L264 ("strength evolution of ice plume deposit simulants studied experimentally"): **supported**.

**Candidate additions.** (1) In the Scope or Discussion: a lab study of saturated, isothermal 12 µm ice found an activation energy of 24.3 kJ/mol, which matches surface self-diffusion (Choukroun et al. 2020). This is a mechanism our model omits, and it should be named as a limitation together with Kingery 1960 and Löwe 2011. (2) Their strength rose linearly with no densification, a lab-observable bulk-property change in step with our k_eff rise; cite it as an analogy only.
