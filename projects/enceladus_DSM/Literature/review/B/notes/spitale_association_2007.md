# spitale_association_2007 — Spitale & Porco (2007), Nature 449, 695–697

**Summary.** Triangulation of plume-jet source locations from Cassini ISS narrow-angle images taken over two years from many viewing directions; comparison with CIRS hotspots and a shear-heating model. Eight sources found, each on one of the four tiger stripes.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- p.1 (journal 695) abstract: "We find that the jets emanate from the four tiger stripes, with the strongest sources on Baghdad and Damascus." "the hottest measured temperatures coinciding with the four ‘tiger stripe’ fractures, named Alexandria, Cairo, Baghdad and Damascus" (the naming/discovery of the plume itself is Porco et al. 2006, ref 1).
- p.1: "Our solution ... consists of eight sources, each on one of the four tiger stripes"; jets coincide with CIRS hotspots.
- Table 1 (p.3): CIRS temperatures of associated hotspots 114–157 K (e.g. "D 145±14", "(F) 157±24") — these are CIRS brightness temperatures at coarse resolution, not the 180 K trough temperatures.
- Spitale & Porco do not discuss plume grain size, composition, or deposition.

**Claims supported / qualified**
- Draft L259 / outline L311–314: "Cassini ... detected numerous jets converging into a plume from four fractures near the south pole, known as the Tiger Stripes (Spitale & Porco 2007)" — **supported** for "jets from the four tiger stripes" (p.1). "Converging into a plume" is described in Spencer & Nimmo 2013 p.8 citing Spitale & Porco; discovery of the plume is Porco et al. (2006, not in library). Acceptable as is; optionally add Spencer & Nimmo (2013) for the merged plume.
- "Tiger Stripes" naming: Spitale uses "tiger stripe" lowercase; fine.
