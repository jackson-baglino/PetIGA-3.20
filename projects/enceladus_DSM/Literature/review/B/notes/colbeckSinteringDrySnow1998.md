# colbeckSinteringDrySnow1998 — Colbeck (1998), J. Appl. Phys. 84(8), 4585–4589, doi:10.1063/1.368684 (printed on the cover page)

**Page convention.** PDF p.1 is the AIP cover; PDF p.N = journal p.(4583+N). The text layer encodes − as 2 and = as 5, so numbers were checked on rendered p.5.

**Summary.** Theory of bond growth between two spherical ice grains by **grain-boundary diffusion** driven by the stress gradient along a flat grain boundary. The dihedral (groove) angle grows towards its equilibrium value of ~145°. Supported by micrographs of bonds in fresh snow, in snow sintered two months at −3 °C, and in snow stored for years at −24 °F. Predicts Y/R ∝ t^0.25 and a time to a given neck ∝ R0⁴.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- p.2 (4585), abstract: "The basic shape of bonds in snow is dictated by the geometrical requirements of grain-boundary grooves and is not a simple concave neck as has long been assumed."
- p.5 (4588), rendered: "Kingery, who first applied this approach to ice grains, concluded that the bonding was due to surface diffusion. ... Kuroiwa concluded that volume diffusion was the dominant process, but Hobbs and Mason believed that sublimation, transfer through the vapor phase, had to be the dominant mechanism. The vapor transfer mechanism ... has since received wide acceptance".
- p.5: "various mechanisms may dominate under different conditions or during different stages of sintering. However, the classical concepts based on a concave neck cannot dominate after the grain boundary groove is established".
- p.5: Keeler found faster bond growth in natural snow than in the laboratory, "almost certainly due to the macroscopic temperature gradients"; "Hosler, Jensen, and Goldshak observed that humidity also affects the rate".
- Growth ∝ t^0.25 is "a little stronger than observed" (Kuroiwa's exponents).
- Depth hoar and avalanches are not mentioned anywhere in the paper (text search).

**Claims supported / qualified**
- Draft L256 (depth hoar under strong gradients creates a weak layer and drives slab avalanches; Colbeck 1998, Fukuzawa, Schweizer): **wrong-citation** for Colbeck 1998, which concerns bond growth between rounded grains and does not discuss depth hoar. Colbeck 1983 p.2 is the Colbeck paper that fits this sentence.
- Draft L262 (example of sintering work): **supported**. It is a sintering theory for snow. Its p.5 mechanism history is the closest library summary of the Kingery / Kuroiwa / Hobbs & Mason disagreement.
- Model scope: our two-phase model has no grain boundaries, so it has no groove or dihedral angle; necks are concave by construction. Colbeck argues that concave-neck theory cannot dominate once a groove forms. The Methods/limitations should state that grain boundaries (dihedral angle ~145°) are not represented.
- Outline (Molaro comparison / exponent discussion): Colbeck's mechanism history supports stating that the attribution of ice-sintering mechanisms was historically contested.

**Candidate additions.** One limitations sentence: "The model has no grain boundaries; real ice bonds carry a grain-boundary groove with an equilibrium dihedral angle near 145° (Colbeck 1998, p.5), which our concave necks do not represent."
