# taillandierRateDecreaseSpecific2007 — Taillandier et al. (2007), JGR 112, F03003

**Summary.** SSA (CH4 adsorption at 77 K, reproducibility 6 %, accuracy 12 %) of natural fresh snow followed for months: isothermal cold-room runs at −4, −10, −15 °C (data partly from Legagneux et al. 2003/2004), temperature-gradient boxes (33 °C/m, −6 to −14 °C) and Alaskan taiga snowpack (natural TG, and quasi-isothermal on tables). Densities < 0.18–0.28 g/cm³. Empirical logarithmic decay law with parameters set by SSA0 and mean temperature.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- Log-time law, p.2 (Eq. 1): "SSA(t) = B − A ln(t + Δt)"; p.10 [36]: valid ~0.5 to ~150 days for ET; "equations (1), (9) and (13) are not valid at long times, because SSA values must remain positive".
- p.12 [53]: "both under ET and TG conditions the SSA decay rate obeys equation (1)".
- Ostwald/LSW form, p.2 Eq. (2): SSA = SSA0 (τ/(t+τ))^{1/n}; Legagneux et al. 2004 showed Eq. (1) approximates (2).
- Temperature, p.11 [46]: "SSA remains lower at all evolution times when temperature is higher"; ET Tm range studied −19.2 to −4 °C (p.10 [40]).
- Density limitation p.11 [45]: equations recommended "only for low- to medium-density snows, <0.30 g cm−3"; dense windpack SSA decays much slower.
- ET/TG threshold between 9 and 20 °C/m (p.10 [38]).
- Fitted functional forms tested (p.6 [22]): log R²=0.94, exponential 0.72, power 0.90, linear 0.51.

**Claims supported / qualified**
- Outline L1035–1036 ("the trend is linear in log θ"), L1050 cite box (functional form only) and §5.4 test (iii) "SSA falls linearly in log time": **supported as a functional-form analogue** — isothermal natural snow SSA decays ∝ −A ln(t+Δt) (Eq. 1, p.2; p.12 [53]). Magnitudes do not carry over (fresh dendritic snow, SSA0 500–1000 cm²/g, density <0.3 g/cm³, vs 50 µm sintering spheres) — consistent with the paper's decision that "only functional forms carry over".
- Outline §5.2 "temperature rescales time": Taillandier show higher T → faster decay but parameterize with T-dependent A, B (not a pure time shift). Not a direct confirmation; do not cite as evidence of time-temperature superposition.

**Candidate additions.** Use Taillandier et al. 2007 Eq. (1) (with Legagneux et al. 2003/2004) as the precedent for "SSA linear in log time" under isothermal conditions, qualified to the density range < 0.3 g/cm³ (their p.11) — the aggregates here (φ = 0.275–0.475, i.e. ~480–665 kg/m³ ice-equivalent) are denser than their snows, so say "the same functional form" only.
