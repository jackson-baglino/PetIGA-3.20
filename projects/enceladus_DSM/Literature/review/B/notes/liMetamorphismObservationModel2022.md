# liMetamorphismObservationModel2022 — Li & Baker (2022), Hydrol. Process. 36, e14696, doi:10.1002/hyp.14696

**Summary.** Summit (Greenland) snow at 311–486 kg/m³ under ±160 K/m in a µCT cooling stage. Depth hoar forms and the snow de-densifies (density and structure thickness fall; SSA and porosity rise). An ice-sphere-pair vapour-transport model with a tuned geometric coefficient ψ reproduces the density change, with critical r_p = 0.1 mm and bond/particle ratio α = 0.1. Not cited.

**Findings relevant.** p.1: depth hoar's weak, porous structure affects "the local thermal distribution (Calonne et al., 2014; Hammonds et al., 2015)". The paper cites quasi-isothermal ice-sphere arrays (Chen 2013; Wang & Baker 2016) in which "neck areas increased while the radii of the ice spheres decreased with time".

**Relevance.** Temperature-gradient work, outside our scope. Their "α" is a bond/particle radius ratio, not the condensation coefficient, so beware the notation clash. No citation recommended.
