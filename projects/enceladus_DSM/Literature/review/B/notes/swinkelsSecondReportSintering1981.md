# swinkelsSecondReportSintering1981 — Swinkels & Ashby (1981), Acta Metall. 29, 259–281

**Summary.** Sintering-mechanism maps (neck size x/a or density vs homologous temperature T/T_M, with time contours) for wires and spheres, built from rate equations for six mechanisms; new coupling of grain-boundary diffusion with surface redistribution (curvatures K1, K2, lengths d1, d2), stage criteria and an approximate particle-rearrangement scheme. Tested against 17 datasets (Cu, Ag, Ni, Fe, W, stainless steel, NaCl). No ice.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- Mechanisms, p.1–2 (journal 259–260), Fig. 1 caption and Table 1: 1 surface diffusion, 2 lattice diffusion (surface source), 3 vapour transport (surface source) are "Non-Densifying"; 4 boundary, 5 lattice (boundary source), 6 lattice (dislocations) are "Densifying". Fig. 1 caption: "All lead to neck-growth. Only mechanisms 4, 5 and 6 cause densification."
- Rate equations (Table 2, p.4) include vapour transport V3 = π x² ρ? P_v (γΩ/kT)(Ω/2πmkT)^{1/2}(K3−Km) form (garbled in text; Molaro Eq. used in outline Appendix A follows this), coupling K1K2 = Km² (Table 2 note 6) — matches outline Appendix A relation "K1K2 = Km²" and d2 relation (note 5).
- Kingery & Berg NaCl spheres (a = 60 µm), p.20–21: "The data, which lies entirely in the vapour transport field, agree well ... vapour transport is dominant" — vapor transport is a classical, observed sintering route in other materials.
- Accuracy target, p.3: "We aim here at equations which predict the rates ... to within a factor of 2".
- Reference list gives Kuczynski G. C., Trans. Metall. Soc. AIME 185, 169 (1949) and Kingery W. D. & Berg M., J. Appl. Phys. 26, 1205 (1955) (p.23) — bibliographic details for the missing classical sources (no DOI here).
