# demmenieIceSinteringSublimation2025 — Demmenie, Woutersen & Bonn (2025), J. Phys. Chem. Lett. 16, 2104–2109, doi:10.1021/acs.jpclett.5c00050 (printed)

**Page convention.** PDF p.N = journal p.(2103+N).

**Summary.** Neck growth between two ice spheres about 1 mm in diameter, frozen from droplets, at **−3 °C** in a humidity-controlled box. The water vapour pressure is set to ice saturation using Murphy & Koop 2005, and the spheres touch horizontally with no external pressure. Four runs over ~2.5 h are imaged from above. Separately, the healing of a ~4 µm-deep, ~280 µm-long scratch is compared against Mullins' four mechanism models. Experiments were at one temperature only.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- p.2 (2105) Table 1 and Eq. 1–2, rendered: (r/R0)^m = C_n(T)·t/R0^n with m = 2n − 1, so r ∝ t^α with α = 1/(2n−1). Viscous flow n=1, m=1 (linear; "should be regarded as 1, not 2"). **Sublimation and condensation n=2, m=3 (t^1/3).** Bulk diffusion n=3, m=5 (1/5). **Surface diffusion n=4, m=7 (1/7).** The sublimation–condensation prefactor C2 = 3P0γsvΩ/(√(2πm)(kT)^{3/2}) is a Hertz–Knudsen (attachment/emission-limited) rate with no gas diffusion and an implicit α = 1.
- p.3 (2106) Fig. 3: "values of exponent α of 0.29 ± 0.01, 0.33 ± 0.01, 0.30 ± 0.01, and 0.26 ± 0.01" at vapour-pressure equilibrium, from fits r ∝ (t − t0)^α where "t0 represents an experimental delay between the initial contact of the spheres and the first image". Errors come from shifting t0 by one frame (±15 s). The spheres start from point contact, with no pre-made neck.
- p.3: "When we deliberately set the humidity too low and let the ice sublimate within 2.5 h, the competition between neck growth and sublimation resulted in an exponent of ∼1/7 (see Figure S2)."
- p.2: Kingery's ~1/7 "resulted from an underestimation of the relative humidity" (their interpretation).
- p.1: reported ice exponents historically range "between 1/3 and 1/7"; confusion "has at least partly resulted from erroneous comparisons with the sintering of snow" (snow densifies under gravity).
- p.4 (2107): "Maeno and Ebinuma predicted that vapor phase mass transport would dominate ice sintering ... An exception was identified for neck sizes that were <8% of the initial sphere diameter (r/R0 < 0.08), where surface diffusion becomes predominant within certain temperature ranges."
- p.4: Chen et al. (2013) protrusions are attributed possibly to a temperature gradient; Demmenie's equal-temperature spheres show none.

**Claims supported / qualified**
- Outline L950 ("Demmenie et al. measure 0.26–0.33 at saturation and about 1/7 when undersaturated"): **supported**. Add the conditions (−3 °C, ~1 mm spheres, in air, from point contact). Our saturated comparison run is at −20 °C with 50 µm grains, so this is a comparison of trend, not a matched experiment.
- Outline L876–877 (they measured, rather than only "theorized"): **supported** (p.3 Fig. 3).
- Outline L877, L1167 (the t^1/3 law is for sublimation and desublimation limited by attachment): **supported**. Their n=2 derivation uses the emission rate ∝ vapour pressure (Kelvin + kinetic gas theory), and C2 is Hertz–Knudsen (p.2).
- Outline L1169–1170 (who reports 1/7 undersaturated): **supported**, Demmenie p.3 with Fig. S2. The supplementary figure is not in the library, but the main text states it.
- Outline L1185 ("surface diffusion alone gives t^1/7"): **supported** by Demmenie Table 1 (p.2), consistent with Kingery 1960.
- Outline L395–404 (classical exponents 1/3, 1/5, 1/7 for evaporation–condensation, volume diffusion, surface diffusion): **supported** by Demmenie Table 1 as a secondary source. For the primary source use Kingery 1960 (in the library), since Kuczynski 1949 and Kingery & Berg 1955 are missing.
- Draft L262 (vapour diffusion dominates; Molaro, Choukroun): Demmenie is a closer citation for vapour-phase transport dominating ice sintering at saturation (p.4 conclusion; "strong evidence that sintering of ice spheres, while placed in vapor pressure equilibrium, is driven by local sublimation and condensation", p.5). Caveat to keep: Maeno & Ebinuma's exception for small necks, r/R0 < 0.08.
- Wording: Demmenie's process is sublimation–condensation (Hertz–Knudsen), not "vapor diffusion", so the draft's "vapor diffusion" at L262 should read "vapor transport (sublimation and deposition)".

**Candidate additions.**
- Intro (outline item on Demmenie, L417–421): "Demmenie et al. (2025) grew necks between ~1 mm ice spheres at −3 °C under controlled humidity and measured exponents 0.26–0.33 at ice saturation, consistent with sublimation–condensation (t^1/3), and ~1/7 when the ice was deliberately undersaturated."
- Discussion: their fits start at contact with t0 equal to the imaging delay. Our pre-necked runs need an assumed growth history, which explains why a fitted exponent depends on t0 (outline L956–971).
