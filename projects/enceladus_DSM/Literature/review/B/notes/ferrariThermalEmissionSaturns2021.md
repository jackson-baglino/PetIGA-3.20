# ferrariThermalEmissionSaturns2021 — Ferrari, Lucas & Jacquemoud (2021), A&A 655, A8 (11 pp), doi:10.1051/0004-6361/202141223 (printed on p.1)

**Summary.** 1-D heat-diffusion thermophysical model of icy regolith on Saturn's moons, coupled to Dione shape models (ellipsoid and topography at 8 km and 1 km). Effective conductivity is either constant (k_E0) or radiative plus contact, following Ferrari & Lucas 2016. Albedo and emissivity come from Hapke and Mie theory. The model is compared with one Cassini CIRS FP1 scan of Dione's leading hemisphere (~80–95 K). Porosity 0.3–0.99, grain size 0.1 µm–5 cm.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- p.2: "the mechanism of grain sintering and the reason for thermal inertia increases in the icy regolith remain poorly understood (Ferrari & Lucas 2016; Schaible et al. 2017)." This is the lens-shaped thermal-inertia anomaly linked to MeV electrons on Mimas, Tethys and Dione.
- p.2: CIRS thermal inertias "were found to be very weak, around 10–20 J m−2 K−1 s−1/2"; "High porosity of the regolith is generally cited to explain such a low thermal inertia."
- p.3: per Ferrari & Lucas 2016, inertia 10–20 MKS at 80 ± 30 K is possible with normal porosity (p = 0.3–0.8) given "loose grain contact or amorphous ice phase".
- p.6: "A constant thermal conductivity kE0 = 10−3 W m−1 K−1 yields similar thermal inertia" to Dione's ~10 MKS.
- p.9: grain-size variation "which may originate from sintering after bombardment by MeV electrons in the lens-shaped region".
- Enceladus appears only through albedo (p.5: A close to unity "as suspected on the Enceladus trailing hemisphere").

**Claims supported / qualified**
- Outline L355–359 (Byrne's conductivity range 0.001–0.1 W/m/K from laboratory (Seiferlin) and thermophysical (Ferrari) values): **supported** for the low end. Ferrari uses k_E0 = 0.001 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹ (p.6, Figs. 4, 7–9). The 0.025 value would come from Seiferlin, which is missing.
- Outline L1343 ("possibly Ferrari 2021 ... check relevance"): **relevant but indirect**. It concerns Dione rather than Enceladus, is airless at ~80 K, and uses no sintering model. It does state that sintering, grain contacts and thermal inertia on Saturn's icy moons are poorly understood, which is a closely matching sentence for the motivation that inter-grain contact quality controls regolith conductivity. Citing it for any Enceladus conductivity value would be a stretch.

**Candidate additions.** In the Implications paragraph: "On Saturn's icy moons, low thermal inertias (10–20 J m⁻² K⁻¹ s⁻¹ᐟ²) are attributed to porosity and loose grain contacts, and how sintering raises thermal inertia remains poorly understood (Ferrari et al. 2021, p.2)." This uses no timescale, so it fits the no-extrapolation framing.
