# piqueuxModelThermalConductivity2009a — Piqueux & Christensen (2009a), "A model of thermal conductivity for planetary soils: 1. Theory for unconsolidated soils", JGR 114, E09005, doi:10.1029/2008JE003308 (printed)

**Summary.** Numerical conduction in cubic cells of touching spheres (fused-silica properties) with pore-gas conductivity set by pressure and grain size through Knudsen-number regimes (free-molecular, transitional, slip). Radiation and contact conduction are estimated separately. Compared with Presley & Christensen's laboratory data.

**Findings relevant**
- p.8 [31–32]: grains are nearly isothermal; "most of the heat flows near the contact of the grains", and Fig. 7 states "The heat flows mostly through the gas located in the intergrain region". p.8: "the contact conductivity of submillimeter spheres is negligible in the presence of a gas".
- Conclusions p.17 [67]: k is "independent of the grain size and pressure at high pressures and/or for large grain sizes (slip flow regime) ... the domain encountered on Venus, Earth, and Titan." [71]: higher porosity gives lower k.
- p.2: thermal inertia is controlled mainly by k, because ρ and c vary only by factors of 2–4.

**Relevance.** It supports our use of bulk k_air at 1 atm (the slip/continuum regime; at 50 µm grains the pore gas is not Knudsen-limited). The point that heat crosses through the gas near contacts in unbonded packings matches Calonne 2011's finding that air conduction matters. It is a possible citation for the limitations sentence "pores air-filled at 1 atm; at low pressure (airless bodies) gas conduction vanishes and contacts dominate". This paper together with Part 2 is the planetary-regolith counterpart of the snow literature. Optional.
