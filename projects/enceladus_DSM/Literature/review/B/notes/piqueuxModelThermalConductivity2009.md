# piqueuxModelThermalConductivity2009 — Piqueux & Christensen (2009b), "A model of thermal conductivity for planetary soils: 2. Theory for cemented soils", JGR 114, E09006, doi:10.1029/2008JE003309 (printed)

**Summary.** Numerical steady conduction through cubic cells of spherical grains joined by pendular "cement" rings at the contacts, with tunable bond volume fraction, cement conductivity and pore-gas conductivity. Porosity is held at 33 % in most runs. Applied to indurated Martian surfaces (duricrust).

**Findings relevant**
- Abstract p.1: "Cement fractions <0.001–0.01% in volume have small effects on the soil bulk thermal conductivity. A significant conductivity increase (factor 3–8) is observed for bond fractions of 0.01 to 1% in volume. In the 1 to 15% bond fraction domain, the conductivity increases continuously but less intensely".
- p.5 [§3]: "intergrain contacts act as bottlenecks where heat can hardly flow from one grain to the next ... cements located between grains significantly increase the cross-sectional area of the contact between the grains, even with very small volumes of cements, resulting in a reduction of the intergrain thermal resistivity."
- p.9 [35]: with cement present, bulk k is nearly independent of gas conductivity over 0.0001–0.1 W/m/K.
- Conclusions p.10 [49–51]: the increase "is largely independent of the cement conductivity" for small bond fractions.

**Relevance (important).** This is the closest planetary precedent for the paper's mechanism, outside snow: enlarging inter-grain contacts at nearly fixed porosity raises bulk conductivity strongly, because contacts are the bottlenecks. It is directly applicable to the Intro and Discussion ("bonds control conduction in granular media"), and it matches the outline's planetary framing without extrapolating timescales. Caveats: the bonds are a foreign cement on regular cubic packings, not sintered ice necks, and the setting is Mars at low gas pressure. Pair it with Calonne 2019 p.5 ("the few bonds between ice grains ... act as heat paths").
