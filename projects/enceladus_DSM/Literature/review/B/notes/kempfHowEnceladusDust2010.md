# kempfHowEnceladusDust2010 — Kempf, Beckmann & Schmidt (2010), Icarus 206, 446–457

**Summary.** Dynamical simulations (gravity, Lorentz force, self-consistent grain charging) of plume grains launched from the south-polar jet sources; fraction escaping to the E ring, vertical ring structure compared with CDA data, and surface deposition maps of returning grains (0.5–5 µm).

**Findings relevant to the manuscript** (numbers checked on rendered PDF p.10, journal p.455)
- Deposition, p.10: "The deposition rate ranges between 0.5 mm per year in the immediate vicinity of the jet sources in the tiger stripe region and about 10⁻⁵ mm per year at the equator ... The majority of jet particle impacts ... happens close to the vents within the south pole terrain. Here, the growth rate of the regolith layer can be as high as 0.5 mm per year." Fig. 8b colour scale 10⁻⁵ to 1 mm/yr.
- Most grains > 1 µm fall back (p.8: "Most of the ejected grains >1 µm fall back onto the moon’s surface").
- Grain sizes, p.10: "the largest jet particles (Hedman et al., 2009) of about 5 µm are much smaller than the 75 µm grains inferred by Jaumann et al. (2008)"; "Alternatively, there may be other physical processes, taking place on the surface, that determine the grain sizes in the south polar terrain. This could be re-crystallisation and compactification of small grains, possibly related to the local temperature of the ice (annealing), which might explain the decrease of grain sizes with distance from the tiger stripes."
- Boulder burial times: 10⁵–10⁶ yr at 100 m from a source (p.10).

**Claims supported / qualified**
- Outline L337–339 ("regions of heaviest fallout, 0.01–1 mm yr⁻¹ (Kempf et al., 2010; Southworth et al., 2019)"): **partially** — Kempf give up to 0.5 mm/yr near the vents (≈10⁻⁵ mm/yr at the equator); Southworth's map tops at ~1 mm/yr. A range "up to ~0.5–1 mm yr⁻¹ near the tiger stripes" is what the two sources state; the lower bound 0.01 is not stated by either (check Choukroun, who is said to quote it).
- Outline 3b (deposited grains form a granular layer; burial competes with sintering): **supported** (regolith growth up to 0.5 mm/yr, p.10).
- **Closely matching support for §5.5:** Kempf et al. propose that recrystallisation/compaction ("annealing") of small grains, temperature-dependent, may explain grain size decreasing away from the tiger stripes (p.10). With Spencer et al. (2009) and Jaumann et al. (2008) this is the published suggestion that warm deposits near the stripes coarsen/sinter.

**Candidate additions.** §5.5: "Kempf et al. (2010) noted that the coarse grains near the tiger stripes (Jaumann et al., 2008) are larger than any jet particle and suggested temperature-dependent recrystallisation of the deposit." Correct the fallout range to "up to 0.5 mm yr⁻¹ (Kempf et al., 2010)".
