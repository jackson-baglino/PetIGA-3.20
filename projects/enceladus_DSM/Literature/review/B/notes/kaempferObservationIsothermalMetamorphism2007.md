# kaempferObservationIsothermalMetamorphism2007 — Kaempfer & Schneebeli (2007), JGR 112, D24101

**Summary.** Four sealed samples of the same sieved fresh snow held isothermally at −1.6, −8.3, −19.1 and −54 °C for ~1 year (signs dropped in the text layer; confirmed by CHECK_COLLECTION against page 1); µCT (10 µm voxels) about monthly; density (weighing and CT), SSA, SMI, trabecular thickness/number. Interpreted with sintering and Ostwald-ripening laws.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- Abstract p.1: "An increase in density and trabecular thickness together with a decrease of the specific surface area ... were observed throughout the experiments ... The continuous densification implies that volume processes like grain boundary diffusion have to occur ... The logarithmic decrease of the specific surface area is an indication that the coarsening is rate limiting." At −54 °C "the specific surface area decreased by only 19%".
- Exponent classification (p.2 [5]): "values of n = 7 and m = 4 would indicate surface diffusion, n = 5 and m = 3 volume diffusion, and n = 3 and m = 2 ... evaporation-condensation" (X^n ∝ D^m t, German 1996).
- History (p.2 [6]): Kingery (1960) → surface diffusion; Kuroiwa (1961) → volume diffusion; Hobbs & Mason → vapor; "The vapor transfer mechanism has since received wide acceptance." Colbeck (1998, 2001): grain-boundary groove geometry, grain-boundary diffusion.
- Fits: trabecular thickness growth exponents n = 2.85, 3.06, 2.10; SSA law (Legagneux form) n = 3.00, 3.55, 2.20 for samples 1–3 (R² > 0.99) (p.5–7).
- Densification is not from overburden (65 mm samples), p.7 [32]: "There has thus to occur a mechanism during the isothermal sintering which leads to densification ... this has to be a volume process"; density and ice thickness related linearly with a temperature-independent slope.
- Two stages: rapid rounding in the first month, then structural parameters stable except coarsening (p.7).
- REV respected: 6×6×2 mm region (p.3).

**Claims supported / qualified**
- Outline L1320 (isothermal SSA decay in the laboratory, functional form only): **supported** (log decrease, R² > 0.99).
- **Qualifier for outline §2.1 "Scope" (L645–653)**: the outline will say vapor-transport sintering does not densify, so porosity is constant. Kaempfer & Schneebeli observed continuous densification of isothermal snow without load and attributed it to volume processes — so the paper should say that real isothermal snow does densify slowly and the model omits that route (not that sintering in general leaves porosity unchanged). Consistent with Swinkels & Ashby Table 1.
- Draft L276 "densification, especially in the presence of overburden pressures": Kaempfer & Schneebeli show densification also occurs isothermally without overburden — "especially" is fine, but add that it occurs without load too.
- §5.1 surface diffusion debate: K&S list Kingery 1960's surface-diffusion interpretation and note the vapor mechanism's "wide acceptance" — the historical disagreement is real and should be acknowledged.

**Candidate additions.** In §2.1 Scope: "In unloaded isothermal snow, density increases slowly, which requires volume or grain-boundary transport (Kaempfer & Schneebeli 2007); the model omits these, so its porosity is fixed."
