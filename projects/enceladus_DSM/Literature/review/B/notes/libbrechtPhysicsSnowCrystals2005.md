# libbrechtPhysicsSnowCrystals2005 — Libbrecht (2005), Rep. Prog. Phys. 68, 855–895, doi:10.1088/0034-4885/68/4/R03 (printed; PDF p.N = journal p.(854+N))

**Summary.** Review of snow-crystal growth from vapour: the Hertz–Knudsen relation with condensation coefficient α, coupled particle and heat diffusion, attachment kinetics on facets (nucleation-limited) versus rough surfaces, growth instabilities, dendrites, and the morphology diagram. Not cited in the manuscript, but Braun 2024 cites it for α.

**Findings relevant**
- p.3 (857): "The parameter α is known as the condensation coefficient, and it embodies the surface physics that governs how water molecules are incorporated into the ice lattice".
- p.22 (876): "it is nearly impossible to make an accurate measurement of α when α_diff < α"; diffusion corrections have limited precision.
- p.23 (877): "Nonfaceted ice surfaces can have α ≈ 1". p.24 (878): "The measurements roughly indicate α < 1 on faceted surfaces and α ≈ 1 on rough surfaces, at least to the limited accuracy of the most reliable data".
- p.10 (864): for an isolated crystal, curvature-driven (Gibbs–Thomson) evolution "plays only a minor role when considering the growth of snow crystals" at atmospheric supersaturations.

**Claims supported / qualified**
- Outline α_c paragraph and draft L501: Libbrecht 2005 supports that α is uncertain and hard to measure (p.22), and that it is ≈1 on rough (rounded) surfaces and ≪1 on facets (p.24). **Implication:** our rounded grains and the small α_c = 10⁻³ are consistent only if α_c is read as an effective, surface-averaged coefficient fitted to SSA or neck data (Bouvet 2022, Braun 2024), not a microscopic sticking probability. The manuscript should say so explicitly. Libbrecht 2005 together with Libbrecht & Rickerby 2013 is the right background.
- It is the source Braun 2024 cites for the Hertz–Knudsen law, so it could be cited for the attachment-kinetics equation in Methods.

**Candidate additions.** In the α_c paragraph: "α ≈ 1 has been suggested for rough ice surfaces and α ≪ 1 for facets (Libbrecht 2005), so the low α_c fitted to snow SSA (Bouvet et al. 2022) is an effective value".
