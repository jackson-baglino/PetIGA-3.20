# sommerfeldClassificationSnowMetamorphism1970 — Sommerfeld & LaChapelle (1970), J. Glaciol. 9(55), 3–17

**Summary.** A process-based classification of snow on the ground into four groups: mechanical damage at deposition; equi-temperature metamorphism (vapour transport driven by surface energy at constant T); temperature-gradient metamorphism (vapour along a thermal gradient, giving depth hoar); and firnification (melt–freeze and pressure). Includes definitions of grain versus crystal. Not cited in the manuscript.

**Findings relevant**
- p.3: "Under equi-temperature conditions, the water molecules move, largely by vapor diffusion (Hobbs and Mason, 1964; Hobbs and Radke, 1967), to new positions to decrease the surface free energy."
- p.3: "both time and temperature are major factors in determining the stage of metamorphism. If the snow is very cold, it will change very slowly".
- p.4: under equi-temperature metamorphism "the process continually slows down"; there is a practical grain-size limit (~1 mm) in seasonal snow.
- p.4: in TG metamorphism, "The thermal conductivity of air is much lower than that of ice, so the thermal gradient is steeper in the pore spaces".

**Relevance.** It is the origin of the terms equi-temperature and temperature-gradient metamorphism, which the manuscript uses. It could be cited where the Intro first distinguishes isothermal from gradient metamorphism, and p.3 is a further (second-hand) source for vapour diffusion in isothermal metamorphism. Optional.
