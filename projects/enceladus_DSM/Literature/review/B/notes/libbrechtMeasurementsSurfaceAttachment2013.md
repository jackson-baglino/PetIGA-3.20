# libbrechtMeasurementsSurfaceAttachment2013 — Libbrecht & Rickerby (2013), J. Cryst. Growth 377, 1–8 (corrected PDF, per coordinator addendum)

**Summary.** Growth of small (<50 µm) faceted ice prisms resting on a sapphire substrate, 1 mm below an ice reservoir, at reduced pressure (p < 0.025 bar), −40 < T < −2 °C; thickness by optical/laser interferometry; >200 crystals; only facets parallel to the substrate used. Attachment coefficients fitted to the polynucleation form α = A exp(−σ0/σsurf) for basal and prism facets; step energies derived.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- p.1 abstract: "measurements of the growth rates of faceted ice crystals in the temperature range −40 < T < −2 °C, from which we infer the surface attachment coefficients for the two principal facets" (minus signs/units confirmed in the text of this replaced file).
- Definition p.1: "v(T, σsurf) = α(T, σsurf) vkin(T) σsurf ... we must have α ≤ 1".
- Conditions p.4: "the pressure in the inner chamber was reduced so typically p < 0.025 during growth observations"; "crystal sizes typically < 50 µm".
- Diffusion, p.2 Eq. (1): σsurf = αdiff/(α + αdiff) σ∞ with "αdiff ≈ 0.15/(p R_µm)"; "In the limit α ≪ αdiff ... growth is primarily limited by attachment kinetics."
- Results p.5–7: "All our growth measurements were well described using the functional form α(σsurf) = A exp(−σ0/σsurf)"; A = 1 for all basal data and prism at T ≤ −10 °C; prism above −10 °C requires A < 1 (e.g. "A = 0.085 and σ0 = 0.03%" at −3 °C, Fig. 7b). Example σ0 values: basal −12 °C ⟨σ0⟩ = 1.95 ± 0.15 %; basal −5.15 °C ⟨σ0⟩ = 0.70 ± 0.10 %; prism −8.3 °C ⟨σ0⟩ = 1.4 ± 0.3 %.
- γ ≈ 0.11 J/m² for the ice/vapor surface energy (p.6).
- Crystal-to-crystal variability is the main limitation (p.5).

**Claims supported / qualified**
- Outline L426–433 (correct citation for the measurements; reduced pressure < 0.025 atm; −40 to −2 °C; both basal and prism facets; α = A exp(−σ0/σsurf)): **supported** (p.1, p.4, p.5). Note: the paper writes p < 0.025 in units of bar (p = P/Patm, Patm = 1 bar, p.2), i.e. ≈0.025 atm.
- Draft L273 "near vacuum ... basal plane": the paper uses reduced pressure (not vacuum) and both facets — correction stands.
- Draft L273 "α_c ∈ [10⁻³, 10⁻¹]": not stated in this paper. With A = 1 and σ0 ≈ 0.7–2 %, α = exp(−σ0/σ) spans many decades depending on σ; at small σ (as in sintering, ~10⁻⁵–10⁻³) α would be extremely small for faceted surfaces. This is an important caveat: the measured faceted-surface α at the supersaturations of the simulations would be ≪ 10⁻³; the manuscript's α_c is an effective (rough-surface/averaged) value — supports treating it as effective (outline §5.1 ¶3).
- Outline L444–448 (α depends on σ as well as T): **supported**.

**Candidate additions.** In §2.2/§5.1 note that for faceted surfaces α = exp(−σ0/σ) with σ0 ≈ 1 % (Libbrecht & Rickerby 2013), so at the 10⁻⁴–10⁻⁵ supersaturations in these runs a faceted surface would barely grow; rounded sintering grains are assumed to behave as rough surfaces with an effective constant α_c. This makes explicit why α_c is effective.
