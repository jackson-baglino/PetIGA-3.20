# grangerOrientationSelectiveGrain2021 — Granger, Flin, Ludwig, Hammad & Geindreau (2021), The Cryosphere 15, 4381–4398

**Summary.** In situ time-lapse diffraction contrast tomography (DCT) at ESRF of a 4 mm rounded-grain snow sample (476 kg/m³) in the CellDyM2 cryogenic cell under vacuum (0.009 Pa around the sample holder), temperature gradient 17 then 52 °C/m at mean −2.1 and −4.1 °C for ~3 days; ~900 grain orientations tracked; local sublimation/deposition fluxes vs c-axis and surface normal orientation.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- Abstract: "Faceted crystals appear during the evolution, and ... we observe higher sublimation–deposition rates for grains with their c axis in the horizontal plane at the beginning of the metamorphism."
- Density constant (476 ± 5 % kg/m³, Fig. 9) while SSA first decreases slightly then rises to ~20 m²/kg (Fig. 11) under gradient.
- Discussion: "surfaces with α = 45°, which have high index orientations, are rougher at the molecular level, they have higher deposition coefficient and are more active"; prismatic faces more kinetically active above −4 °C.
- REV ~2.34 mm for density and SSA (Fig. 8, 10).
- Intro: "An important aspect of temperature gradient metamorphism is that the grain size increases while the density remains constant (Marbouty 1980; Schneebeli and Sokratov 2004; ...)."

**Claims supported / qualified**
- Outline L1220 cite box ("Granger et al. 2021 for faceting under a gradient"): **supported** — facets form and the effective attachment rate depends on crystal orientation/surface roughness (orientation-dependent α), which supports treating a single α_c as an effective average. It is under a temperature gradient, not isothermal; use only as evidence that α varies with surface orientation.
- Outline L852–853 REV: Granger's REV ≈ 2.3 mm for density/SSA in 476 kg/m³ snow is consistent with the 2 mm domain claim (3-D snow; analogy only).

**Candidate additions.** None beyond the cite-box use.
