# jaumannDistributionIcyParticles2008 — Jaumann et al. (2008), Icarus 193, 407–419

**Summary.** Cassini VIMS band depths of water-ice absorptions (1.04, 1.25, 1.5, 2.0 µm) during three 2005 flybys compared with model spectra (2 µm–1 mm) to map ice particle diameters across Enceladus; correlation with geologic units and crater ages; ~30 % accuracy.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- Particle diameters by unit (text p.~5): "heavily cratered terrains (hct) exhibit a mean particle size of 15 ± 5 µm ... fractured and ridged terrain (frt) peak at a size of about 30 ± 10 µm ... tectonically deformed regions (tdr) exhibit particles ranging from 40 µm to about 120 µm with the largest ones located close to the center of the south polar tiger stripes." Tiger stripes inner zone: "definitely larger than 100 µm and even reach sizes >150 µm".
- Note these are diameters (manuscript grains are 50 µm *radius*, i.e. 100 µm diameter).
- Interpretation: "As large icy particles are heavy, they tend to be concentrated closer to the vent. Another indication ... is temperature. Higher temperatures support the growth of large particles (Clark et al., 1983) and indeed CIRS measurements across the tiger stripes indicate the highest temperatures ... exactly in those areas where VIMS identifies the largest ice particles (Fig. 12)." Grain size correlates with surface age.
- Comminution by impacts/sputtering on older terrain (weathering).

**Claims supported / qualified**
- Outline §5.5 L1330–1331 ("grain size increasing toward the fractures in VIMS data (Jaumann)"): **supported** — and Jaumann explicitly link the largest grains to the warmest areas (growth favored by temperature), alongside preferential fallout. This is a closely matching citation for "sintering/coarsening most advanced near the tiger stripes", stated as one hypothesis.
- Useful scale note: the simulated 50 µm-radius grains (100 µm diameter) are comparable to tiger-stripe grain sizes (60–150 µm diameter), though far coarser than plume particles (µm) — supports framing that the laboratory-scale grains are not plume-grain sized.

**Candidate additions.** §5.5: "VIMS grain diameters rise from 15 µm on cratered plains to >100 µm in the tiger stripes, where temperatures are highest (Jaumann et al., 2008)" — qualitative, no timescales.
