# wangEvolutionSpecificSurface2014 — Wang & Baker (2014), JGR Atmospheres 119, 13,690–13,703, doi:10.1002/2014JD022131

**Summary.** In-situ µCT (15 µm voxels) of low-density snow (100–220 kg/m³: fresh PP, 6-month-sintered RGsr, 1-year RGlr) under 45–533 K/m for 48 h at a mean of −4 °C (minus sign dropped in the text layer). SSA is per unit ice volume (mm⁻¹). Includes SEM of depth-hoar forms and steady FE heat and vapour fluxes. The paper is not cited in the manuscript.

**Findings relevant**
- p.1: TGM-faceted grains "do not sinter as quickly as the well-rounded grains created by isothermal metamorphism [Colbeck, 1983]. Therefore, the snow layer ... will lose its strength and transform into a weak layer".
- p.5–7: PP shows logarithmic SSA decrease. Pre-sintered rounded snow shows **SSA increases** under strong gradients (complex depth-hoar surfaces); RGsr increases for TG > 320–430 K/m.
- p.2: "The decrease of SSA with time can be fitted logarithmically and explained using Ostwald ripening theory" (isothermal).

**Relevance.** Supports draft L256 (weak layers) as a secondary source. It shows that SSA is not monotonic under strong gradients, which reinforces that an SSA-based description of k_eff is specific to isothermal sintering. It also gives a precedent for reporting SSA per unit ice volume. No changes needed; optional citation only.
