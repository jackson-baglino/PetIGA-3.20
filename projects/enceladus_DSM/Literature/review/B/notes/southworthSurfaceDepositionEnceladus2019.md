# southworthSurfaceDepositionEnceladus2019 — Southworth, Kempf & Spitale (2019), Icarus 319, 33–42

**Summary.** Numerical deep-source plume model (Schmidt et al. 2008 speed distribution), millions of particle trajectories (0.6–15 µm, M+ = 25 kg/s, size slope α = 3.1) for the 8 Spitale & Porco jets, ~100 Porco et al. (2014) jets and a curtain model; surface impact flux and deposition-rate maps (mm/yr); comparison with IR/UV colour maps to constrain jet zenith angles.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- Deposition maps, PDF p.4 (journal 36), Fig. 1 colour bar "Ice Deposition (mm/yr)" spans 10^−6 to 1; brightest values (approaching 0.1–1 mm/yr) lie along the tiger stripes; Fig. 2: large grains (6–15 µm) deposit close to the stripes. No numerical range is stated in the text.
- Deposition is a lower bound, p.3 (35): "our simulated deposition depth assumes perfect compaction ... if particles deposit with some porosity ψ ... scaling results presented here by 1/(1−ψ) ... deposition is almost certainly greater than presented here."
- p.3: "most particles larger than 1 µm do not escape Enceladus' gravity"; "total ejected mass and the total redeposition mass are dominated by large particles".
- p.1: plume is "the dominant source of the resurfacing of Enceladus (Jaumann et al., 2009; Kempf et al., 2010)".

**Claims supported / qualified**
- Outline L337–340 ("heaviest fallout, 0.01–1 mm yr−1 (Kempf et al., 2010; Southworth et al., 2019)"): **partially** — Southworth's Fig. 1 colour scale tops at 1 mm/yr near the stripes, consistent with the upper end; the 0.01–1 range is not stated numerically in the text (it is Choukroun's summary). Add that the rates assume zero porosity, so a porous deposit thickens faster (p.3). Suggest wording "up to ~0.1–1 mm yr−1 of ice-equivalent near the tiger stripes".
- "the same places get the most fallout" (outline L326) — supported (Fig. 1, 2, 5: maxima along the fractures).

**Candidate additions.** Note the zero-porosity (ice-equivalent) assumption when quoting deposition rates; with deposit porosity ψ the burial rate is 1/(1−ψ) larger (p.3), which strengthens the "burial competes with sintering" point.
