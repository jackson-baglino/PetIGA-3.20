# prokschDensitySpecificSurface2015 — Proksch, Löwe & Schneebeli (2015), JGR Earth Surf. 120, 346–362

**Summary.** Statistical model retrieving density, exponential correlation length and SSA from SnowMicroPen (SMP) penetration force, calibrated against micro-CT of Alpine, Arctic and Antarctic snow (73–574 kg/m³, SSA 5.8–50.8 mm⁻¹). Errors 10.6 %, 16.4 %, 23.1 %. Applied to a 46 m 2-D stratigraphy at Kohnen Station.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- SSA as a field measurement, p.1 (346): field methods "near-infrared photography (NIP) [Matzl and Schneebeli, 2006], shortwave-infrared (SWIR) photography ..., infrared profilers ..., contact spectroscopy ..., and SWIR reflectance [Gallet et al., 2009]"; SMP gives a meter profile "in less than 1 min" (abstract).
- Density and SSA as inputs to properties, p.1: "With density and specific surface area (SSA), as the most prominent objective morphometric measures, key properties as thermal conductivity, dielectric permittivity, or air permeability can be computed [Arons and Colbeck, 1995; Mätzler, 1996; Calonne et al., 2012]. Although these two morphometric properties are strictly not sufficient to characterize snow microstructure [Löwe et al., 2013]".
- SSA from a µCT image, p.3 Eq. (4): SSA = 4(1−φ_i)/l_c from the slope of the two-point correlation function at the origin (Debye 1957), averaged over directions; "We use the SSA in terms of surface area per ice volume". Agrees with surface triangulation, R² = 0.996 (p.9).
- Typical SSA-method errors 10–15 % (p.13 [358]): NIR 15 %, IRIS/SWIRcam 10 %, DUFISSS <12 %, POSSSUM 10–15 %.

**Claims supported / qualified**
- Outline L561–564 ("Why SSA": SSA is measured in the field and laboratory; Matzl & Schneebeli 2006; Gallet et al. 2009; Proksch et al. 2015) — **supported** (p.1, abstract). Proksch is itself a field method (SMP) and lists the others.
- Outline §5.4 test (ii) and Cite box (L1317–1320): supported as a citation for field SSA measurement.
- Outline §2.3 "What is reported: SSA, how it is measured from φ, and its units" (L799–802): Proksch Eq. (4) is an alternative definition for a binary field; the manuscript should state its units (per ice volume vs per mass: SSA_mass = SSA_vol/ρ_ice) since the snow literature uses both (Proksch per ice volume, m⁻¹; Taillandier/Legagneux per mass, cm²/g).

**Candidate additions.** State SSA units explicitly and, if SSA is per ice volume, note SSA_mass = SSA_V/ρ_ice so readers can compare with gas-adsorption values (Proksch p.3 vs Taillandier p.1–2).
