# schweizerSnowAvalancheFormation2003 — Schweizer, Jamieson & Schneebeli (2003), Rev. Geophys. 41(4), 1016

**Summary.** Review of dry-snow slab avalanche formation: contributory factors (terrain, new snow, wind, temperature, stratigraphy), snow mechanics, microstructure, failure (bond breaking vs sintering), fracture propagation, spatial variability.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- Persistent weak layers, PDF p.1–2 (2-1/2-2): climax avalanches "follow from old snow instabilities due to the failure of a buried layer of kinetic growth crystals. These crystal types include surface hoar, (near-surface) faceted crystals, and depth hoar, and they compose persistent weak layers".
- p.9 (2-9) [35]: "70% of 186 skier-triggered avalanches were released by weak layers of persistent grain types (i.e., surface hoar, faceted crystals, and depth hoar)". Weak layer is "a necessary prerequisite" for dry slab release (p.8).
- p.7 [30]: "the weak layer is relatively unaffected because of the generally low thermal conductivity of snow [Sturm et al., 1997]".
- p.14 [53]: "Snow can be considered as cellular solid ... It is a sintered material".
- Sintering vs bond fracture as the competing microscale processes (p.15–17); Table 1 (p.7): warming increases bond-formation rate (delayed, stabilizing).
- SNOWPACK model is "primarily microstructure-based" and runs operationally for the Swiss warning service (p.10).

**Claims supported / qualified**
- Draft L256 / outline ¶2: "Depth hoar formation under strong temperature gradients creates a mechanically weak layer within snowpacks and drives slab avalanche initiation (Colbeck 1998; Fukuzawa 1993; Schweizer 2003)" — **supported** for Schweizer: depth hoar is one of the persistent weak-layer types that cause slab releases (p.1–2, p.9). Strictly, Schweizer lists depth hoar together with surface hoar and near-surface facets; "drives slab avalanche initiation" is slightly strong — "is one of the persistent weak layers on which slab avalanches release" is closer.
- Outline ¶2 "snow conductivity sets heat transfer": Schweizer p.7 supports "low thermal conductivity of snow" qualitatively (secondary, cites Sturm 1997).

**Candidate additions.** None needed beyond softening "drives" to "on which slab avalanches commonly release".
