# kingery_regelation_1960 — Kingery (1960), J. Appl. Phys. 31, 833–838

**Summary.** Neck growth and shrinkage between ice spheres (sprayed into liquid O2; radii 0.1–3 mm; pairs/rows on a glass slide under a microscope) in a constant-temperature room at −2.2, −9.5, −17.8 and −25.1 °C, no separate enclosure. Mechanism identified from time exponent, particle-size scaling, absence of shrinkage and activation energy.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript** (Table I exponents checked on rendered PDF p.3, journal p.834)
- Classical laws, Table I: viscous flow (x/r)² ∝ t/r; evaporation–condensation (x/r)³ = B(T)t/r²; volume diffusion (x/r)⁵ = C(T)t/r³; surface diffusion (x/r)⁷ = D(T)t/r⁴. Shrinkage "none" for evaporation–condensation and surface diffusion. p.4: "the time dependence of neck growth varies ... from the one-half to the one-seventh power of time".
- Measured exponent, p.5 (836): "The average value for −17.8 °C ... was (x/r) ∼ t^{1/6.9}, in close agreement with the value to be expected from surface diffusion"; Table II means of inverse slope n ≈ 6.1–7.1 across temperatures; no approach of centers; time to reach x/r = 0.1 ∝ (size)⁴ (Fig. 4); activation energy 27.5 kcal/mol (≈115 kJ/mol).
- Evaporation–condensation, p.6 (837): rate computed for vacuum (α = 1) "is less than that experimentally observed at temperatures above −20 °C. Under atmospheric conditions, the evaporation-condensation mechanism is even less important. However, there may be some contribution from this mechanism at −25 °C".
- Experiments in open cold-room air ("no separate enclosure for the particles", p.4) — humidity not controlled.

**Claims supported / contradicted / qualified**
- Outline L395–404 (classical exponents: 1/3 evaporation–condensation, 1/5 volume, 1/7 surface diffusion; cite Kuczynski 1949, Kingery & Berg 1955, Hobbs & Mason 1964): **supported** by Kingery (1960) Table I (which cites Kuczynski and Kingery & Berg). Kingery (1960) is local and checkable; Kuczynski 1949 and Kingery & Berg 1955 are not. Recommend citing Kingery (1960, Table I) for the exponents (it is in Zotero but not in the manuscript .bib, per MANIFEST).
- **Important counter-evidence for §3.2/§5.1 and Appendix A:** Kingery measured t^{1/7} neck growth between ice spheres at −2 to −25 °C (−17.8 °C most data), with size scaling and Ea consistent with surface diffusion, and argued vapor transport was too slow. Molaro et al. (p.12, Fig. 8b) found their model slower than Kingery's data. The manuscript's statement that surface diffusion is negligible (by Molaro's equations) must acknowledge that the classic direct measurement in air attributed ice neck growth to surface diffusion. One plausible reconciliation (not claimed by Kingery): his open cold room was likely undersaturated, and Demmenie et al. (2025) report ~1/7 exponents when undersaturated — check Demmenie before stating this.
- Outline "Decisions" L98–100 ("no Thomas or Kingery runs"): consistent; citing the papers' results is still needed.
- Draft L262 "Other modes of diffusion tend to have limited effects": **contradicted** by Kingery (1960) for the conditions of his experiments.
