# bouvetHeterogeneousGrainGrowth2023 — Bouvet, Calonne, Flin & Geindreau (2023), The Cryosphere 17, 3553–3573, doi:10.5194/tc-17-3553-2023 (printed)

**Summary.** Two cold-room experiments on snow layers under ~100 K/m, for 20 and 28 days: A starts as DF at 210 kg/m³, B as RG at 287 kg/m³. Sampled by µCT along the full vertical extent, with temperature and humidity sensors. Findings: heterogeneous depth-hoar growth (coarser in the middle), basal mass loss (an air gap >3 mm in 28 d), and hard depth hoar with SSA increasing in B. Experimental only, with no model and no condensation-coefficient fitting (the word "phase field" does not occur).

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- p.1: FC and DH "are characterized by angular shapes and coarser grains, often loosely bonded. They constitute typical weak layers involved in slab avalanches (Schweizer et al., 2003)."
- p.17 (3568): SSA increasing during hard depth-hoar growth, "as opposed to the typical logarithmic decrease in SSA reported in most of the metamorphism studies (e.g., Cabanes et al., 2003; Flin et al., 2003; Domine et al., 2009)".
- p.2: "At higher temperatures, the rate of metamorphism is enhanced"; Kamata & Sato: snow at −55 °C "barely evolved" while at −15 °C it became 1 mm depth hoar.
