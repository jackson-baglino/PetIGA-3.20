# schneebeliTomographyTemperatureGradient2004 — Schneebeli & Sokratov (2004), Hydrol. Process. 18, 3655–3665

**Summary.** Miniature "snow breeder" (48 mm diameter, 25 mm high) inside a µCT (36 µm voxels, scans every 8 h); temperature gradients 25–100 K/m at mean ~−8 °C for about a week (13 d for one sample); six samples of aged rounded-grain snow, 147–514 kg/m³. Effective heat conductivity (EHC) measured from heat fluxes; SSA (per ice volume), structure size, SMI, anisotropy from µCT.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- Abstract p.1: "The density in the observed volume remained constant ... The heat conductivity changed by as much as twice its initial value, caused by changes in structure and texture, but not due to changes in density." "heat conductivity increased during temperature gradient metamorphism, instead of the expected decrease due to a shrinking of the bonds." "We also observed a plateau".
- Two behaviours, p.5 (3659): "The EHC of LDS [low-density snow] decreased as soon as a temperature gradient was applied and then increased or remained constant. The EHC of HDS [high-density, 490–514 kg/m³] increased immediately, almost doubled from the initial value and reached a plateau." Fig. 7a labels "Stage 1 / Stage 2" for LDS only.
- SSA, p.9 (3663): "SSA decreases only for LDS, not for HDS"; anisotropy principal direction switches from horizontal to vertical (Table V, p.7).
- Density-only parameterization insufficient, p.10 (3664): "EHC increased by about 60% from the initial value at a constant snow density ... The parameterization of EHC by snow density alone (Sturm et al., 1997) is insufficient, especially when TGM exists."
- What EHC includes, p.1: "the aggregated coefficient of (i) conductivity through the ice matrix and the pore space, (ii) the latent heat release and gain by recrystallization and (iii) the convective heat exchange" — measured EHC includes vapor/latent transport; convection excluded by sample height (p.8).
- SSA defined per ice volume (m⁻¹), p.3.

**Claims supported / contradicted / qualified**
- Outline L510–511 (Schneebeli & Sokratov 2004 and Calonne 2014 followed conductivity at constant density under a gradient): **supported** (abstract; p.5).
- Outline L554–556 "conductivity changed at nearly constant density, in two stages (as described by Calonne et al. 2014; not yet read)": **partially** — two stages (initial drop, then rise/plateau) only in the low-density samples; dense samples (≈500 kg/m³) rose immediately by ~2× then plateaued. Also: their EHC is measured heat flux and includes latent-heat transport (p.1), not the purely conductive k computed in this paper.
- Outline L556–558 "under a gradient, losing surface area did not raise conductivity": **contradicted / needs rewording** for this source — in the HDS runs EHC nearly doubled while SSA did not decrease (p.9), and in LDS SSA fell while EHC first fell. The correct statement from S&S is that EHC under a gradient is controlled by texture/anisotropy (vertical ice structures), not by density or SSA alone.
- Outline §5.3 ¶6 "Density is not enough": **supported** (p.10 quote) — closely matching citation.

**Candidate additions.** For "What is known about snow conductivity" use: "At constant density, conductivity under a temperature gradient changed by up to a factor of two, rising in dense snow and first falling then rising in light snow, through texture and anisotropy rather than density (Schneebeli & Sokratov 2004)". Note that their measured EHC includes latent heat (contrast with this paper's conductive k, cf. Fourteau et al. 2021).
