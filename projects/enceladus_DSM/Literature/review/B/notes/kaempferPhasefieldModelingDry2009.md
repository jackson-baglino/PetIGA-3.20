# kaempferPhasefieldModelingDry2009 — Kaempfer & Plapp (2009), Phys. Rev. E 79, 031502

**Summary.** Phase-field model of dry snow metamorphism (φ ∈ [−1, 1]) coupling a double-well phase equation driven by vapor supersaturation, asymmetric heat conduction and one-sided vapor diffusion; parameters linked to the sharp-interface problem (Gibbs–Thomson with capillary length d0 and kinetic coefficient β) by analogy with solidification; time-scaling of diffusion coefficients and sources (factor ξ) for quasi-steady fields. Validation: 1-D lamellae vs pseudoanalytical solution, 2-D bubble migration vs Stehle (1967); qualitative 2-D/3-D runs on µCT snow under 190 K/m. Finite differences, PETSc.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript** (Table I and Eqs. 37–43 checked on rendered PDF p.4 and p.8)
- Table I (p.4), at T0 = −10 °C: d0 = 1.3×10⁻⁹ m; m = 2.99×10⁻²⁶ kg; Dv = 2.178×10⁻⁵ m²/s [Massman]; ν = 1.81; K_j = {−0.5865×10⁴, 0.2224×10², 0.1375×10⁻¹, −0.3403×10⁻⁴, 0.2697×10⁻⁷, 0.6918} [Wexler]; L_sg = 2.60×10⁹ J/m³ (= 2.83×10⁶ J/kg with ρi = 918.9); γ = 1.09×10⁻¹ J/m² [Libbrecht web]; κi, κa = 2.29, 0.02 W/m/K; ρi, ρa = 918.9, 1.341 kg/m³; Patm = 1.01325×10⁵ Pa; "10⁻³ < α < 10⁻¹" [ref 31, Libbrecht 2006]; "3×10⁴ < β < 3×10⁶, in simulations β = 5.5×10⁵ s m⁻¹".
- Eq. (2), p.3: P_vs(T) = exp(Σ_{j=0}^{4} K_j T^{j−1} + K5 ln T) — the K5·ln T form.
- p.6 Eq. (16)–(17): β = (ρi/ρvs)√(2πm/kT)/α; "From the most recent ice crystal growth rate experiments by Libbrecht [31], the condensation coefficient is believed to have values between 10⁻³–10⁻¹".
- Gibbs–Thomson, Eq. (11) p.5: ρv = ρvs(T)(1 + d0K + βvn); d0 = γa³/kT ≈ 10⁻⁹ m (Eq. 13–14).
- Thin-interface, p.8: Eq. (37)–(38) "a1 = (5/8)√2"; Eq. (39) β′ = a1(τ/(Wλ) − a2 W/D), "a2 is a second constant of order unity"; Eq. (40) β′ = β′0(1 − a1a2 W/(Dβ′0)); Eq. (41) two-sided form with W/αT and W/Dv; sharp-interface conditions Eq. (42)–(43): W ≪ (κ/C)β0 ..., W ≪ Dv(ρvs/ρi)β0. p.3: "the intrinsic properties of the ice-vapor interface (in particular its slow kinetics) ... justify the use of the simpler sharp-interface limit." Also W ≪ 1/K and W ≪ d0/(β0 vn) (p.9 Eq. 45).
- Time scaling, p.10: diffusion coefficients and the source terms (ice density, latent heat) are scaled together by ξ so interface velocities are unchanged; "the relative magnitude of the different transport mechanisms is preserved"; valid for ξ ≳ 10⁻⁵ (Eq. 48).
- Validation, p.11–13: 1-D fluxes converge linearly in W to the pseudoanalytical solution; 2-D bubble velocities "within the order of magnitude of the experimental values". Conclusion p.15: "validated it quantitatively for simple one- and two-dimensional structures and performed qualitative simulations ... on real snow microstructures obtained by µCT".
- Scope p.3/p.15: "Other phenomena, in particular grain boundary diffusion or the anisotropies ... will be neglected"; isotropic d0 and β.
- Motivation p.1: "The snow microstructure, in turn, determines macroscopic snow properties such as the thermal conductivity of a snowpack."

- Draft L273 / Table 4 note "α_c ∈ [10⁻³, 10⁻¹]" (no citation): **supported by K&P** Table I and Eq. 17 (citing Libbrecht 2006, unpublished). Cite K&P (and Braun et al. 2024 who call it the commonly found range).
