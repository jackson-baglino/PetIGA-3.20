# colbeckThermodynamicsSnowMetamorphism1980 — Colbeck (1980), J. Glaciol. 26(94), 291–301, doi:10.3189/S0022143000010832 (printed)

**Summary.** Thermodynamic analysis (Gibbs phase rule, Laplace and Kelvin equations) of curvature-driven ("equi-temperature") metamorphism in dry snow, liquid-saturated snow, and wet snow with low liquid content. Order-of-magnitude estimates only; no experiments.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- p.3 (293): "Hobbs and Mason (1964) and Hobbs and Radke (1967), examined the sintering of ice spheres and found that mass transfer through the vapor is more important than transfer by surface or volume diffusion. Accordingly, it is most important to consider vapor transfer."
- p.4 (294): the Kelvin equation at constant temperature "is the most nearly correct description of dry-snow metamorphism in the absence of imposed temperature gradients."
- Abstract p.1: curvature-driven dry metamorphism is "dominated by the slow process of vapor diffusion"; "radius of curvature contributes to but does not control metamorphism except for short periods in very fresh snow".
- p.1 (Intro): metamorphism changes "strength, permeability, reflectivity, and thermal conductivity".
- p.6 (296): his Eq. 26 "would be a poor approximation to the sintering of spherical grains as analyzed by Hobbs and Mason (1964)."
