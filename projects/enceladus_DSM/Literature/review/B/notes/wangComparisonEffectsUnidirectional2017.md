# wangComparisonEffectsUnidirectional2017 — Wang & Baker (2017), Hydrol. Process. 31, 871–879, doi:10.1002/hyp.11067

**Summary.** Vertical 1-D arrays of ~3 mm polycrystalline ice spheres in a sealed tube at about −9 °C. Conditions: unidirectional gradients (~83 K/m, either direction) or a gradient alternating every 12 h, for 48 h. Necks imaged by µCT (7.8 µm) and SEM, with 2-D COMSOL conduction and vapour-flux modelling on cross-sections. Not cited in the manuscript.

**Findings relevant**
- p.1: "The structural evolution of snow during quasi-isothermal metamorphism is often interpreted as a sintering process (Colbeck, 1998)"; polycrystalline spheres sinter faster than monocrystalline ones (Hobbs & Mason).
- p.3: on their 2-D modelling, "the effective conductivity estimated from 2D sections is probably lower than that of the real 3D structure", because connectivity in the porous neck region is 3-D.
- p.5: under an alternating gradient, necks grow without a net position shift, "similar to that for isothermal sintering: the vapor diffuses from the grains to the bond, but the neck position does not change"; under a unidirectional gradient, faceted depth hoar grows at the neck.

**Relevance.** The p.3 caveat that 2-D sections underestimate conductivity relative to 3-D is a closely matching citation for the outline's "Why periodic, why two dimensions" paragraph, where our 2-D k_eff should be stated as not quantitatively transferable to 3-D. Otherwise not needed.
