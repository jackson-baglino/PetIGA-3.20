# martinMeasurementsRegolithThicknesses2023 — Martin, Whitten, Kattenhorn, Collins, Southworth, Wiser & Prindle (2023), Icarus, "Measurements of regolith thicknesses on Enceladus: Uncovering the record"

**Summary.** Regolith thickness on Enceladus inferred from tectonic pit-chain morphometry (Slope Angle Method preferred). Thicknesses reach 700 m (mean 250 m, n = 116). Combined with plume deposition models (Southworth), these imply very long deposition times unless the regolith is low-density and high-porosity (0.2 g/cm³, 90 %) or the mass flux was higher in the past. Not cited.

**Findings relevant.** Abstract: "The porosity and density of the plume-generated regolith are unconstrained parameters"; matching maximum thicknesses within the age of the solar system requires "the lowest density (0.2 g/cm³) and highest porosity (90%)".

**Relevance.** It is the source of the 0–700 m porous-layer thicknesses that Byrne 2024 uses (Byrne p.9 cites Martin et al. 2023). It could support a planetary Intro clause that "Enceladus is mantled by a plume-derived porous regolith up to hundreds of metres thick, whose porosity and microstructure are unconstrained (Martin et al. 2023)". This uses no sintering timescale and so fits the framing. Optional but closely matching.
