# colbeckTheoryMetamorphismDry1983 — Colbeck (1983), J. Geophys. Res. 88(C9), 5475–5482, doi:10.1029/JC088iC09p05475 (from the download banner)

**Summary.** A theory of crystal growth in dry seasonal snow under imposed temperature gradients. Interparticle vapour diffusion is computed with electrostatic capacitance solutions and a log-normal geometric enhancement factor. The rounded-to-faceted transition is set by a critical supersaturation, corresponding to a 0.1–0.2 deg/cm gradient. Results are compared with Pahaut & Marbouty and with de Quervain. Theory only. The OCR text is poor, so quotes were read through the fused words.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- p.1 (5475): with no imposed gradient, curvature differences "would cause slow grain and neck growth by vapor diffusion and heat flow. However, in nature these self-imposed temperature gradients are small in comparison with the temperature gradients imposed on the snow cover".
- p.2 (5476): "a layer of snow undergoing rapid grain growth will lose its strength unless the new crystals sinter. While well-rounded grains sinter quickly, highly faceted grains do not sinter quickly until the growth rate slows."
- p.3 (5477): in snow, "the growth rate is limited by vapor diffusion rather than surface kinetics" (β·Δρ ≫ 1). This holds under temperature-gradient conditions.
- Faceted crystals grow fastest near the warm ground (depth hoar).
