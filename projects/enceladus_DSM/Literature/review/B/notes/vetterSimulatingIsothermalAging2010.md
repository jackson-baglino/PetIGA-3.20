# vetterSimulatingIsothermalAging2010 — Vetter, Sigg, Singer, Kadau, Herrmann & Schneebeli (2010), EPL 89, 26001 (accepted-manuscript copy)

**Summary.** Monte Carlo coarse-grained model with "effective ice particles" (10 µm voxels), combining sublimation–deposition (random-walk vapour) and surface diffusion (Metropolis hops). It is fitted by eye and by SSA to the Kaempfer & Schneebeli 2007 µCT series at −54, −19, −8 and −2 °C. A simple falling-grain gravity rule is added. Not cited in the manuscript.

**Findings relevant**
- Abstract p.1: "sublimation-deposition is the dominant contribution for temperatures close to the melting point, whereas surface diffusion dominates at temperatures far below the melting point."
- p.1: "Surface diffusion and sublimation-deposition are believed to be the most dominant ones [8,9]."
- p.4: simulation steps are rescaled to real time to match experiments; the two rate parameters "mainly lead to an effective rescaling of the simulation time, i.e. only their ratio is important". Without gravity, the long-term behaviour at −2 and −8 °C departs from the experiments.
- p.6: the fitted SSA exponents vary continuously with temperature, which they ascribe to a vapour/surface-diffusion transition.

**Relevance.** This is a fitted model whose temperature conclusions come from parameter matching, so the evidence is weak. Still, it is a second source (with Löwe 2011 and Choukroun 2020) suggesting surface diffusion matters at colder temperatures. That is relevant to our −40 °C runs and to the outline's "no surface diffusion" limitation. Optional citation in the limitations paragraph, labelled as a model result.
