# postberg_sodium_2009 — Postberg et al. (2009), Nature 459, 1098–1101

**Summary.** Cassini Cosmic Dust Analyser (CDA) time-of-flight mass spectra of thousands of E-ring ice grains (radii 0.1–1 µm), Oct 2004–Dec 2005, compared with laboratory laser-dispersion spectra of salt solutions. Identifies Na-rich and Na-poor grain populations and infers a salty, alkaline subsurface ocean.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- Grain size measured: p.1 (1098): CDA "recorded thousands of mass spectra of E-ring particles, predominantly with radii of 0.1–1 µm".
- Composition, p.1: "Previous CDA results show that the bulk material of E-ring particles is water ice"; "About 6% of these spectra (hereafter ‘type III’ spectra) show ... particularly high sodium contents ... the sodium salts NaCl, NaHCO3 and/or Na2CO3 as significant constituents"; abstract (garbled): salt-rich grains "(0.5–2% by mass), which can arise only if the plumes originate from liquid water".
- Majority are Na-poor, p.1–2: "The majority of E-ring ice spectra are of types I and II ... Na features are identified in over 90% of these spectra ... albeit at much lower abundances"; Na/H2O 5×10⁻⁵ to 5×10⁻⁸ (Fig. 1 caption, p.2).
- Fallout, p.3 (1100): "most of the total ejected grain mass falls back to the moon’s surface (the cryovolcanically active regions should be covered with Na-bearing plume particles)".

**Claims supported / qualified**
- Draft L259 / outline L314–315 "The plume's grains are micron-sized, mostly water ice, and contain percent-level NaCl (Postberg et al., 2009)": **partially**. Supported: water ice bulk; salts at 0.5–2 % by mass with NaCl the main salt. Qualifications: (i) only ~6 % of the sampled E-ring grains are salt-rich; >90 % carry only trace Na (Na/H2O ≤ 5×10⁻⁵); (ii) measured grains are E-ring grains of 0.1–1 µm radius (sub-micron to micron), sampled in the E ring, not plume deposits. Suggested wording: "...mostly water ice; a fraction of them carry 0.5–2 % sodium salts, mainly NaCl (Postberg et al., 2009)". For the near-surface/larger grains that dominate fallout, Postberg et al. (2011, not in library; cited by Spencer & Nimmo 2013 p.9 "most contain approximately 1% salt, mostly NaCl") would be the closer source.

**Candidate additions.** p.3 statement that most ejected grain mass falls back on the active regions supports outline 3b item (1) "deposited grains form a granular layer" — a closely matching citation alongside Kempf et al. (2010).
