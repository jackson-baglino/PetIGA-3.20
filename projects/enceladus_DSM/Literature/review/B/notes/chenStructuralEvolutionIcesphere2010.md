# chenStructuralEvolutionIcesphere2010 — Chen & Baker (2010), Hydrol. Process. 24, 2034–2040, doi:10.1002/hyp.7787 (printed)

**Summary.** 2-D single-layer arrays of ~4 mm ice spheres, made by dropping water into liquid nitrogen, held in a −10 °C cold room. One array was quasi-isothermal (insulated box, 18 days); the other was under a ~500 °C/m gradient (Peltier stage, −20/−10 °C, 8 h). Optical time series plus cryo-SEM/EBSD, and a steady conduction model of two spheres with a wide versus a narrow neck. The text layer drops minus signs (CHECK_COLLECTION).

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- p.1 (2034): Kuczynski's power law (x^n/R^m = f(T)·t) identifies the mechanism. Kingery (1960) "concluded that surface diffusion is the dominant mechanism. This, however, requires a very high surface diffusion coefficient". Kuroiwa concluded that volume diffusion dominates. "Hobbs and Mason (1964) proposed sublimation–condensation and water vapour transport as the dominant mechanisms, a proposal that has since received wide acceptance (Blackford, 2007; Adams et al., 2008)." Colbeck proposed grain-boundary diffusion.
- p.2–3: under quasi-isothermal conditions, necks grew by vapour condensation filling pores, with "fine protrusions"; some protrusions retreated; a solid neck showed no significant evolution in 18 days.
- p.4 (2037): under the gradient, horizontal necks grew by large faceted crystals; "the evolution of necks ... primarily determined by the direction and rate of water vapour transport".
- p.4 Fig. 10: a narrow neck (x/D = 0.05) concentrates the temperature difference, so slight end-to-end differences create a "very high temperature gradient locally at the narrow neck". This is conduction-only, steady state.
