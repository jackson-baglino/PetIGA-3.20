# thomas_crushing_1994 — Thomas, Ratke & Kochan (1994), Adv. Space Res. 14(12), 207–216

**Summary.** Laboratory sintering of ice particles and porous ice/ice–olivine powders (spheres sprayed into liquid N2) held isothermally at −20 °C (253 K) in a cold lab; two-particle neck growth observed under a microscope; crushing strength of powder beds by spherical indentor vs time (minutes to ~10^4 min) and mineral content; Hall–Petch/notch strength theory; extrapolation to comets by surface diffusion at 30 K.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- Classical exponents, PDF p.3 (journal p.209): "x ~ t^{1/n} (n=3 for vapour transport, 4<n≤5 for surface diffusion, n=6 for grain boundary diffusion, and 4<n≤5 for lattice, volume diffusion)" (after Exner 1978). Also: "these power laws hold only for small ratios of the neck radius x to the particle radius r, i.e. x/r < 0.5".
- Measured exponent for ice, p.4 (journal p.210): two ice particles, microscope, −20 °C: "We find a neck growth proportional to t^{1/3}, i.e., the sublimation and recondensation process predominate at a temperature of −20°C." Fig. 3 label "r ≈ 120 µm" (garbled; check figure), "T=253K".
- Strength of porous ice bodies rises with sintering time at −20 °C (Fig. 4–5, p.5–6); 10 vol% olivine raises strength tenfold (p.6).
- Low-T: sublimation route "can be excluded" below 100 K because vapor pressure negligible; surface diffusion assumed instead (p.8). Q_s = 14.8 kJ/mol from a Gjostein metals/ceramics scaling, not measured for ice (p.9): "there are no experimental values for D_s of water ice at very low temperatures". 0.1 µm grains reach x = 0.1 r in "a few thousand years" at 30 K.
- Two-sphere model figure with four mechanisms (sublimation/recondensation, surface, grain-boundary, lattice diffusion), p.3 Fig. 1.

**Claims supported / contradicted / qualified**
- Outline L395–404 (classical exponents: "1/3 for evaporation–condensation, smaller exponents (1/5, 1/7) for volume and surface diffusion"): Thomas et al. **support n = 3 for vapor** and directly **measured t^{1/3} for an ice pair at −20 °C**, but give 4<n≤5 for both surface and volume diffusion (not 1/7 for surface diffusion). The 1/5–1/7 assignment is Kuczynski's (missing PDF) — not checkable here; the exponents differ between authors, so the paper should cite one source for the numbers it uses.
- Outline L1184–1186 ("surface diffusion alone gives t^{1/7}"): **partially** — per Thomas n=4–5; the argument (adding a route with a lower exponent cannot raise the fitted exponent toward 1/3) holds for either value.
- Draft L452/L499 attributing w ∝ t^{1/3} to Demmenie et al. (2025): Thomas et al. (1994) is an earlier, ice-specific, measured t^{1/3} at −20 °C. **Closely matching citation** for Figure 4's t^{1/3} curve and Intro ¶4 (the outline asks for "a classical source").
