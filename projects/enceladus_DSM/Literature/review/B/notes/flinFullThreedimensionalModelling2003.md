# flinFullThreedimensionalModelling2003 — Flin, Brzoska, Lesaffre, Coléou & Pieritz (2003), J. Phys. D: Appl. Phys. 36, A49–A54

**Page convention.** PDF page N (IOP cover sheet first) = article page A(N−1)+48, i.e. PDF p.2 = A49.

**Summary.** 3-D curvature-driven evaporation–condensation model (Kelvin + Langmuir–Knudsen; vapour diffusion assumed fast, surface diffusion and bulk diffusion neglected) applied to voxel images (9.82 µm), compared with the same −2 °C three-month isothermal µCT experiment as Flin 2004. Rate constant k0 set to 1 because α is "not precisely known", so model time is arbitrary.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- p.2 (A49): "Near 0 °C, the vapour pressure of water is high: the metamorphism can be considered, in first approximation, as fully curvature-driven."
- p.3 (A50) §2.1.2: "Intensive experimental work [11, 12] used laws from metallurgy [13] to find out the governing mechanisms. For experimental [14] and theoretical reasons [15], these works led to contradictory results." Also: "Surface diffusion, which generally occurs at first stages of the metamorphism [16] is neglected here."
- p.4 (A51): "As k0 depends on numerous physical constants whose a part (in particular, α coefficient) is not precisely known, its value was arbitrary set to the unity. It implies that experimental and numerical times differ by an undetermined multiplicative constant." — a direct precedent for α_c acting as a time rescaling.
- p.5 (A52): neck evolution "is directly related to its initial size ... in two dimensions, a neck is always growing, whatever its size is" (3-D-specific behaviour).
- p.6 (A53): both mean curvature and SSA follow a logarithmic law in time; simulated slope within 15 % of experiment; real snow differs because of packing (connectivity, densification).

**Claims supported / qualified**
- Draft L262 (sintering has been studied extensively; Flin 2003 among examples): **partially** — Flin 2003 is a curvature-driven isothermal metamorphism model of real snow, not a sintering (neck-growth) study per se, though it shows neck growth/removal (Fig. 2d,e). Acceptable as an example of grain-scale isothermal metamorphism modelling.
- Draft L262 "dominating mode ... vapor diffusion": Flin 2003 assumes vapour transport, and notes surface diffusion "generally occurs at first stages", and that metallurgy-based mechanism studies gave "contradictory results". This qualifies the vapour-dominance claim (supports stating it as an assumption).
- Draft L279: **supported** as terrestrial snow-community literature.
- 2-D vs 3-D neck behaviour (p.5) is relevant to the 2-D-domain caveat in our model.

**Candidate additions.** p.4 quote is a close precedent for the outline's statement that α_c (and T) enters as a time rescaling: model and experimental times differ by an undetermined multiplicative constant because α is unknown.
