# calonneStudyTemperatureGradient2014 — Calonne, Flin, Geindreau, Lesaffre & Rolland du Roscoat (2014), The Cryosphere 8, 2255–2274, doi:10.5194/tc-8-2255-2014 (printed)

**Page convention.** PDF p.N = journal p.(2254+N).

**Summary.** A sieved rounded-grain slab (294 kg/m³ on average, porosity 0.68) under a vertical gradient of 43 K/m in a cold room at −4 °C for 3 weeks. Seven µCT samples over 0–500 h. Computed: density, SSA, correlation lengths, curvatures, tortuosities, the k_eff tensor (homogenization) and permeability, each with anisotropy coefficient A = vertical/horizontal. Comparison with self-consistent and dilute-spheroid estimates that use no fitted parameters.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- p.1 (2255): the slab was held "at −4 °C" under 43 K/m. Depth hoar and faceted crystals "constitute often the weakest layers of the snowpack".
- p.9 (2263): "The snow density shows no significant evolution ... average value over the experiment is 294 kg m−3 (porosity of 0.68) ... between 275 and 315 kg m−3" from image to image. SSA "decreases continuously with time from 27.7 to 13.4 m2 kg−1".
- p.7 Eq. 23: readjustment of k to ρ = 294 kg m⁻³ using the Calonne 2011 fit, to remove sample-to-sample density scatter.
- p.12 (2266): "The anisotropy coefficient of the thermal conductivity A(k) increases from 0.90 to 1.34", mostly in 0–144 h. Ice tortuosity A goes 0.86 → 1.90 (then 1.70); correlation length 0.96 → 1.25; l_SSA 0.92 → 1.01.
- p.15–16 (2269–2270): "two distinct stages in the evolution of the effective thermal conductivity (Fig. 6e): a decrease between 0 and 73 h, followed by an increase", in agreement with Schneebeli & Sokratov 2004 (−8 °C, 100 K/m, 268 kg/m³). Also: "Figure 6 shows an overall increase of the effective thermal conductivity and permeability at a constant density, underlying the role of the microstructure rearrangement".
- p.15 Sect. 3.x: analytical estimates have errors E(k_z) = −22.6 ± 4.3 %, E(k_xy) = −15.6 ± 6.0 %, E(A(k)) = −7.9 ± 7.4 %.
- p.17 (2271), conclusions: "confirm that the density alone, or any isotropic quantity, is not sufficient to describe the time evolution of physical properties during a TG metamorphism."

**Claims supported / qualified**
- Outline L524–548 (Calonne 2014 description: 294 kg/m³, 43 K/m, −4 °C, 3 weeks, seven samples, SSA 27.7 → 13.4, two stages, Eq. 23, A(k) 0.90 → 1.34, tortuosity and correlation numbers, ellipsoid errors 23/16/8 %, "density alone ... not sufficient"): **supported**. All numbers check against the text and p.12.
- Outline L540–542 (our reconstruction: the orientation average ends where it started, ≈0.19): this is the authors' own reconstruction, and it differs in emphasis from Calonne's "overall increase of the effective thermal conductivity ... at a constant density" (p.16). With k_z 0.18 → 0.23 and k_xy 0.20 → 0.18, (k_z + 2k_xy)/3 goes 0.193 → 0.197 (≈+2 %), so both statements hold. The manuscript must not say "conductivity did not rise". Say instead: "vertical conductivity rose by ~28 % while horizontal fell, so the orientation average changed little".
- Outline L556–558 ("losing surface area did not raise conductivity"): **partially / overstated**, for the reason above. Calonne report an overall increase, driven by the vertical component.
- Outline L509–511 (Schneebeli & Sokratov and Calonne 2014 followed conductivity at constant density under a gradient; isothermal sintering at fixed porosity is not quantified): **supported**.
- Outline L1278–1281 ("Density is not enough"): **supported** by the p.17 quote, with the caveat that it was written for TG metamorphism.
- Draft L256 (depth hoar weak layer): p.1 is an additional supporting citation ("constitute often the weakest layers").

**Candidate additions.** The p.16 sentence on overall increase at constant density, quoted accurately, is the closest precedent for "Conductivity changes while porosity stays fixed".
