# flinThreedimensionalGeometricMeasurements2004 — Flin, Brzoska, Lesaffre, Coléou & Pieritz (2004), Ann. Glaciol. 38, 39–44

**Summary.** 3-month isothermal metamorphism of natural fresh snow at −2 ± 0.2 °C (Col de Porte, slab in closed Styrodur box); cores impregnated with 1-chloronaphthalene and imaged by synchrotron µCT (ESRF ID19, 4.91 µm voxels, binned to 9.82 µm). Image-analysis algorithms for porosity, SSA (adaptive normals + voxel projection), normal-vector anisotropy and mean-curvature distributions; 2.5 mm analysis cubes.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- p.1 (39): "Isothermal metamorphism is probably the simplest situation, mainly governed by the distribution of mean curvature along the grain/pore interface, like many sintering processes."
- p.1: "Near 0 °C, one can expect a reduced influence of possible surface or bulk diffusion at grain boundaries ... owing to the large saturation vapour pressure of water."
- p.4 (42), rendered: SSA(t) = −157.23 log(t) + 681.52 (cm²/g, t in h), r = 0.986 — SSA ∝ −log t, consistent with Legagneux/Cabanes.
- p.4 Fig. 2 (rendered): porosity fell from ~0.89 to ~0.71 over 2000 h and layer thickness to ~40 % — natural isothermal snow densifies (settling) while it coarsens.
- p.4: "a slight but persistent anisotropy along the z axis" after 66 h, attributed to vertical packing.
- Conclusion p.6: data meant for "a quantitative calibration of the present empirical laws of grain coarsening"; REV caveat ("sampling below the representative elementary volume").

**Claims supported / qualified**
- Draft L279 (literature on how DSM affects macroscopic properties centred on terrestrial snow; Bouvet 2022, Flin 2003, Flin 2004): **supported** as an example of terrestrial isothermal metamorphism characterization (SSA, porosity, anisotropy). It does not compute thermal conductivity.
- Contrast with our model: their snow densified substantially (porosity 0.89→0.71). Our simulations hold porosity nearly fixed with no compaction, which supports the "absence of compaction is unquantified" framing at L279.
- The SSA ∝ −log t form supports the outline's logarithmic-SSA precedents (Legagneux, Taillandier, Kaempfer & Schneebeli).

**Candidate additions.** Can be cited alongside Legagneux/Taillandier for the log-time SSA decay under isothermal conditions (−2 °C). Its µCT SSA method (normals + voxel projection) is a precedent for computing SSA from 3-D fields.
