# blackfordSinteringMicrostructureIce2007 — Blackford (2007), J. Phys. D: Appl. Phys. 40, R355–R385, doi:10.1088/0022-3727/40/21/R02 (cited so in Chen 2010)

**Page convention.** PDF p.1 is the IOP cover; PDF p.N = journal p.R(353+N).

**Summary.** Review of ice sintering and microstructure: surface energies and dihedral angles, imaging (optical, LT-SEM, XMT), sintering of ice spheres and snow (pressureless and pressure), sintering maps, densification of firn, temperature-gradient effects, and modelling at the particle and snowpack scale.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- p.14 (R367): the Kuczynski power law (X/D)^n = B·t/D^m with "surface diffusion, n = 7, m = 4; lattice diffusion, n = 5, m = 3; vapour transport, n = 3, m = 2; viscous or plastic flow, n = 2, m = 1". **This is the library-checkable statement of the classical exponents 1/7, 1/5, 1/3, 1/2.**
- p.14: Kingery 1960 used particles of 0.1–3 mm radius at −2 to −25 °C. Kingery found "no shrinkage", which is "consistent with evaporation–condensation (vapour diffusion) or surface diffusion", and concluded surface diffusion. This was "later rejected by Hobbs and Mason (1964) as it implied too large a value for surface diffusion coefficient. This may have been because the ice spheres were not contained within an ice saturated enclosure."
- p.15–16 (R368–369): Hobbs & Mason used 50–700 µm particles at −3 to −20 °C and concluded vapour transport. Because the molecular mean free path (0.1 µm) is much smaller than the neck, they modified Kuczynski to vapour **diffusion**, which "gave values of n = 5 and m = 3 for vapour transport; these are the same values as Kucynzski determined for volume diffusion". They separated the two by absolute rates; measured rates were ~5× below the vapour prediction.
- p.17 (R370): Maeno & Ebinuma sintering maps give vapour diffusion first and surface diffusion second. "However, they predicted two regions where surface diffusion would dominate; at small neck radii (ca <2 μm) at homologous temperatures corresponding to 0 to −6 °C, and (for neck radii of ca <1 μm) below ∼−40 °C."
- p.18 (R371): "the vapour transport mechanism stands out as playing a dominant role in sintering of ice particles under most laboratory conditions ... However, it is certain that other transport mechanisms contribute to sintering." Also: "This increase in density cannot occur by vapour diffusion as this transport mechanism does not lead to densification."

**Claims supported / qualified**
- Draft L262 (vapour dominance; example of sintering studies): **supported** by p.18. Blackford is the right review to cite for this sentence, rather than Molaro/Choukroun, and it states the caveats (other mechanisms contribute; Maeno & Ebinuma small-neck exceptions).
- Outline L395–404 (classical exponents 1/3, 1/5, 1/7; classical sources missing from the library): Blackford p.14 gives the Kuczynski exponents and is in the .bib. **Important nuance:** n = 3 (1/3) is Kuczynski's vapour transport with free-molecular (kinetics-controlled) exchange. Hobbs & Mason's vapour **diffusion** gives n = 5 (1/5). The manuscript should say 1/3 is the attachment-limited (evaporation–condensation) result. That fits our α_c = 1e-3 attachment-limited regime and supports outline L1167 ("saturation does not make growth attachment-limited; a small α_c does").
- Outline L1185 ("surface diffusion alone gives t^1/7"): **supported** (p.14).
- Scope (no densification in our model): p.18 supports "vapour transport does not densify" (with Swinkels Table 1).
- Draft L276 (densification "especially in the presence of overburden pressures"): p.18 ("gravity plays a role") supports it qualitatively.
