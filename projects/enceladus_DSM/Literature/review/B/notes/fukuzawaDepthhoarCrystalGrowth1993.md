# fukuzawaDepthhoarCrystalGrowth1993 — Fukuzawa & Akitaya (1993), Ann. Glaciol. 18, 39–45

**Summary.** Field observations (Hokkaido, 50 days, 1989–90) of new snow just below the surface turning into depth hoar in one or two nights under 100–300 K/m near-surface gradients, plus cold-lab experiments on low-density snow (80–100 kg/m³) at −16 °C with gradients 100–300 K/m for ~50 h; crystal size from photographs.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- Intro: "A layer with relatively low strength plays an important role in a slab-avalanche release. The kinetic growth metamorphism produces a depth-hoar layer, which is typically weak because of thin bonds between particles."
- Field: "new snow metamorphosed to depth-hoar crystals in one or two nights" (10 of 50 days); lab at 150 K/m: d = 0.20 mm (16 h) → 0.39 mm (48 h); "crystal size increased linearly with time and the crystal growth rate increased as the given temperature gradient increased" (abstract). Final remark: "Avalanche danger will be high if the metamorphosed layer is covered by heavy snow in a later storm."
- "Thermal conductivity of low-density snow is small, therefore high temperature gradients persisted in the new-snow layer during the night."

**Claims supported / qualified**
- Draft L256 (depth hoar under strong gradients creates a weak layer and drives slab avalanches; Colbeck 1998, Fukuzawa 1993, Schweizer 2003): **supported** for Fukuzawa (weak layer, avalanche danger), with the nuance that it concerns near-surface depth hoar.
- Note the bib title says "Depth-hoar crystal growth in the surface layer under high temperature gradient" (Ann. Glaciol. 18, 1993) — consistent with the PDF.

**Candidate additions.** None.
