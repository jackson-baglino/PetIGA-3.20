# presleyEffectBulkDensity1997 — Presley & Christensen (1997), JGR 102(E4), 9221–9229

**Summary.** Line-heat-source measurements of particulate materials (glass beads and angular particles, 25–30 µm and mixtures) under Martian CO₂ pressures, testing the effect of bulk density and size sorting on conductivity. The OCR is poor, so the text was read through fused words.

**Findings relevant**
- Abstract p.1: lower bulk density of angular particles gives lower conductivity; for 25–30 µm particles k "appears to increase linearly with increasing bulk density and with the square root of the atmospheric pressure"; for mixtures, k equals that of a material made only of the largest size (tentative).
- p.1: gas conduction across pores dominates in particulate media not under vacuum; radiation is negligible below 300 K for most particulates.

**Relevance.** Not cited. Mars regolith in CO₂ at low pressure is not comparable to our sintered ice in air at 1 atm. No citation recommended. If the Discussion names gas-phase conduction as a control in unbonded packings, the Piqueux 2009 papers are the better citation.
