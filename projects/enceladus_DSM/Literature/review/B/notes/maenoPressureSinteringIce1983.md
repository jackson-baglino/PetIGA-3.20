# maenoPressureSinteringIce1983 — Maeno & Ebinuma (1983), J. Phys. Chem. 87, 4103–4110

**Summary.** Reviews ice-sphere sintering experiments (Kingery, Kuroiwa, Hobbs & Mason, Ramseier & Keeler, Hobbs & Radke, Jellinek & Ibrahim; Table I) and builds pressureless sintering diagrams for ice-sphere pairs (r = 35 and 350 µm) with six simultaneous mechanisms. Surface diffusion includes a quasi-liquid-layer contribution. Second half: pressure-sintering diagrams for snow and firn densification (diffusional and dislocation creep). The text layer is jumbled in the diagram section, so the abstract, Sect. 1 and Blackford 2007's summary of Fig. 3 were used.

**Findings relevant**
- Abstract p.1 (4103): "six or more different mechanisms contribute simultaneously ... The vapor transport mechanism was concluded to be dominant in most natural and laboratory conditions, but in the very initial stage surface diffusion is also an important mechanism."
- p.2 (4104): discrepancies among earlier studies come from differences in temperature, particle radius and the accuracy of neck measurement, and from ice's high homologous temperature (T/Tm ≥ 0.9), high vapour pressure and surface phase change, so "several mechanisms work together".
- p.2: sintering-mechanism diagrams "were first shown by Ashby and Swinkels and Ashby". This supports correcting draft L262's "first proposed by Swinkels & Ashby".
- Via Blackford p.17: surface diffusion dominates only for necks < ~2 µm at 0 to −6 °C, and < ~1 µm below ~−40 °C.
- Densification of natural snow is pressure sintering, driven by surface energy plus applied load.

**Claims supported / qualified**
- Draft L262 (vapour dominance): **supported**. This is the primary review-and-calculation source, and a better citation than Molaro/Choukroun. It carries the caveat of initial-stage surface diffusion, which matters for our starting necks (28 µm wide on 50 µm grains, so well outside the small-neck exception).
- Draft L276 (densification needs overburden): **supported** (pressure sintering).
- Draft L262 ("first proposed by Swinkels"): **contradicted** (Ashby 1974 first; p.2).

**Candidate additions.** Cite Maeno & Ebinuma 1983 with Blackford 2007 for vapour dominance and its small-neck exception.
