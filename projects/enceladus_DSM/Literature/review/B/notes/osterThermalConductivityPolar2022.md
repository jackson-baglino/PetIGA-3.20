# osterThermalConductivityPolar2022 — Oster & Albert (2022), J. Glaciol. 68(272), 1141–1148, doi:10.1017/jog.2022.28

**Summary.** A custom guarded hot plate (steady state), validated on reference materials, measures the effective conductivity of South Pole firn cores from 4 to 48 m depth. Fits: k_firn(ρ) = 0.144·e^(0.00308ρ) and k_firn(z) = 0.536·e^(0.0144z). Not cited in the manuscript.

**Findings relevant**
- Abstract: "the first direct measurements of the effective thermal conductivity of polar firn over depths down to 48 m"; k increases with depth and density.
- Intro: needle probes underestimate k by factors of 2–3 in snow and firn (Riche & Schneebeli 2013); firn has "different microstructural characteristics than seasonal snow due to its significant post-depositional metamorphism and sintering processes".
- Discussion: microstructure differences at similar density are noted as a possible source of scatter, left to future work.

**Relevance.** It would support the Intro sentence on firn conductivity (outline L297, "snow, firn and porous ice") with a direct measurement, alongside Calonne 2019. It is also a steady-state measurement consistent with our steady-state k_eff. Optional.
