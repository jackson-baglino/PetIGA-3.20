# calonneThermalConductivitySnow2019 — Calonne, Milliancourt, Burr, Philip, Martin, Flin & Geindreau (2019), GRL 46, 13079–13089, doi:10.1029/2019GL085228 (HAL copy hal-03053507; PDF p.1 is the HAL cover)

**Summary.** k_eff tensors computed by homogenization (Geodict) on 64 µCT images: 37 seasonal snow (102–~550 kg/m³) and 27 Antarctic firn/porous ice samples (566–888 kg/m³). Each was run at −3, −20 and −60 °C with k_ice = 2.107, 2.330, 2.900 and k_air = 0.024, 0.023, 0.019 W/m/K. Results are compared with common formulas, and a new density- and temperature-based formula is proposed.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- p.2 abstract: "Estimating thermal conductivity of snow, firn, and porous ice is key for modeling the thermal regime of alpine and polar glaciers." p.3: "How temperature fluctuations are propagated through snow, firn, and porous ice is therefore crucial to understand".
- p.5 §3.1: "At low densities, ice grains are insulated by the predominant surrounding pore space. The few bonds between ice grains play then a critical role in the overall heat conduction as they act as heat paths. Hence, during a change in density, the portion of ice added or removed elsewhere than at ice bonds has little impact". The quadratic snow relation becomes linear above ~450 kg/m³.
- p.6: anisotropy "tend[s] to vanish above 600 kg/m³". For snow, the k_z/k_xy ratio "ranges from 0.66 for a rounded grains sample with a lc of 0.67, to 1.57 for a depth hoar sample with a lc of 1.34 (Figure 2b)" (Fig. 1c).
- p.5–6 (temperature): k rises as T falls; for >600 kg/m³ it rises ~10 % from −3 to −20 °C and ~37 % to −60 °C; at 300 kg/m³ the rises are ~5 % and ~20 %; the effect is negligible for light snow, and the sign reverses at 102 kg/m³.
- p.4: k_ice at −20 °C = 2.330 W/m/K (Paterson 1994).

**Claims supported / qualified**
- Outline L551–553 (Figs. 1c, 2b: 0.66 at lc ratio 0.67; 1.57 at 1.34; vanishes in firn above ~600 kg/m³): **supported** (p.6).
- Outline L297 ("sets how fast heat crosses them and so the temperature profile ... (Calonne et al. 2011, 2019)"): **supported** by Calonne 2019 (p.2–3: "thermal regime", "how temperature fluctuations are propagated"). Calonne 2011 is weaker for this.
- Draft L256 (stability of glacial ice / firn): Calonne 2019 supports the glacier and firn thermal-regime statement. Permafrost is still unsupported.
- Outline L1355 (T-dependent conductivities as missing physics): **supported**. Our constant k_ice = 2.29 vs 2.33 at −20 °C (Paterson) is a ~2 % difference, and for porous media at ~300–500 kg/m³ k changes ≲5–10 % between −3 and −20 °C. Quantify this in the limitations sentence.
- Outline L1288 (conductivity vs density as the contrast): **supported**.

**Candidate additions (important).** The p.5 sentence on "the few bonds between ice grains ... act as heat paths" is the closest library support for the paper's central mechanism, that neck growth at fixed porosity raises k_eff. It is a direct, closely matching citation for the Intro/Discussion: "at low density, heat crosses the few inter-grain bonds, so ice added away from the bonds changes conductivity little (Calonne et al. 2019)".
