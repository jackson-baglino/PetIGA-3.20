# auriaultHeterogeneousMediumEquivalent1991 — Auriault (1991), Int. J. Engng Sci. 29(7), 785–795 (DOI not printed)

**Summary.** A methods paper on when a finely heterogeneous medium under a given excitation admits an intrinsic macroscopic description. The condition is separation of scales, ε = l/L ≪ 1, for both the structure and the excitation. Double-scale asymptotic expansions (x macro, y = x/ε micro, periodic or stationary unknowns) give a self-consistent test: compatibility conditions on successive cell problems yield either the macroscopic law or non-homogenizability. The examples are Stokes flow giving Darcy's law (with non-homogenizable regimes) and elastodynamics of composites (diffraction when wavelength ~ l). Heat conduction is not treated.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- p.1–2 (785–786): "the macroscopic domain must contain a (very) large number of heterogeneities and the characteristic lengths of the heterogeneities must be limited so that there exists an elementary representative volume (ERV) ... small compared to the macroscopic volume"; "Let l be a characteristic length of the ERV or the periodic cell and L a characteristic macroscopic length"; separation of scales l/L = ε ≪ 1. "The ERV reduces to the periodic cell when the medium is periodic."
- p.3: "If we limit ourselves to the discovery of the structure of the macroscopic description, periodic and random media are equivalent, if homogenization is possible. It is therefore convenient to assume that the medium is periodic."
- Conclusion: the method "introduces a basic cell problem where the cell is either the period when the medium is periodic or the elementary representative volume when it is random."

**Claims supported / qualified**
- Draft L353 ("l describing ... grain and pore sizes, and L describing the size of a representative element volume (REV), such that l ≪ L"): **contradicted**. In Auriault (and Calonne 2011 [8]) l is the size of the ERV or periodic cell, and L is the macroscopic length of the sample or excitation. Fix: "l is the size of the periodic cell (the REV), which contains many grains, and L the macroscopic length; homogenization requires l/L ≪ 1." The grain size must in turn be small compared with l.
- Draft L353 ("assume, without loss of generality, that the medium is periodic, as described in Calonne 2011 and Auriault 1991"): **supported** (p.3), with the wording "for deriving the structure of the macroscopic law, periodic and random media are equivalent".
- Draft L362 / outline L792 (the macroscopic description is "as shown in" Auriault 1991): **partially**. Auriault 1991 gives the method and the separation-of-scales condition but not the heat-conduction result or the k_eff formula. For those, cite Auriault et al. 2009 (book; to be checked) and Calonne 2011 Eqs. 5–11.

**Candidate additions.** None beyond the fixes.
