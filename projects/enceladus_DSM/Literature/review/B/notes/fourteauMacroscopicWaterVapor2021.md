# fourteauMacroscopicWaterVapor2021 — Fourteau, Domine & Hagenmuller (2021a), The Cryosphere 15, 389–406

**Summary.** Theory and finite-element simulations on idealized and µCT snow microstructures of the macroscopic (volume-averaged) water vapor flux in snow under a temperature gradient, bounded by the infinitely-fast and inert (slow) surface-kinetics limits. Refutes the "hand-to-hand" enhancement of Yosida et al. (1955).

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- Abstract: "although sublimation and deposition ... do enhance microscopic diffusion in the pore space, this effect is more than countered by the restriction of diffusion space due to ice ... water vapor diffusing more than inert molecules in snow but still less than in free air, regardless of the value of the sticking coefficient".
- Separation of scales and REV (Auriault 1991; Auriault et al. 2010) invoked for treating snow as equivalent medium (Sect. 3).
- α "will depend on the local saturation of water vapor and on the crystallographic properties of the ice surface (Saito, 1996; Libbrecht and Rickerby, 2013)".
- Macroscopic flux must be a volume average over the whole REV including ice (Sect. 3) — same averaging principle as the homogenized K_eff.

**Claims supported / qualified**
- Outline L565–568 / L1357 (which Fourteau paper is about apparent conductivity): this one (2021a) is about vapor diffusivity, **not** conductivity — the outline's choice of `fourteauImpactWaterVapor2021` for the conductivity point is correct; this paper is the wrong one for that sentence.
- Supports the manuscript's averaging definition (volume average over the cell) in §2.3 only loosely; no need to cite.
