# molaroMicrostructuralEvolutionWater2019 — Molaro et al. (2019), JGR Planets 124, 243–276 (published online Feb 2019; corrected 2020)

**Summary.** Full implementation of the Swinkels & Ashby (1981, "SA81") sintering-diagram model for water ice (six mechanisms; Stage 1 neck growth with surface/boundary coupling; smoothing function; Stage 2/3 not used for ice). Validated on metals, compared with historical ice neck data (Hobbs & Mason 1964; Kingery 1960; Thomas et al. 1994) and new experiments: (i) isolated grain pairs (LN2-sprayed spheres, 212 µm sieve) in a Peltier-cooled Instec TS102V/G cryostage at −20 and −5 °C under an optical microscope; (ii) bulk aggregates (~10 µm mean radius, ~50 % density) stored at −20 and −80 °C for 2–10 weeks. Then Stage-1 timescales for planetary surfaces (power-law fits), salts, other ices.

**Findings relevant to the manuscript**
- Mechanisms in ice, PDF p.13 (journal 255): "Stage 1 in ice is strongly dominated by surface V1 and vapor diffusion V3, both nondensifying mechanisms (Figure 10)" (25 µm grains, T/Tm = 0.8). App. B2, p.29–30 (271–272): "Neck growth during sintering of water ice is dominated by vapor diffusion (V3)"; surface diffusion "dominates over vapor diffusion during the early stages" for 1 µm grains; "Grains 100 µm in radius showed no significant effects from neglecting surface diffusion". Intro p.2 (244): "Later studies found both vapor transport and surface diffusion to be important, the latter being primarily dominant at temperatures below −15 °C when neck sizes are small".
- Densification much slower than neck growth (key point; abstract, p.13, p.21). Only mechanisms 4–6 densify (p.6).
- Vapor term, App. A p.27–29 (269–271): Hertz–Knudsen with α = 1 ("Kingery and Berg (1955) assumed that sublimation occurs with perfect efficiency and thus set (α = 1)"); "ignores gas‐gas diffusion rates"; "assumes that sintering occurs in a vacuum" (p.12); "This ignores the presence of an atmosphere".
- Grain-pair experiment set-up, p.15 (257): "The vacuum ports of the stage were capped in order to seal the chamber during the experiment ... The clearance between the slide and cover is ~1 mm. Though this area does not seal off from the rest of the chamber ... The surface of the slide was coated with ice to help saturate the atmosphere above it, except for its center".
- −20 °C pair: radii 101 and 73 µm (shrinking 3 % and 4 %), neck 33→65 µm "after 57 min" (Fig. 11 caption, p.17); Table B1 (p.31) lists neck widths at 0, 2, 5, 9, 18, 29, 40, 57, 78 min: 32.81 … 60.54 (57 min) … 64.78 µm (78 min); diameters 202.00/145.00 → 193.90/144.00 µm. Fig. 12 (p.18) plots −20 °C "over a period of 100 min".
- −5 °C pair: radii 99 and 78 µm, shrinking 9 %, neck 32→44 µm (p.15); Table B1: 0, 26, 48 min → 32.51, 41.57, 44.00 µm; diameters 197/153 → 179/139 µm.
- Neck "size" is the neck width (diameter): Fig. 12 caption "relative neck sizes ... equal to the shared neck size between grains divided by each of their diameters".
- Time anchoring, p.16 (258): "we anchored the first measurement to the time at which the model shows the same neck size ... The grains at −20 °C show a good match to the model, particularly when the neck size is calculated relative to the larger grain ... The grains at −5 °C show a slower sintering rate than predicted by the model, though these measurements also have higher uncertainty."
- −5 °C uncertainty, App. B5 p.31 (273): "fogging on the cryostage window ... and a very high humidity that day. If the saturation ice ... did not fully saturate the atmosphere above the slide, the higher ambient humidity could have also influenced sintering rates".
- Agreement level: abstract "agreement to some studies at the order of magnitude level"; Conclusions p.26 "consistent with the model at the order of magnitude level".
- Historical data inconsistent with each other (p.12): model slower than Kingery (1960), faster than Thomas et al. (1994), closest to Hobbs & Mason (1964); hypothesis: humidity/local vapor pressure differences.
- Some necks "showed no growth at all"; "No densification ... was observed" (p.16).
- Table 5 (p.15): Ω = 1.181×10⁻²⁹ m³; Δo = 917; γs = 0.06 J/m²; δb, δs = 9.04×10⁻¹⁰ m; Dob = 8.4×10⁻⁴ (0.03–10⁻¹²) m²/s, Qb = 4.9 kJ/mol; Dos = 1.4×10⁻⁸ (1.4×10⁻⁸–1.35×10⁻¹³) m²/s, Qs = 23.156 kJ/mol; P0 = 5.5×10⁹ Pa; Murphy & Koop (2005).
- Enceladus, §5.4 p.24 (266): "Enceladus has a maximum equatorial surface temperature of ~80 K ... For a 0.1‐µm grain deposited by plume fallout, the sintering timescale at this temperature is ~10³ years", ~10² yr with 2 % NaCl. §5.3 p.24: plume fallout grains 0.25–2.5 µm radius (Kempf et al. 2010).
- Conductivity, §5.2 p.23 (265): "As neck growth occurs, the surface area of solid‐to‐solid contact between ice grains will increase, causing an increase in the thermal conductivity of the aggregate ... the thermal behavior of icy surfaces is not only a function of density and grain size ... but also of age."
- Stage 1 timescale ∝ power law in T and grain size (Eq. 31, Table 6).

**Claims supported / contradicted / qualified**
- Draft L259 "Much of Enceladus' surface experiences conditions that cause little to no sintering ... (Choukroun 2020; Molaro 2019)": **contradicted for Molaro** — Molaro predicts neck growth of fine plume grains on ~10²–10³ yr at 80 K (§5.4) and "ice may undergo significant modification over geologic timescales, even in the outer solar system" (abstract). Outline L347–353 correctly drops Molaro here.
- Draft L262 "dominating mode ... vapor diffusion ... Other modes of diffusion tend to have limited effects or operate over planetary timescales (Molaro; Choukroun)": **partially** — Molaro: vapor dominates neck growth for ≥ tens-of-µm grains, but surface diffusion is co-dominant early and for small grains/cold T (p.13, p.2, App. B2); densifying mechanisms are slow. The outline's fix "cite Molaro alone, and say for necks larger than a few micrometres (their own rates; Appendix A)" attributes to Molaro a result that is the authors' own calculation with Molaro's equations — state it as "by the rate equations of Molaro et al. (2019), surface diffusion is <1 % of vapor transport for the necks in their experiment (Appendix A)", not as Molaro's finding.
- Draft L262 Molaro "adapting a sintering model originally used in metallurgy first proposed by Swinkels and Ashby": supported (p.3 "came out of the field of metallurgy"); "No modifications have been made to SA81" (p.5) — "adapting" → "applying".
- Draft L439 "fig. 11 ... over a 78 minute period": **partially/wrong figure** — the 78-min series is Table B1 (and Fig. 12); Fig. 11 shows 57 min. Cite "their Table B1".
- Draft L439 "within a sealed cryostage": **partially** — chamber sealed by capping vacuum ports, but the slide area "does not seal off from the rest of the chamber", and grains sublimated (3–9 %) (p.15).
- Draft L441 "authors acknowledge the measurements at −5 °C have a larger uncertainty and the cryostage chamber may have not been fully saturated": **supported** (App. B5, p.31), but note Molaro argue this would *increase* rates, yet measured rates were slower.
- Draft L497 "the authors state their measurements ... at −5 °C had higher uncertainty and instead only make conclusions by the order of magnitude": **partially** — higher uncertainty yes; "order of magnitude" is their overall conclusion for both temperatures (abstract, conclusions), not specific to −5 °C.
- Outline L405–412 (what Molaro measured; anchor; good match −20, slower −5; no exponent reported): **supported** (p.15–16). They report no exponent — correct.
- Outline L924–927 ("Their own model matches through its vapor term, which assumes α_c = 1 and no gas diffusion"): **supported** (App. A).
- Outline L1155–1157 ("present its agreement as order-of-magnitude"): **supported**.
- Outline Appendix A parameters: **supported** (Table 5 values match). "P_v from Murphy & Koop (2005), 103 Pa at −20 °C": Molaro uses Murphy & Koop (Table 5); 103 Pa matches Wexler 103.276 Pa (Wexler p.7).

Initial neck radius r0 = 14 µm (width 28 µm) is **smaller** than Molaro's first measured widths (32.8 µm at −20 °C; 32.5 µm at −5 °C); the outline (L1464–1466) says the simulation "starts from the measured neck" — inconsistent unless t = 0 of the comparison is after relaxation to 33 µm. - Manuscript Table 1 γ_ia = 0.109 J/m² vs Molaro's γs = 0.06 J/m² — different literature values; the manuscript should give a source for 0.109.
